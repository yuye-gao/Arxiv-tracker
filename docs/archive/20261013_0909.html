Towards Physically Grounded Articulated 3D Assets: A Multi-Agent Pipeline and Benchmark
Authors: Xudong Cai, Ruijie Zhu, Haoliang Shang, Ruining Li, Marc Pollefeys, Shangzhe Wu, Andrea Vedaldi, Chuanxia Zheng
First: 2026-10-09T17:59:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:59:41+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In this paper, we study physically grounded articulated 3D asset generation---moving the goal from reproducing how objects look to capturing how they move and function. Inspired by the literature on procedural and agentic generation, we present Artisan, a system that extends the latest method Articraft by introducing a multi-agent pipeline with physical simulation feedback, enabling comprehensive attributes beyond geometry, appearance, and physically plausible articulation, such as collision-free motion, stability under gravity, and accurate mass and material properties. Furthermore, we construct a unified benchmark spanning four key aspects: spatial fidelity, physical plausibility, downstream task performance, and overall quality. The benchmark encompasses 100 reference assets from ArtVIP and LightWheel, 50 stability prompts, and 50 ABO objects with reference masses, with protocols evaluating various metrics. Through extensive evaluation of more than 10 representative models, Artisan achieves competitive spatial fidelity while attaining the lowest collision rates and highest stability rates among the evaluated methods. Together, our system and benchmark establish insights for advancing 3D generation towards physically plausible, functionally usable assets.

Constructive Bounds for the Topological Expressivity of Neural Networks
Authors: Kosio Beshkov
First: 2026-10-09T17:55:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:55:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The expressivity of a neural network $Φ$ describes the class of functions that a network can express. The term topological expressivity refers to a family of measures particularly suited to understand binary classification and is often formulated in terms of the Betti numbers of a decision boundary $β(Φ^{-1}(0))$. Despite its popularity, this measure is not as useful for regression and other problems that do not involve a decision boundary. Instead, we propose to study topological expressivity in terms of the Betti numbers of the output of a neural network $β(Φ(X))$. We formally prove that a ReLU network with architecture $(d,\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d } β_k(3k+2),\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}β_k(k+1))$ can express spaces homotopy equivalent to wedge sums of spheres with arbitrary Betti numbers. This gives upper bounds for any arbitrary connected ($β_0=1$) sequence of Betti numbers, but by constructing spaces homotopy equivalent to products of $N$ spheres of dimension $d_1,...,d_N$ we achieve much better bounds $(\sum\limits_{n=1} d_n, 3\sum\limits_{n=1}\limits^N d_n + 2N, \sum\limits_{n=1}\limits^N d_n + N) $ for Betti numbers given by $\prod_n (1+x^{d_n})$. Finally we conjecture a much stronger tight bound which gives the architectural bound $(d,d+1,d+1)$ for a space homeomorphic to the sphere.

The expressivity of a neural network $Φ$ describes the class of functions that a network can express.
Make It Flow: Animating Water in 3D Gaussian Splatting Scenes
Authors: Kjell Keune, Florian Hahlbohm, Jannis Möller, Susana Castillo, Martin Eisemann, Marcus Magnor
First: 2026-10-09T17:08:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:08:02+00:00
Comments: Overview video at https://youtu.be/qf04D73kgrc
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Explicit 3D scene representations such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have recently enabled high-quality, real-time novel view synthesis. However, these methods assume static scenes, limiting realism in the presence of dynamic phenomena ubiquitous in real-world environments. While coherent motion (e.g., humans or vehicles) can be captured and reconstructed, high-frequency effects such as flowing water remain intractable under casual capture conditions, where dense multi-view, high-speed acquisition is not feasible. We present a method to animate water in static 3DGS scenes, enabling visually plausible motion without requiring dynamic capture or physical simulation. Our approach estimates 2D optical flow from reference images or model renderings and lifts it into a consistent 3D Eulerian motion field. We propose a cinemagraph-inspired algorithm that applies this motion field to deform Gaussian primitives, producing temporally seamless looping animations. The resulting representation retains real-time rendering performance, significantly enhances perceptual realism, and preserves free-viewpoint navigation within the scene. We evaluate our method on a newly captured dataset of in-the-wild waterfall scenes and demonstrate clear improvements over prior work in dynamic Gaussian splatting and 3D cinemagraph generation in visual quality and computational efficiency.

Explicit 3D scene representations such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have recently enabled high-quality, real-time novel view synthesis.
Revisiting the Task of Building Damage Assessment by Satellite Imaging
Authors: Anthony Cioppa, Virgile Blua, Sébastien Piérard, Pietro Ceccato, Marc Van Droogenbroeck
First: 2026-10-09T17:07:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:07:03+00:00
Comments: 82 pages, 2 figures, 9 tables, 160 references
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Natural disasters such as earthquakes, tropical storms, and floods cause extensive damage to buildings, and rapid, reliable damage maps are essential to guide emergency response. Automating this task, referred to as building damage assessment (BDA), from satellite imagery, has become an active research topic at the intersection of remote sensing, computer vision, and machine learning, particularly since supervised deep learning methods were shown to outperform earlier handcrafted approaches. Yet, despite a decade of intense activity and the release of several large-scale datasets, the field lacks a shared foundation. This chapter revisits the task of BDA from first principles, organized around three pillars: data, evaluation, and methods. We first clarify the candidate task for- mulations and argue for a sensor-agnostic, instance-based definition that tolerates missing pre-event imagery. We then review existing datasets along the axes of sensing modality, temporal configuration, and geographic coverage. On the evaluation side, we show that prevailing practices, largely based on a single aggregate score, are theoretically fragile, and we lay the foundations of a more informative, ranking-based, multi-score protocol. We further review methods ranging from task-specific deep networks to recent Earth observation foundation models, noticing that generalization to unseen events and regions remains an open difficulty. Finally, we discuss how these elements can inform the organization of a BDA challenge, including cross-validation, challenge, and private data-sharing setups designed to build trust between developers and users.

Natural disasters such as earthquakes, tropical storms, and floods cause extensive damage to buildings, and rapid, reliable damage maps are essential to guide emergency response.
Vision2Code: A Multi-Domain Benchmark for Evaluating Image-to-Code Generation
Authors: Ajay Vikram Periasami, Junlin Wang, Bhuwan Dhingra
First: 2026-05-11T22:50:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T16:51:01+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://image2code.github.io/vision2code/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
We study whether vision-language models (VLMs) can reconstruct structured, graphics-based images by writing executable code to generate them. Existing benchmarks on image-to-code generation are generally limited in the domains they cover, rely on reference source code for evaluation, and do not provide metrics to quantify domain-specific issues. To address this, we introduce Vision2Code, a multi-domain, reference-code-free benchmark containing 539 test examples from 15 different datasets, spanning charts and plots, geometry, graphs, scientific diagrams, documents and 3D spatial scenes. Evaluation is done by executing the generated programs and comparing their rendered outputs to the source images using dataset- and domain-specific rubrics for a VLM judge. We find that the final scores obtained in this manner have substantially higher correlation with human ratings than those obtained via generic VLM rubrics or standard pixel-based, embedding-based or perceptual similarity metrics. We further demonstrate the reliability of our evaluation under repeated sampling from the VLM judges, varying rubric weights, and in the presence of controlled errors. Our results highlight the variability across domains of existing image-to-code generation models, with accuracy on charts and graphs being significantly higher than other domains. Lastly, we demonstrate the utility of our scoring method for rejection sampling of generated programs for fine-tuning, improving Qwen3.5-9B by 16% and Phi-4-MM by 28% compared to their base checkpoints. Our code and data are publicly available at https://image2code.github.io/vision2code/.
Summary / 总结
We study whether vision-language models (VLMs) can reconstruct structured, graphics-based images by writing executable code to generate them.
How Well Do Prior-Data Fitted Networks Amortize Bayesian Inference?
Authors: Manuel Hülskamp, Julius Kobialka, Emanuel Sommer, Thomas Nagler, David Rügamer
First: 2026-10-09T15:49:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T15:49:24+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) are trained to approximate the Bayesian posterior predictive distribution (PPD) of a synthetic prior, yet results about the approximation quality are surprisingly thin. Taking an empirical risk minimization perspective, we decompose the deployment error of PFNs into prior, context, and amortization error, and measure the latter against a reference PPD under a realistic prior. Results show that PFNs generally recover the targeted PPD very well. Nevertheless, we find that parts of the residual amortization error persist, especially in \textit{rare} settings under the prior pretraining distribution, even when scaling the model size, which manifests in disagreement between independently pretrained PFNs and leaves uncertainty about the predictor at inference time. As a remedy, we study multiple types of Bayesian model averages and find that these can consistently reduce this uncertainty and improve predictions up to full-size TabICLv2 models.
Summary / 总结
Prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) are trained to approximate the Bayesian posterior predictive distribution (PPD) of a synthetic prior, yet results about the approximation quality are surprisingly thin.
A Privileged Teacher That Only Scores Cannot Search: Critique-Conditioned On-Policy Self-Distillation for Reasoning
Authors: Mayug Maniparambil, Ayush Rai, Ekansh Verma, Noel E. O'Connor, Fergal Reid
Venue: NeurIPS 2026 oral
First: 2026-10-09T15:36:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T15:36:47+00:00
Comments: Accepted as an oral at the FLLMPT workshop, NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
On-policy self-distillation promises to replace the sparse outcome signal of verifiable-reward RL with dense token-level credit: a teacher conditioned on the reference solution scores the student's own rollout, and the resulting distributional shift is distilled back into the policy. We investigate whether this scoring signal actually contains corrective credit. Using gold error spans in student rollouts, we test the two signals consumed by self-distillation objectives, the full-distribution KL and the token log-ratio, for two properties: whether they localize errors and whether they push probability away from erroneous student tokens. They exhibit neither of these properties nor can they reliably distinguish correct from incorrect rollouts within the same problem. We argue that this failure is structural: locating an error in a reasoning trace is itself a search problem, while a scoring teacher is never explicitly asked to perform that search. The same model, with the same privileged information, localizes errors substantially better when allowed to generate a critique. This forms the motivation for \emph{critique-conditioned self-distillation}: first generate a critique of the current rollout, then condition the scoring teacher on that critique. The resulting signal localizes errors, is corrective within error spans, and shifts probability toward tokens that initiate backtracking such as ``Wait'', ``But'', and ``Actually''. We further show that the critique is useful both at test time, where resuming a wrong rollout with the critique in context converts math problems the model cannot solve by sampling, and in cold-start GRPO, where it provides an advantage signal on prompts for which verifiable rewards alone provide none.
Summary / 总结
On-policy self-distillation promises to replace the sparse outcome signal of verifiable-reward RL with dense token-level credit: a teacher conditioned on the reference solution scores the student's own rollout, and the resulting distributional shift is distilled back into the policy.
KVeil: First-Place Solution to ECCV 2026 LifeGenIP Challenge on Unlearnable Videos
Authors: Boheng Li, Yueming Xing, Yiming Li, Jie Zhang, Han Qiu, Tianwei Zhang
Venue: ECCV 2026
First: 2026-10-09T15:29:23+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T15:29:23+00:00
Comments: Technical report for the winning solution (1st place, both phases) of the ECCV 2026 LifeGenIP Challenge on Unlearnable Videos against Diffusion-based Customization
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
The rapid development of personalized video generation has raised serious concerns over identity privacy, as a few facial videos of a person now suffice to reproduce their identity in synthetic videos. This report presents the first-place solutions to both phases of the ECCV 2026 LifeGenIP Challenge on Unlearnable Videos, which aims to protect facial videos from unauthorized customization under a limited and imperceptible perturbation budget. For Phase 1, we introduce Reference Capacity Collapse, a pair of complementary losses that degrade the information carried by the reference condition while keeping subsequent video representations dependent on it, together with a Motion-Guided EOT mechanism that improves robustness across unknown inference conditions. Phase 2 further considers tuning-based customization and unseen generative models. To address both threats within a single perturbation, we design a two-layer framework that protects the facial and background regions separately, constructing structure-aligned perturbations from a synthetic target identity in the former and strengthening the white-box disruption of Phase 1 in the latter. Our method achieves nearly perfect protection under white-box reference-based customization, and remains effective under other customization paradigms, various models, and temporal attacks. Both solutions are built upon our recent work LETHE. We hope our solutions can inspire future work on protecting facial videos against unauthorized customization. Our code is available at https://github.com/AntigoneRandy/KVeil.

Solving VeriContest with a Lean-Backed Rust Verifier
Authors: Traian Serbanuta, Jun Xu, Andrei Stefanescu, Cosmin Radoi
First: 2026-10-02T19:56:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T15:19:31+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
VeriContest is a benchmark of 1007 competitive-programming problems in Rust, each with a Verus specification, a judge-accepted solution, and a Verus proof. Its authors report that proof generation is the bottleneck for frontier models: given the specification and the code, the best model produces an accepted Verus proof for 13.95% of the problems on the first attempt. We report on solving the same proof-generation task with Rust-Prover, a verifier for Rust backed by Lean 4. The Verus specification and the Rust code are restated and translated into Lean, each specification becomes a theorem, and agents prove the theorems with Lean's kernel as the final check. All 1325 theorems of all 1007 problems were proved. 1259 of them were proved in one run of under 32 hours on Claude Opus 5.5, at a median of 3.2 minutes and $1.17 per proof, and 70% of them on the first iteration. The restated specifications were checked against the benchmark's test suites, and reviewed where no suite applies. None was wrong or weakened. The translated Lean programs were run on 21,413 of the benchmark's test cases and produced the same output as the Rust programs on every one. Across four Claude and four GPT models at five reasoning-effort settings, every current frontier model proves nearly all of a ten-theorem sample at every setting, and more effort raises the cost without raising the number of proofs. The cheapest Claude setting, Sonnet 5.5 at low effort, proves all of the 50 hardest theorems. We also rerun the benchmark's own Verus protocol with Claude Opus 5.5 on the 50 problems with the longest reference proofs. Opus 5.5 alone fails to prove one of them.

VeriContest is a benchmark of 1007 competitive-programming problems in Rust, each with a Verus specification, a judge-accepted solution, and a Verus proof.
PhysForge: Material-Controllable 3D Reconstruction with Separable Components
Authors: Yuchen Wang, Yanqi Bao, Zheng Gu, Zhengqing Chen, Jieqi Shi, Jing Huo
First: 2026-10-09T14:47:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T14:47:56+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Recent advances in physical 3D generation have enabled visually faithful assets with simulatable mechanical behavior. However, existing methods provide limited control over material behavior beyond appearance-based inference and struggle to estimate physical properties from complex interactions between objects and semantic parts. Therefore, we present PhysForge, a Video Diffusion Model (VDM)-based framework for addressing these challenges, comprising two complementary modules for material-controllable physical fitting and componentlevel interaction modeling. The first, PhysFit, leverages material-conditioned VDM dynamics as references for physical-property fitting, introducing windowed fitting to focus on material-revealing interactions and Hallucinated Forcing to disentangle nonphysical motion hallucinated by the VDM from material responses. The second, PhysComp decomposes complex interactions into individual objects and semantic components, enabling their physical parameters to be optimized independently and flexibly composed for joint simulation. Experiments on PhysAssets and Google Scanned Objects demonstrate that, compared with existing methods, PhysForge provides stronger control over material-dependent physical responses while supporting component-level physical modeling for complex interactions. It achieves the best CLIP and physical controllability scores on both common and uncommon materials, with qualitative comparisons, component-level ablations, and a user study further supporting these improvements. Our project page is available at https://masterwanna.github.io/PhysForge.html .
Summary / 总结
Recent advances in physical 3D generation have enabled visually faithful assets with simulatable mechanical behavior.
