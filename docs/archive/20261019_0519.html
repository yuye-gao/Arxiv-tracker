DK-VLM: Geometry as Position for Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Han Su, Meng Tian, Tianyu Huang, Mingyu Gao, Lennox Wei, Dongxu Li, Jianhua Han, Hang Xu, Xiaohe Wu, Wangmeng Zuo
First: 2026-10-15T17:58:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:58:15+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://sumuru789.github.io/DK-VLM-website/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-language models still struggle to understand 3D spatial relations across views and update spatial judgments as the observer moves. A common approach fuses 3D reconstruction features into visual tokens, but aligning these features with pretrained semantic representations remains challenging under limited spatial supervision. We propose DK-VLM with Dual-Kernel Attention, which computes attention from both a visual token's input position and its geometric position in the scene. A semantic kernel retains the pretrained model's native attention and positional encoding, while a separate geometric kernel uses estimated 3D points and camera poses to directly modulate attention through point displacements and relative view transformations. Then, a learned gate fuses their outputs. Geometry thus enters attention as position rather than as fused reconstruction features. We further introduce Reference-Frame Composition (RC) supervision to train the model to update its reference frame across successive judgments. It provides stepwise turn and heading targets derived from verified single-step annotations. Among the compared methods, DK-VLM-8B achieves the highest average score across six spatial benchmarks and the highest overall scores on VSI-Bench (74.33) and ReVSI-32 (58.28). RC supervision further improves multi-step spatial reasoning, while the full model preserves general video understanding. Project page: https://sumuru789.github.io/DK-VLM-website/.

Vision-language models still struggle to understand 3D spatial relations across views and update spatial judgments as the observer moves.
SUPRA-TEX: 3D Generation with Super-Aligned Texture
Authors: Chen Yang, Jiemin Fang, Fanpeng Meng, Zihao Huang, Jichen Pang, Guanjun Wu, Zhikuan Bao, Qi Tian
First: 2026-10-15T17:58:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:58:02+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://supra-3d.github.io/supratex/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Single image 3D generation aims to dress a given 3D mesh with relightable materials while faithfully preserving the visual details of a reference image. Existing methods either introduce artifacts when fusing inconsistent multi-view predictions or struggle to preserve fine details through implicit image-feature conditioning. To resolve these challenges, we introduce SUPRA-Tex, built on the principle of decoupling visible-material estimation, surface alignment, and observation-guided 3D completion. This division of labor allows the 3D generator, trained on limited 3D data, to focus its capacity on preserving visible evidence and completing unobserved surfaces. Specifically, we first train an MLLM-based image editing model to predict lighting-separated PBR maps in the reference view. We then project these maps onto the visible surface and encode them with the pretrained material VAE encoder into native observation latents that share the generation target's latent space. An interleaved flow transformer holds these latents as fixed, read-only anchors while alternating unobserved-region and full-surface updates to generate complete material latents. This design achieves super-alignment with the reference, delivering What You See Is What You Get fidelity on visible regions and coherent completion on unobserved surfaces. Trained solely on public datasets, SUPRA-Tex achieves SOTA performance in both geometry-conditioned texturing and end-to-end asset generation, matching or even exceeding leading commercial systems on in-the-wild images. Project page: https://supra-3d.github.io/supratex/

FDE-Gym: The Last Mile of AI Delivery
Authors: Dongxu Yang, Hongda Yan, Renxin Wang, Baoli Zhang, Haojia Zuo, Ying Zhao, Youmin Chen, Xiaojian Liao, Congming Gao, Tianyang Jiang, Yixiao Chen, Qing Wang, Zhiguo Yu, Xiaqing Li, Yuhao Zhang, Jiawen Sun, Renzhe Xu, De Mi, Tiancheng Wu, Xianya Fu, Xuanlu Zhao, Bo Feng, Meixuan Ren, Guanming Lin, Ziheng Zhou, Xinhua Wang

Comments: 14 pages, 8 figures, 4 tables. Website: https://fde-gym.com/paper ; Code: https://github.com/deeplethe/fde-gym ; Data: https://huggingface.co/datasets/DeepLethe/FDE-Gym
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3 · Project1
Abstract
Existing agent benchmarks typically evaluate agents on well-specified tasks defined by issue reports, specifications, or task tickets. Real-world customer delivery is different: forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) must clarify ambiguous requirements, work in live systems, and uncover unstated constraints. We introduce FDE-Gym, a benchmark of 95 end-to-end delivery tasks from 20 customer scenarios across 18 sectors, each adapted by industry experts from real-world delivery projects. In simulated customer environments with scripted stakeholders and modifiable customer systems, agents interact with stakeholders and operate on production-like systems to deliver working solutions. FDE-Gym scores a delivery by how much it improves the customer's own success metric, normalized between 0 (unchanged system) and 1 (reference solution), with penalties for stakeholder interactions (messages, trial runs, and waiting time). An online platform lets humans practice FDE skills under the same grading criteria. We evaluate 13 model configurations from seven providers. In the realistic setting, where unstated constraints must be discovered, the best configuration achieves a mean FDE-Gym score of 0.56 and resolves only 14 of the 95 tasks (a score of at least 0.8 with no incident). Discovering unstated constraints is the primary bottleneck: performance drops sharply when models must uncover them without explicit guidance. Yet information discovery alone does not account for the gap: even on the 23 tasks where the best configuration obtains every key fact from stakeholders, it reaches a normalized business uplift of 0.8 or above on only 10. These results reveal a substantial gap between solving well-specified tasks and delivering effective AI systems for real customers.
Summary / 总结
Existing agent benchmarks typically evaluate agents on well-specified tasks defined by issue reports, specifications, or task tickets.
Dense4D: Pixel-Level 4D Human Correspondence
Authors: Rıza Alp Güler
First: 2026-10-15T17:52:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:52:14+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reconstructing human motion in 4D requires identifying the same surface points through articulation, deformation and changes in visibility. We introduce Dense4D, to our knowledge the first feed-forward model dedicated to dense 4D correspondence across the body, clothing and hair from a set of monocular RGB images, without a body template. Trained specifically for human geometry, Dense4D jointly processes person-centred crops with known image-to-crop transforms to predict Human Point Maps (HPMs) and depth for each image. An HPM assigns each person pixel the 3D position of its observed surface point at a common reference time. These maps support amodal correspondence: points hidden in the reference image can be localised from observations in other frames. Matching HPM coordinates across crops establishes correspondence without predicting camera poses or large scene displacements; per-image depth then lifts the matches into 3D tracks. Experiments on real people demonstrate substantially higher tracking accuracy and closer agreement with scanned surfaces than the evaluated baselines. Points from different frames assemble into coherent human geometry in the reference pose, providing visible evidence of consistent dense correspondence through articulation and clothing deformation. Additional qualitative results, videos, and an interactive viewer are available at https://alpguler.com/dense4d.

Reconstructing human motion in 4D requires identifying the same surface points through articulation, deformation and changes in visibility.
Improving Environment-Agnostic Coding Agents: The Role of Scaffolding
Authors: Sergey Rodionov
First: 2026-10-15T17:43:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:43:16+00:00
Comments: 37 pages, 5 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Stronger language models may increasingly construct useful procedures for themselves, reducing the advantage of developer-supplied scaffolding. We investigate why this advantage persists and whether an environment-agnostic agent can be improved without environment-specific engineering. We evaluate a Codex-based agent in two nearly identical prompt variants on ARC-AGI-3, AGI Maze, and Terminal-Bench. Each variant is deployed with unchanged code, prompts, and general-purpose tools across environments, receiving environment-specific information as input. Changing only an instruction to encourage reporting difficulties and returning control for automatic continuation improves mean scores on both ARC-AGI-3 and AGI Maze, while Terminal-Bench shows small, mixed changes. Yet the general agent remains below an ARC-AGI-3-specific reference, including with GPT-5.6-sol. The reference prescribes organization and control rather than supplying new game rules or solutions. To investigate this gap, we transfer reset decisions to a scripted controller that follows a convention already given to the agent. This small change recovers part of the specialized agent's advantage. A complementary AGI Maze experiment shows that additional tutorial experience can reduce performance. Together, these findings highlight the distinction between having information and tools available and using them effectively. Supplied procedures still matter for the models tested here. The performance gains also come with higher token-based cost. The experiments do not separate better use of information and procedures from additional or differently allocated inference. We provide the EnvTraverse framework and an evaluated baseline agent for testing whether further environment-independent changes improve performance in some environments without degrading it in others.
Summary / 总结
Stronger language models may increasingly construct useful procedures for themselves, reducing the advantage of developer-supplied scaffolding.
InverFill: One-Step Inversion for Enhanced Few-Step Diffusion Inpainting
Authors: Duc Vu, Kien Nguyen, Ngan Nguyen, Trong-Tung Nguyen, Phong Nguyen, Khoi Nguyen, Cuong Pham, Anh Tran
Venue: CVPR
First: 2026-03-24T17:32:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:30:56+00:00
Comments: Accepted to CVPR'26 (Main Conference)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recent diffusion-based models achieve photorealism in image inpainting but require many sampling steps, limiting practical use. Few-step text-to-image models offer faster generation, but naively applying them to inpainting yields poor harmonization and artifacts between the background and inpainted region. We trace this cause to random Gaussian noise initialization, which under low function evaluations causes semantic misalignment and reduced fidelity. To overcome this, we propose InverFill, a one-step inversion method tailored for inpainting that injects semantic information from the input masked image into the initial noise, enabling high-fidelity few-step inpainting. Instead of training inpainting models, InverFill leverages few-step text-to-image models in a blended sampling pipeline with semantically aligned noise as input, significantly improving vanilla blended sampling and even matching specialized inpainting models at low NFEs. Moreover, InverFill does not require real-image supervision and only adds minimal inference overhead. Extensive experiments show that InverFill consistently boosts baseline few-step models, improving image quality and text coherence without costly retraining or heavy iterative optimization.
Summary / 总结
Recent diffusion-based models achieve photorealism in image inpainting but require many sampling steps, limiting practical use.
Whole-Body Dexterity
Authors: Conor Mc Gartoll, An Dang, Pranay Thangeda, Arjun Gupta, Carolina Higuera, Nima Fazeli, Mustafa Mukadam, Manikantan Nambi
First: 2026-10-15T16:40:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:40:48+00:00
Comments: 20 pages, 13 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Current humanoid dexterous manipulation remains largely confined to end-effectors and simple grasps, falling short of humans' ability to engage the entire body by bracing and sliding bulky objects against the arms and torso. We introduce Whole-Body Dexterity (WBD), a general framework that uses the robot's body and appendages as contact surfaces to grasp and manipulate objects. Our sim-to-real pipeline first generates a dataset of feasible whole-body grasps, then trains reinforcement-learning experts on a general any-to-any repositioning objective by sampling initial and goal grasps from the dataset, and distills these experts into a single policy that uses proprioception and object geometry. We show that the distilled policy transfers zero-shot to a real Unitree G1, where it repositions a basketball and a cardboard box to regions around the torso and completes a long-horizon task that stows a box under one arm, carries it from a shelf to a table, and places it. The controller operates interactively, steered by coarse motion references from a human teleoperator. Across real-world whole-body manipulation tasks, the distilled controller completes 90% of trials, compared with 18% for direct retargeting and 4% for a generalist whole-body controller.
Summary / 总结
Current humanoid dexterous manipulation remains largely confined to end-effectors and simple grasps, falling short of humans' ability to engage the entire body by bracing and sliding bulky objects against the arms and torso.
Lexical Reference Frames: Interpreting LM Residual Streams in Native Semantics
Authors: Jian Gu, Hongyu Zhang, Chunyang Chen, Aldeida Aleti
First: 2026-10-15T16:29:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:29:27+00:00
Comments: Code: https://github.com/jianguda/lexframe
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
The vocabulary of a language model is its native semantics: the embedding and the unembedding fix the meaning of a state at the two ends of the residual stream. Between them, existing methods either reuse a fixed lens or treat the states as a point cloud, and neither reads a state the way the model does. We argue that every hidden state has a native reference frame, induced by the layers that read it next and adaptive to the context. Lexical reference frames approximate it by reading a state through the model's own remaining layers with a stated number of rounds of attention between positions and a typical attention output for the rest, without fitting to the model's output. No lens, with fixed directions per layer, can exceed the square root of the native frame's coherence, 0.30 and 0.35 at mid-depth of SmolLM3-3B and OLMo3-7B, whereas the first-order frame, LexFrame[1], reaches 0.97 and 0.97 and reads a state within 0.16 nats of the model's output. Its coefficients attribute predictions more faithfully than attribution patching, its lexical basis steers a prediction while sparing other tokens, and its confident readings would let an early exit skip 0.45 and 0.42 of the layers, where lenses skip almost none. In this way, lexical reference frames make every hidden state of the model readable, attributable and steerable.
Summary / 总结
The vocabulary of a language model is its native semantics: the embedding and the unembedding fix the meaning of a state at the two ends of the residual stream.
Realtime-Bench: Can Frontier Models Solve Real-time Interactive Problems?
Authors: Jingyu Tang, Xu Yang, Zhehao Zhang, Zheng Wang, Qi Cheng, Haozhe Zhao, Manling Li, Wenxuan Li, Xide Xia, Jiaxin Pei
First: 2026-10-15T16:20:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:20:47+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Real-time interaction requires agents to perceive evolving environments and act under temporal constraints, yet existing evaluations of real-time streaming models focus on conversational quality and streaming perception, leaving effective action under strict temporal constraints untested. We introduce Realtime-Bench, a suite of carefully curated dynamic tasks where delayed actions directly affect success. Under Wall Clock, where environments advance according to actual inference latency, the best success rate reaches only 44% and the best Pass^3 is only 36%, demonstrating that real-time interaction remains challenging. To separate serving latency from models' real-time streaming capability, we compare Wall Clock with Paused Clock, which removes inference delays as an idealized reference, and introduce Token Clock, which advances simulated time according to generated token counts, testing temporal awareness independently of physical serving speed. We find that temporal costs limit success in evolving environments and that higher reasoning effort has mixed effects under time pressure, reflecting a trade-off between decision quality and timely execution. These findings suggest directions for improving real-time multimodal agents, including adaptive reasoning with temporal awareness and continuous perception and action.
Summary / 总结
Real-time interaction requires agents to perceive evolving environments and act under temporal constraints, yet existing evaluations of real-time streaming models focus on conversational quality and streaming perception, leaving effective action under strict temporal constraints untested.
FALCON: FPGA-Accelerated Latency-Certifiable Occupancy Networks for Camera-Based Autonomous Driving
Authors: Haohua Que, Mingkai Liu, Handong Yao
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-10-15T16:14:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:14:24+00:00
Comments: Accepted to WACV 2027 (Applications track). 10 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A perception system that must brake for a pedestrian does not need a low average latency. It must deliver an answer before the deadline on every frame, the worst case included. Commodity edge accelerators optimize the mean through dynamic scheduling, OS jitter, thermal throttling, and contention, so their per-frame latency can be measured but not bounded. We build FALCON, a camera-based 3D occupancy front-end on the AMD Kria KR260, an embedded FPGA drawing 4.3 W idle and up to 6.6 W under inference, and make the latency guarantee layered and explicit: programmable-logic (PL) compute is cycle-bounded analytically; inference is a measured envelope over 100k-frame traces; and a watchdog enforces output delivery, publishing the previous output flagged stale when a frame is late. The deadline runs use a fixed-work proxy of six ResNet-50 DPU inferences per frame, not the full occupancy network: across three 100k-frame enforced runs, all 300k proxy frames delivered an output within the 100 ms deadline plus at most 10.2 ms, the worst of the three runs. The full six-camera FlashOcc pipeline composes to about 1.3 s per frame on the board and is not yet a 10 Hz system. The two operations the DPU cannot run, a view-transform gather and a bilinear resize, become fixed-trip-count PL IPs numerically matched to their software references. On the full Occ3D-nuScenes validation set the board reaches mIoU 29.88, 92.8% of an FP32 baseline the DPU cannot run. On a ResNet-50 probe over 100k frames the KR260 tail stays within 1.5 times its median (2.0 times for the six-inference proxy), where Jetson Orin reaches 4.4 times and the worst datacenter GPU run (NVIDIA L4) 23.6 times. The deployment recipe, HLS sources, and raw timing traces are released.
Summary / 总结
A perception system that must brake for a pedestrian does not need a low average latency.
