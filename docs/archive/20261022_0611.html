SolarRenderer: Few-Step Generative Reference Rendering
Authors: Guian Fang, Shengju Qian, Dongyue Lu, Junchao Huang, Lutao Jiang, Xin Wang, Yiren Song, Li Jiang, Mike Zheng Shou
First: 2026-10-20T17:59:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:59:48+00:00
Comments: Technical Report; 28 pages, 17 figures, 10 tables; Project page at https://enderfga.cn/SolarRenderer Code at https://github.com/Enderfga/SolarRenderer
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Game engines and coding agents can specify the layout, motion, and camera of a scene, yet turning such coarse structure into realistic video requires a renderer that follows the structure and completes the appearance it leaves open. Existing renderers are tied to the representation of one engine, need tens of denoising steps or a costly distillation of their own, and learn from conditions that are not aligned with their target videos. We present SolarRenderer, a structure-conditioned video renderer that adapts a frozen MiniMax-H3 33B backbone with two lightweight LoRAs. Structural videos enter the native reference pathway of the backbone at the positions of the frames they describe, without a type identifier and described only in plain text, so that a single control LoRA follows arbitrary structural representations. Starting from a pretrained four-step LoRA, structure-guided distribution matching renders in four steps, 12.3 times faster than 50-step sampling, while preserving the learned control. A block-causal variant extends the same design to streaming and renders 768p video in real time. To train the model, we build a frame-aligned corpus of 136K clips from game engines, simulators, and YouTube videos. Experiments on an in-distribution test set and a generalization benchmark of 16 structural representations show that SolarRenderer achieves state-of-the-art results across both, and generalizes to 11 unseen representations, including pose skeletons, drawings, and point clouds.

Game engines and coding agents can specify the layout, motion, and camera of a scene, yet turning such coarse structure into realistic video requires a renderer that follows the structure and completes the appearance it leaves open.
Learning Adaptable Dexterous Skills via Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking
Authors: Yuxuan Kuang, Jiashun Wang, Yixuan Sun, Yufei Ye, Sungjae Park, Umar Iqbal, Katerina Fragkiadaki, Shubham Tulsiani
First: 2026-10-20T17:59:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:59:32+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://yuxuank.com/AdaDex/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Dexterous multi-finger hands hold promise to unlock diverse skills like functional grasps, in-hand reorientation, and dynamic handover that the human-designed world assumes, yet existing learning recipes force a trade-off. Goal-reaching reinforcement learning generalizes to arbitrary object poses but, lacking any human prior, converges to unnatural and unreliable finger strategies; motion tracking reproduces human-like behavior from a single clip but cannot deviate from it. We present AdaDex, a goal-conditioned RL framework that turns a single hand-object trajectory of a few seconds---from mocap data or HOI reconstruction from in-the-wild RGB(D) video---into an adaptable dexterous skill. At the core of AdaDex is Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking: a shared policy is trained on two populations of massively parallel environments: motion tracking environments trained with dense motion tracking objective, which instill the human prior from human motion; and generalization environments with noised initial states and goals and a sparse object-success reward, which force the skill to work far off the reference. From seconds of human motion per skill, the resulting policies can learn diverse adaptable dexterous skills, such as functional grasping, object reposing, in-hand reorientation with finger gaiting, and dynamic behaviors like throw-and-catch. Our method outperforms baselines in terms of generalization and functionality, and we demonstrate transfer across simulators and to the real world. Project page: https://yuxuank.com/AdaDex/.

PsycheMME: A Psychology-Informed Benchmark for Social Understanding and Interaction in Videos
Authors: Heran Hu, Yuhang Dong, Zhucun Xue, Kaiwei Chen, Yong Liu, Jiangning Zhang
First: 2026-10-20T17:59:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:59:00+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Understanding human needs and selecting responses require distinct judgments that aggregate accuracy can obscure. We introduce PsycheMME, a video benchmark informed by psychology, with eight capabilities spanning grounding, states, needs, interaction decisions, memory, and risk. Drawing on Social Information Processing and the Perception-Action Cycle, this organization separates human understanding from response selection. We construct 1,000 questions from 630 episodes through independent answering with proposed references withheld and expert review. Our Reasoning-Verified protocol weights answer credit by the support and logical consistency of explicit rationales against annotated cues. Across 17 models, Timing has the lowest Verified scores and credit retention, with weaker support judgments for correct Timing answers. Models with similar Need scores still differ in answer completeness on open questions. Input controls and SFT/GRPO examine context dependence and adaptation. Evaluating answers alongside rationales locates where models fail across understanding, response selection, and justification.
Summary / 总结
Understanding human needs and selecting responses require distinct judgments that aggregate accuracy can obscure.
MindView: Benchmarking Generative Multi-View Spatial Intelligence
Authors: Yanbo Wang, Siqi Luo, Siyu Zhang, Chenming Zhu, Wenzhao Zheng, Qian Yu, Juncheng Yan, Bin Fu, Tai Wang, Yu Qiao, Yihao Liu, Jiwen Lu
First: 2026-10-20T17:58:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:58:25+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Spatial intelligence has recently been assessed through generative tasks such as spatially grounded image editing, yet language-guided generation of spatially consistent views from partial multi-view observations remains underexplored. We introduce MindView, a benchmark for generative multi-view spatial intelligence with 1,500 samples from 513 scenes across three indoor datasets. Its seven tasks form two families: geometry-dominant tasks specify changes in camera position and orientation, while semantics-dominant tasks require inferring target viewpoints by grounding referred objects and interpreting spatial relations and reference-frame cues. Evaluation of eleven generators reveals substantial room for improvement on MindView. A paired context analysis further shows that all eleven generators obtain lower overall scores under the expanded context setting. These findings motivate us to investigate whether task-specific supervision can improve target-view generation and whether models can use additional multi-view evidence effectively. We therefore fine-tune BAGEL on the MindView training dataset, improving its overall score by 24.3 points and yielding gains across all seven MindView tasks. Beyond these generation gains, the fine-tuned model also yields higher scores on three spatial-understanding benchmarks without training on their annotations, suggesting that learning to generate spatially grounded views may benefit spatial understanding. To improve evidence utilization under expanded context, we propose MindCue, a training-free spatial evidence selector guided by a target-view hypothesis. MindCue reverses the drop in overall score under expanded context and outperforms the standard-context baseline. Overall, MindView provides a concrete testbed for generative multi-view spatial intelligence.
Summary / 总结
Spatial intelligence has recently been assessed through generative tasks such as spatially grounded image editing, yet language-guided generation of spatially consistent views from partial multi-view observations remains underexplored.
Continual Concept Personalization in Diffusion Models via Residual-Space Adaptation
Authors: Yuzhi Liu, Enis Simsar, Giulia Lanzillotta, Thomas Hofmann
First: 2026-10-20T17:26:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:26:18+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Text-to-image personalization aims to equip pretrained diffusion models with the ability to learn user-specific concepts from only a few reference images. Existing methods, however, typically train each concept independently or assume that all concepts are available simultaneously. In practical applications, concepts often arrive sequentially, requiring models to continually learn new subjects, objects, or styles while minimizing interference with previously acquired concepts. We address this continual concept personalization setting by extending residual-space adaptation to the stochastic adaptation process of pretrained diffusion models. Our work achieves the highest concept fidelity among competing continual personalization methods while maintaining near-zero forgetting. Beyond these empirical gains, the learned residual-space dynamics reveal where adaptation capacity is concentrated within the diffusion model. Restricting adaptation to the identified layers by our method preserves nearly the same performance while reducing the number of trainable parameters by up to 72\%, demonstrating that residual-space dynamics can identify the layers that contribute most to continual concept personalization and directly guide more parameter-efficient adaptation.
Summary / 总结
Text-to-image personalization aims to equip pretrained diffusion models with the ability to learn user-specific concepts from only a few reference images.
OverSeer: Multi-Agent World Models Beyond What Agents See
Authors: Runjia Li, Kam Woh Ng, Minghao Chen, Junlin Han, Jianyuan Wang, Zhaochong An, Juan-Manuel Perez-Rua, Andrea Vedaldi, Shikun Liu
First: 2026-10-20T17:17:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:17:59+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Despite the success of video world models, extending them to multiple agents is difficult because it requires generating several agent-specific video streams that are consistent in time and between agents. To address this gap, we introduce OverSeer, a multi-agent world model that augments egocentric agent views with auxiliary camera views. We suggest that, by fixing the auxiliary views in space, they act as a proxy for a world-centric representation: Unlike the agents' views, which can change entirely due to a simple change in agent position, changes in the auxiliary views directly map to localized changes in the world state. We show that these fixed views substantially help the model to maintain consistency. We also show that, due to their redundant and semi-static nature, the auxiliary views can be efficiently compressed with a delta-VAE that encodes only the changes relative to a static reference image, reducing the number of tokens required to process them. Experiments in Minecraft and Doom demonstrate improved world and agent consistency, with qualitative results on real-world multi-camera captures indicating that the approach is not tied to game engines. Our compression analysis further shows that delta-VAE substantially reduces the computational overhead of auxiliary-view generation. We conclude that these fixed views provide a simple and efficient way to improve multi-agent world models.
Summary / 总结
Despite the success of video world models, extending them to multiple agents is difficult because it requires generating several agent-specific video streams that are consistent in time and between agents.
TreeWalker: Partial Evaluation for Grouped Tree-Ensemble Inference
Authors: Durmus Karatay, Richard Newman
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-02T18:49:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:00:07+00:00
Comments: 23 pages, 9 figures, 11 tables. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Code and data: https://github.com/Shopify/treewalker/tree/neurips2026-final
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Many prediction tasks evaluate a trained tree ensemble on groups of rows that share feature values: discrete-time survival models expand each patient into $G$ time steps, click-through-rate models score every item in a search session, and scenario analyses vary a few inputs while holding the rest fixed. Standard inference treats each row independently and ignores this input-side correlation. We present TreeWalker, which instantiates partial evaluation for grouped tree-ensemble inference: constant features are static, varying features dynamic. TreeWalker walks each tree once per group, partitions a row bitmask at varying splits, and skips empty subtrees via an unsplit shortcut. It reads standard LightGBM and XGBoost models; training is unchanged. We prove a structural work decomposition: per-tree work splits into the constant-projected subtree size $|T_c|$, $G$ leaf writes, and a predicate-mask provisioning cost $Q$. For the trace evaluator, per-row work approaches $(d_v+1)/(d+1)$ as $G \to \infty$, giving asymptotic speedup $1/(1-\barρ_G)$. On Intel, TreeWalker is ${\sim}3\times$ faster than a row-independent traversal at the reference model size ($T{=}500$, $L{=}8$; $G{=}16$ for SUPPORT/FLCHAIN and empirical groups for Expedia) and $7.7$--$9.1\times$ faster at $G{=}128$ on the survival datasets; on Arm, $2.5$--$2.9\times$ and $5.5$--$6.0\times$. On a scenario-analysis benchmark it is faster in all $16$ configurations on both machines. For f64 models, outputs match treelite GTIL up to tree-ordering roundoff; for f32 models, TreeWalker's f64 leaf accumulation is closer than native f32 to an exactly rounded reference on every audited row.

Many prediction tasks evaluate a trained tree ensemble on groups of rows that share feature values: discrete-time survival models expand each patient into $G$ time steps, click-through-rate models score every item in a search session, and scenario analyses vary a few inputs while holding the rest fixed.
Copyright Leakage Detection in AI Generated Videos
Authors: Shaif Chowdhury, Moshiur Rahman Tonmoy
First: 2026-10-20T16:59:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T16:59:22+00:00
Comments: Accepted in the ICECER 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Video generation models can create realistic-looking video footage from images and text prompts. Some of the recent advances have raised concerns about the reproduction of copyrighted visual content. However, copyright leakage detection in generated videos remains largely unexplored. In this paper, we introduce a new benchmark data set for copyright leakage detection, comprising 50 copyrighted concepts, each with 5 images. For each concept, we have 5 videos created using the image+prompt video generation model and the prompt-only video generation model. Following that, we develop an AI toolset based on a contrastive retrieval framework that formulates copyright leakage detection as a video-to-image retrieval problem by matching aggregated video frame embeddings against a database of copyrighted image embeddings. Our method uses a single similarity threshold and retrieves the most similar copyrighted reference as supporting evidence. We conducted experiments showing strong retrieval and detection performance, achieving 99.6\% detection accuracy with only a single false negative. These results show the effectiveness of the retrieval-based method for scalable and explainable copyright leakage detection in AI-generated videos. Data can be accessed publicly from https://zenodo.org/records/21386117 and the source code for this project is publicly available at https://github.com/Shaif95/video-cp-Leakage.
Summary / 总结
Video generation models can create realistic-looking video footage from images and text prompts.
SenseAlign: Understanding Vision and Touch through Language at Test Time
Authors: Chuyang Ye, Qinting Jiang, Jingyan Jiang
First: 2026-10-20T16:50:13+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T16:50:13+00:00
Comments: 29 pages, including references and appendices
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Tactile-visual-language models combine vision and touch through a shared language space to recognize objects and materials. In real-world deployment, changes in lighting, contact quality or sensor hardware can shift visual or tactile inputs and degrade recognition performance. When both modalities contribute to a prediction, an unreliable modality can override correct evidence from the other. Since labeled data are generally unavailable during deployment, test-time adaptation offers a way to recover recognition performance from unlabeled observations. In this setting, we find that comparing accumulated observations with the text embeddings of candidate classes reveals modality reliability differences that confidence can miss. Refining features against competing classes also improves recognition beyond geometric alignment alone. These findings motivate SenseAlign, which uses a common semantic reference for test-time modality weighting and feature correction. Alignment Trust weights each modality according to its support for shared predicted classes. Anchor Realignment uses the same class text embeddings to fit a shared feature correction that separates predicted classes from alternatives. Both modules use the same unlabeled history while keeping all encoders frozen. Extensive experiments across diverse datasets demonstrate strong recognition performance under both natural shifts and controlled corruptions.
Summary / 总结
Tactile-visual-language models combine vision and touch through a shared language space to recognize objects and materials.
Learning from Automatic 3D Gaussian Splatting Edits for Online Bayesian Scene Change Detection
Authors: Changrui Zhu, Simon Julier
First: 2026-10-20T16:36:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T16:36:39+00:00
Comments: 34 pages, 10 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Online scene change detection requires integrating incoming observations to determine which regions have changed relative to a reference scene. We update change beliefs as new images arrive using generalised Bayesian evidence fusion. However, the prior probability of change and evidence models are unknown beforehand, and numerical Bayesian inference can be costly. We address these challenges by learning offline from automatic edits to a reference 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) model. These edits generate labelled training views without manual annotations. Together with unchanged reference observations, they provide supervision for learning scene-specific evidence models and estimating the effective pixel prior and response statistics. Online prediction combines current evidence with historical support, while closed-form local Bayes factors, parameterized by the learned response statistics, update an accumulated 3D Gaussian field. Object-guided refinement produces the final change masks. Our method achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on PASLCD and CCS3D, with $67.64\%$ and $75.82\%$ mIoU, respectively. On PASLCD, the complete pipeline runs at $17.2$\,FPS over complete query sequences, the fastest among the evaluated online methods.
Summary / 总结
Online scene change detection requires integrating incoming observations to determine which regions have changed relative to a reference scene.
