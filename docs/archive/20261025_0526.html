WorldForge V2: Eliciting Camera-Trajectory Alignment in Video Diffusion Models
Authors: Chenxi Song, Yanming Yang, Tong Zhao, Ruibo Li, Joey Tianyi Zhou, Chi Zhang
First: 2026-10-22T17:53:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:53:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Video diffusion models have learned rich 3D/4D priors, making them promising for scene generation and video re-rendering. However, their use in spatial tasks is limited by imprecise camera control, spatial and temporal inconsistencies, and entangled camera motion with scene dynamics. Existing approaches, such as task-specific fine-tuning and post-process warping, often introduce visual artifacts, generalize poorly, or incur high computational costs. We propose WorldForge V2 to achieve accurate camera control while preserving the generative capabilities of pretrained video models. We first develop a training-free guidance framework using warped references to iteratively refine generation along a target camera path. Optical-flow analysis identifies motion-related latent channels for selective guidance, helping separate motion control from appearance. A dual-path strategy further compares guided and unguided predictions to correct artifacts from imperfect references. Together, these components guide viewpoint changes while preserving scene appearance and object motion. To reduce refinement costs, v-cache approximates the correction using the preceding denoising prediction, avoiding an additional model evaluation. We further extend motion localization from latent channels to network blocks and introduce Trajectory LoRA, which concentrates adaptation on blocks most responsive to trajectory guidance. A two-stage training strategy teaches these adapters to denoise the states encountered during guided generation, using limited supervision effectively. This enables reliable camera control with very little training data, with a single training scene already supporting generalization to unseen scenes. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations across multiple datasets show that our method achieves state-of-the-art generation quality and trajectory accuracy in camera-controlled video generation.

Video diffusion models have learned rich 3D/4D priors, making them promising for scene generation and video re-rendering.
Action Learning for Scientific Simulations
Authors: Sarah Liaw, Yuanqi Du, Yilun Du
First: 2026-10-22T17:22:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:22:08+00:00
Comments: 25 pages, 7 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Understanding and controlling how systems evolve is central to science and engineering, where many problems require reasoning about complete trajectories. Existing approaches typically learn local dynamics or generate trajectories, but objectives constructed from individual transitions can miss dependencies across time, while generative models do not necessarily provide a tractable objective for evaluating and optimizing arbitrary paths. We study $\textit{action learning}$, the problem of recovering a stochastic action from trajectory observations, yielding a common objective for reasoning about dynamical systems. We introduce $\textbf{LEAP}$ ($\textbf{L}$earned $\textbf{E}$nergy on $\textbf{A}$ction $\textbf{P}$aths), which anchors the unknown action to a tractable reference process and learns the remaining correction by comparing observed trajectories with reference trajectories. This construction yields a differentiable objective on path space that can capture both local dynamical structure and nonadditive dependencies across time. Once learned, the same action can be flexibly reused for path ranking and inference, mechanism steering, constrained optimization and sampling. Across stochastic physical systems, many-body orbital dynamics and molecular simulations, LEAP recovers underlying dynamics, captures dependencies across time and enables transfer across inference objectives and physical conditions. We show that $\textit{action learning}$ is a promising framework for turning trajectory observations into reusable objectives for scientific inference and control.

Understanding and controlling how systems evolve is central to science and engineering, where many problems require reasoning about complete trajectories.
What Makes a Good Unmasking Policy? A Controlled Study of Learned Unmasking in Diffusion Language Models
Authors: Jinya Sakurai, Xun Xu, Jaehong Yoon
First: 2026-10-22T17:15:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:15:04+00:00
Comments: Preprint.32 pages, 11 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Masked diffusion language models generate tokens in parallel, but their efficiency depends on which predictions are committed. Learned unmasking policies improve the speed--accuracy trade-off, yet differ in supervision, inputs, and capacity, obscuring which choices matter. We view supervised policies as distilling a decoding procedure through a teacher and examine independently what the teacher labels as committable, on which states the policy imitates it, what the policy observes, and how much the number of function evaluations (NFE) must decrease to offset policy overhead. We find that labeling predictions matching the final output is overly permissive at low NFE, and hidden states are weak alone but can complement confidence. About a 10% NFE reduction suffices to offset policy overhead for policies up to 7.5M parameters across tested settings. We therefore train a policy on trajectory-contraction targets, which accept only commitments staying on a reference trajectory, using confidence and hidden states. On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, the resulting 856K-parameter policy achieves the best accuracy--NFE frontier and highest throughput on GSM8K, is competitive at moderate targets on MATH-500 and HumanEval, and largely transfers to Dream-7B-Instruct.
Summary / 总结
Masked diffusion language models generate tokens in parallel, but their efficiency depends on which predictions are committed.
Squaring-the-Sphere: Distortion-Free and Seamless Panorama Generation via CubeAtlas
Authors: Xuhui Zuo, Chenjie Cao, Tengfei Wang, Junta Wu, Yisu Zhang, Chunchao Guo
First: 2026-10-22T17:06:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:06:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Generating 360-degree panoramas requires both local perspective fidelity and global spherical continuity. Existing representations face a trade-off: equirectangular projection (ERP) enables seamless panorama modeling but suffers from severe distortion and a domain gap to pre-trained image generators, whereas cubemaps preserve perspective geometry but often produce inconsistent faces and visible seams. We present Squaring-the-Sphere, a panoramic image generation framework built on a novel representation CubeAtlas, which unfolds all cubemap faces into a single compact canvas. By arranging orientation-aligned, rotated polar regions alongside the equatorial faces, this representation preserves the perspective geometry of cubemaps while organizing the cross-face relationships required for seamless panorama assembly. It further enables a pretrained diffusion transformer to model the complete panorama jointly in a single pass, without altering the backbone architecture or positional encoding. While CubeAtlas has a token count comparable to ERP, its structured layout makes many dense attention interactions unnecessary. We therefore introduce a layout-aware sparse attention mask that preserves the interactions required for panorama continuity while removing geometrically irrelevant token pairs. As a result, CubeAtlas achieves better quality than ERP modeling with lower attention computation. Furthermore, we introduce a training-free super-resolution technique and a fusion scheme that enhance fine details in distortion-free perspective space before achieving the final ERP panorama. Experiments on various benchmarks demonstrate that our method improves geometric fidelity, cross-view consistency, and high-resolution visual quality over previous methods.
Summary / 总结
Generating 360-degree panoramas requires both local perspective fidelity and global spherical continuity.
Beyond Causal Masks: Auditing and Localizing Hidden Future-Token Leakage in Adaptive Sequence Models
Authors: Huiting Liu, Jinsong Wu
First: 2026-10-22T16:34:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:34:32+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Hidden future-token leakage can create false architectural gains: an autoregressive model may appear substantially better under teacher-forced evaluation while its free-running generation deteriorates. We investigate this failure in an adaptive residual-routing decoder whose sequence-level routing summary exposes prefix positions to future tokens despite a correct causal attention mask. An anomalous 24-layer checkpoint reduces validation loss from the baseline's 3.5958 to 1.4259, yet it fails a prefix-invariance audit and has substantially higher reference-model perplexity during generation. Layer-wise diagnostics and implementation inspection trace the violation to a full-sequence summary broadcast through the dynamic routing-query path. Two independently trained 12-layer causal repairs eliminate detectable leakage and return validation loss and reference perplexity to the control scale, removing the large apparent advantage. As diagnostic tools, we use the established prefix-invariance contract, maximum prefix-output discrepancy (LeakScore), complementary behavioral metrics, and layer-wise profiles. Supporting experiments cover six synthetic implementation pairs spanning five module families, clean pretrained controls, controlled fault injection, and cache consistency across numerical precisions. These results illustrate how hidden future-token access can produce misleading claims of architectural progress and why lower teacher-forced loss should be interpreted together with whole-model causal checks and free-running evaluation.

Hidden future-token leakage can create false architectural gains: an autoregressive model may appear substantially better under teacher-forced evaluation while its free-running generation deteriorates.
Distribution-Calibrated Candidate Selection for Personalized Text-to-Image Generation
Authors: Wenyan Xu
First: 2026-07-08T09:07:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:33:40+00:00
Comments: 4 pages, 2 figures, 4 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Subject-driven personalized text-to-image systems commonly generate multiple candidates but return a single output, yet the selection stage is rarely evaluated as an explicit system component. Identity-only selection may overemphasize reference similarity at the expense of prompt alignment and output diversity. We propose distribution-calibrated candidate selection (DCAL), a training-free sequential selector for a predefined ordered prompt set that jointly scores calibrated identity, text alignment, and cross-prompt novelty. All selectors operate on identical pre-generated K=4 candidate pools for all 30 DreamBooth subjects using a fixed LoRA-adapted generator. Relative to the fixed-seed Random-of-4 control, DCAL yields higher 1-LPIPS, CLIP-I, DINO-I, and CLIP-T for 22, 25, 28, and 22 of the 30 subjects, respectively. Compared with identity-only selection, DCAL improves CLIP-T (0.3264 versus 0.3179), CLIP diversity (0.2444 versus 0.2368), and DINO diversity (0.3286 versus 0.3170), while CLIP-I and DINO-I decrease from 0.8250 to 0.8204 and from 0.7560 to 0.7453. Compared with identity-text scoring, prompt-wise novelty calibration increases CLIP-T from 0.3253 to 0.3264 and CLIP diversity from 0.2366 to 0.2444. We further formalize finite-pool Pareto and perturbation-stability properties and analyze inference complexity.
Summary / 总结
Subject-driven personalized text-to-image systems commonly generate multiple candidates but return a single output, yet the selection stage is rarely evaluated as an explicit system component.
Structure-preserving reduced-order models of thin panels in supersonic and hypersonic flow
Authors: Miguel A. Aguilo
First: 2026-10-22T16:19:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:19:57+00:00
Comments: 68 pages, 15 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We build structure-preserving reduced-order models for both challenge problems of the AePW-4 High-Speed Working Group. They are a clamped thin panel at Mach 1.92 (RC-19) and a cantilever plate at Mach 5.85 (HyMAX). The published data for each case hold no time-resolved response and no repeated runs. The physics must therefore be carried by the form of the model. One construction gives two models per problem. A Ritz-Galerkin reference model describes the plate. A learned model keeps the reference model's exact potential, fits the remaining generalized forces, and trains only a dissipation that cannot add energy. Its energy balance holds for any weights by construction. On the trained model the worst relative residual is 2.2e-15. On RC-19, shock relations predict a pressure ratio of 1.521 across the panel. Pressure-sensitive paint measured the same value. Thirty runs of the learned model place the mean dominant frequency within 1.0% of the measured 236 Hz. At one periodic and one chaotic condition, the learned model reproduces the response regime of a published reduced-order model in all sixteen runs. It does so without that model's 113 Hz spectral peak, which the measurements do not show. Measured displacements can also correct the chaotic response through one more force that respects the energy balance. We tested this on synthetic data at the experiment's 5 kHz sampling rate. The state error is 1.25% of the panel thickness without noise and 3.5% with 10 micrometers of noise. On HyMAX the learned model follows the reference model's trailing-edge displacement for 65 ms with a relative error of 3.7%. These results show that a model whose structure carries the physics can match measured flutter behavior when the available data cannot train it.
Summary / 总结
We build structure-preserving reduced-order models for both challenge problems of the AePW-4 High-Speed Working Group.
AnyGameRenderer: Entity-Consistent Generative Rendering Across Games and an Entity-Level Benchmark
Authors: Ruicong Liu, Liangyang Ouyang, Fengbo Lan, Zhixiang Wang, Kaipeng Zhang
First: 2026-10-22T15:58:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T15:58:39+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
AI coding agents can now build small playable games in minutes, but the results usually look like programmer art: gray blockouts, colored geometric primitives, and flat semantic blobs. We present AnyGameRenderer, a video-to-video generative renderer that turns such footage into high-quality visuals with characters, motion detail, and effects. Throughout, it keeps the layout, camera, and every entity's position and timing intact. Our AnyGameRenderer generalizes across a wide range of genres that agents build, such as tower defense, auto-battlers, top-down survivors, turn-based battles, kart racing, and crowd runners. AnyGameRenderer takes plain video as its game-state input, with no complex representations such as engine G-buffers, per-object class labels, or low-poly proxies. Gray blockouts, colored primitives, semantic maps, and wireframes all work as input. It also preserves entity correspondence: group counts stay correct, and entities appear and disappear on the right frame. We implement AnyGameRenderer by LoRA fine-tuning MiniMax-H3 ref2va, a 33B reference-video-conditioned video generation model. Training uses $\sim$10k paired clips from three sources: (i) multi-stream rendering data from 38 agent-made games exported from GEAR, an AI game-creation tool; (ii) two-stream rendering data from Unreal Engine, with programmatically controlled crowds, count changes, and entity scales; and (iii) real gameplay recordings, with pairs reconstructed via 3D-box detection. To evaluate the entity correspondence, we propose an entity-level benchmark of 59 held-out cases with 753 entities, scored against ground-truth boxes. Compared with the base model, AnyGameRenderer better preserves entity correspondence: temporal match rises from 50.9\% to 62.9\%, missed count changes fall from 34.7\% to 21.3\%, and fewer entities are hallucinated or left unrendered.
Summary / 总结
AI coding agents can now build small playable games in minutes, but the results usually look like programmer art: gray blockouts, colored geometric primitives, and flat semantic blobs.
MixPT: Point Transformer with Interleaved Window and Global Attention
Authors: Minkyun Seo, Hyungtae Lim, Jaesik Park
First: 2026-10-22T15:49:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T15:49:28+00:00
Comments: 23 pages, 14 figures, 12 tables. Project page: https://minkyunseo.github.io/MixPT/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Point cloud transformers aim to capture detailed local geometry and relationships across a scene at a manageable computational cost. Two common approaches are window attention and global attention. They differ in how they enable scene-wide interaction, which refers to information exchange among tokens across a scene. Window attention reduces computational cost by restricting self-attention to groups of tokens called windows; in serialized designs, each window contains a fixed number of consecutive tokens from a spatially ordered sequence. Because each block only directly connects tokens within the same window, information exchange across windows relies on changes in window membership across blocks and hierarchical pooling. Global attention, in contrast, directly connects all tokens within a scene, but computing attention over all token pairs can be costly when the number of tokens is large. To balance computational cost and scene-wide interaction, we introduce MixPT, a point transformer that uses a mix of window and global attention blocks in an interleaved schedule, while retaining a single fixed serialization pattern for its window blocks. For scenes with a large number of tokens, MixPT applies global attention to spatially pooled tokens and returns residual updates to the original-resolution stream. Across indoor and outdoor benchmarks, MixPT achieves competitive accuracy with lower latency than representative window and global attention baselines. We further demonstrate the feasibility of scaling the attention stage in depth by repeating the interleaved pattern of window and global attention blocks.
Summary / 总结
Point cloud transformers aim to capture detailed local geometry and relationships across a scene at a manageable computational cost.
A Sparse Topometric Graph for Scalable Cross-Session Indoor Navigation
Authors: Eun Sun Lee, Minkyun Seo, Gwangtak Bae, Junho Kim, Seunggu Kang, Jaesik Park, Young Min Kim
First: 2026-10-22T15:30:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T15:30:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A conventional embodied agent in an indoor environment builds a map by accumulating noisy real-world measurements and results from heavy inference to guide navigation. While the detailed measurements may differ due to scene changes or sensor settings, the unique topological structure of navigable passages or the connectivity between rooms may provide training-free guidance that complements local observations. We propose a sparse topometric graph as an efficient structural representation for robust indoor navigation, alleviating the need for fine-grained matching, which is computationally expensive, memory-intensive, and prone to real-world variations. The map consists of nodes that encode local 3D geometry and distance-weighted edges that capture traversable connectivity. A learned graph-matching network finds consistent subgraph structures across sessions by considering both node-level geometric descriptors and neighborhood connectivity. The holistic contextual matching is robust to local ambiguities caused by weak distinctiveness or repetitive structure, and establishes stable correspondences despite local scene changes, different sensor modalities, or limited spatial overlap. We analyze the practicality of the graph through cross-session point-goal navigation, where a goal specified in a prior reference map must be reached from an unknown initial pose in a later session. Experiments show that our method achieves the highest success rates (77.1% on Gibson and 40.0% under 3D-FRONT scene changes), while reducing memory by more than two orders of magnitude compared to point-level representations. The proposed approach further supports cross-session alignment and goal-directed navigation with strong scalability and robust real-world transfer.

A conventional embodied agent in an indoor environment builds a map by accumulating noisy real-world measurements and results from heavy inference to guide navigation.
