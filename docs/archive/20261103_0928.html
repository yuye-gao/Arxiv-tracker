Cost-Aware Stopping for Freeze-Thaw Bayesian Optimization: When Does One-Step Lookahead Suffice?
Authors: Long Vu, Jongseong Brad Choi, Binoy Ravindran, Zhoulai Fu
First: 2026-10-30T17:36:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:36:06+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Freeze-thaw Bayesian optimization (BO) evaluates candidates in resumable increments but usually stops at a budget or convergence test. We maximize a budget-free utility, the best observed score minus a cost per increment, with a one-step lookahead: advance the candidate with the largest expected improvement (EI) under a fixed surrogate, and stop once no EI exceeds the cost. This rule, EI stopping, never falls below stopping immediately in expected utility, yet it can stop too early. We construct a certificate, a lower bound on the value of two-step lookahead, that proves such a stop premature, and repeating this correction never lowers expected utility under the surrogate. Under the surrogate, EI stops are premature on average at low costs and lose little at high costs. On three hyperparameter optimization benchmarks, a pre-registered test finds EI stopping noninferior to the correction in most high-cost settings, while the correction gains at low costs on two of the three; more information sharing in the surrogate raises EI stopping's utility. On the surrogates of two published methods, EI stopping trades lower scores for fewer training increments. EI stopping is a simple cost-aware reference whose utility depends on the surrogate.

Freeze-thaw Bayesian optimization (BO) evaluates candidates in resumable increments but usually stops at a budget or convergence test.
The Knowledge Layer Model (KLM): Evidence-Visible AI Output, Routing Diagnostics, and Negative Results
Authors: Omer Asim Ozturk, Cihan Aydiner
First: 2026-10-30T17:26:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:26:18+00:00
Comments: 47 pages, 1 figure, 14 tables. Includes Appendix A
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
AI answers require more than apparent correctness: reviewers need to inspect their recorded basis. We introduce the Knowledge Layer Model (KLM), an open, implementation-neutral specification for inference records with versioned epistemic assessments, evidence relations, and explicit unknowns. SEDIM, the knowledge and evidence architecture of Nage AI OS, is a reference implementation. We reference KLM v0.3 (repository tag v0.3.0). Empirical material consists of separate internal experiments, not end-to-end validation. A historical platform-domain routing benchmark reports 137/150 classifications across 15 classes (91.3%; nominal 95% Wilson interval 85.7-94.9%); the numerator is recovered from a rounded summary, and historical example membership is unverified. A native-model weight-path configuration yielded 0/15 successful answers in a corrected two-source test. A capacity-growth comparison could not establish a reliable effect because of a defective legacy scorer and missing original outputs. Lexical and scoped diagnostics each yielded AUC 0.008 for silent-versus-contradicted examples, below the registered minimum of 0.65. Legacy packing lost gold-containing text in 118/221 selected cases; repaired packing retained it in 221/221. These measure text presence, not answer quality. Python and TypeScript verifiers reportedly agreed on six checks per synthetic fixture; a separate production disclosure was not accepted. Restoration, re-derivation, human-rubric support, boundary isolation, and bounded-response evaluations remain proposed. Findings are internal and author-reported; historical observations are distinguished from later reproductions. Evidence visibility does not establish correctness, causal reliance, security, or regulatory compliance. Independent replication and a complete public audit package remain future work.

AI answers require more than apparent correctness: reviewers need to inspect their recorded basis.
A richness-sensitive Acoustic Biodiversity Index from species-level detections: classifier benchmarking, false-positive control and validation against standard soundscape indices in an African savanna and an urban zoo
Authors: Muhammad Saeed, Dmitry Mikhaylov, Mohamed H. Alhosani, Yasser F. Al Wahedi, Rory Pilossof, Ahmed Arabi, Walid Shaaba
First: 2026-10-30T17:05:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:05:52+00:00
Comments: 35 pages, 16 figures, 7 tables; supplementary tables and figures included
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Acoustic indices are widely used as rapid proxies for biodiversity, but they summarise the distribution of sound energy rather than the species producing it and often track richness poorly. We revise and test an Acoustic Biodiversity Index (ABI) built from species-level detections. Its primary form, ABI_E, is Shannon diversity of detections normalised by the regional species pool expected at the site and season (H / ln S_ref); temporal stability T is reported as a separate component. We first benchmarked four classifiers on a strongly labelled tropical dataset (1,216 calls, 24 classes) with recording-grouped cross-validation. BirdNET embeddings + logistic regression performed best (macro-F1 0.750 +/- 0.029), and random frame-level splits inflated accuracy from 0.41 to 0.70. We then applied BirdNET v2.4 with a location and season filter to recordings from a riverine savanna in the Zambezi Valley, Zimbabwe, and from three habitats at Emirates Park Zoo and Resort, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates. A stricter post-hoc location filter removed 517 high-confidence detections of 9 implausible species, all at the zoo. After diel-matched rarefaction to equal effort (6 h), the savanna site held 21 species (Hill number 1D = 9.83; ABI_E = 0.38) against 1.3-13 species (ABI_E = 0.03-0.21) at the zoo sites. Across 149 recorded hours, ABI_E correlated strongly with richness (Spearman rho = 0.93), whereas six standard indices did not (best: ACI, rho = 0.48). The stability term was lower at the savanna site (0.39) than at the zoo (0.65-0.67), showing that it penalises the natural diel cycle of bird activity. Species-level indices with explicit false-positive control, a declared regional reference pool and effort standardisation provide a more interpretable biodiversity indicator than energy-based acoustic indices.
Summary / 总结
Acoustic indices are widely used as rapid proxies for biodiversity, but they summarise the distribution of sound energy rather than the species producing it and often track richness poorly.
Any Forcing: Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability for Autoregressive Video Generation
Authors: Jiahua Xu, Yijie Fang, Nannan Wang, Xunhao Lin, Li Cao, Lei Hao, Mingrui Zhu, Fei Gao, Xinbo Gao
First: 2026-10-30T16:56:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T16:56:42+00:00
Comments: Autoregressive video generation
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Autoregressive video generation enables efficient inference through streaming prediction. However, existing methods are typically restricted to fixed sampling steps, limiting the flexibility to balance generation quality and efficiency, while one-step generation suffers from degraded visual quality and error accumulation over long sequences, leading to content drift and generation collapse. To address these issues, we propose Any Forcing, a unified framework for achieving Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability. Specifically, we introduce a unified adversarial discriminator that jointly performs real-distribution alignment, inter-step consistency alignment, and first-frame quality enhancement, enabling flexible sampling while preserving one-step generation quality. Furthermore, we propose a training-free adaptive long-term memory selection mechanism that selects historical states based on global semantic relevance and local value contribution, while remapping their temporal positions to preserve temporal relationships within the memory cache, thereby enabling stable one-step long-video generation. Experiments demonstrate that Any Forcing achieves high-quality and stable one-step autoregressive video generation, achieving 20.9 FPS and 24.7 FPS for short and long video generation on a single H100 GPU, respectively.
Summary / 总结
Autoregressive video generation enables efficient inference through streaming prediction.
GeoFlip: Benchmarking Geometry-Sensitive Physics in Video Generation
Authors: Yuqi Li, Junming Liu, Bowen Hu, Ruize Niu, Huiran Duan, Yingli Tian, Hao Wu
First: 2026-10-30T16:45:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T16:45:07+00:00
Comments: 14 pages, 7 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Video generation models synthesize realistic motion, but can they respond correctly to changes in initial geometry? We introduce GeoFlip, a video generation benchmark centered on physical outcome flips induced by geometry. With shared physical settings and textual prompts held fixed, GeoFlip applies controlled interventions to support extent, object placement, and mechanism configuration, constructing paired scenes with contrasting reference events, such as remaining stable versus falling or tipping. These pairs test whether models translate geometric differences in the first frame into correct physical evolution. The benchmark comprises 482 scene pairs and 964 reference videos across six physical categories, with reference outcomes established through procedural scene construction, physics simulation, and numerical validation and verification of physical events. We evaluate 16 models for image to video generation along three dimensions: event correctness, structural integrity, and similarity to reference videos. We examine evaluation consistency with independent vision language judges. On the 442 conditions shared by all models, the highest event correctness averaged across tasks is 27.98%, revealing substantial limitations in physical generation conditioned on geometry. Model strengths vary across physical mechanisms, and the three evaluation dimensions capture distinct capabilities. Typical failures include ignoring geometric changes, generating incorrect object motion, bending rigid objects, and introducing additional components. Rank correlations between judges reach 0.906 for event correctness and 0.929 for structural integrity. By combining controlled geometric interventions, verifiable physical references, and multidimensional evaluation, GeoFlip assesses progress from visual realism toward physical consistency conditioned on geometry.
Summary / 总结
Video generation models synthesize realistic motion, but can they respond correctly to changes in initial geometry?
TwinPast: Benchmarking Historical Evidence Use in Streaming Video Understanding with Evidence Contrastive Video Pairs
Authors: Haotian Yang, Yong Chen, Cheng Peng, Haobo Wang, Zhongle Xie, Xuanru Li, Gang Chen
First: 2026-10-30T16:31:23+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T16:31:23+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
High accuracy on streaming video benchmarks does not establish whether a model uses visual history. Our review of OVO-Bench and StreamingBench finds that recent frames suffice for 32% of sampled historical questions, while models also answer many questions correctly from the question and options alone. We introduce TwinPast to test whether answers correctly follow changes in history, using 350 human-reviewed video pairs spanning five historical tasks. Each pair holds the recent video context, question, and ordered options fixed while changing critical historical evidence and the correct answer. Each video is evaluated independently, and PairAcc requires correct answers for both histories. We evaluate 34 model and input configurations. For Qwen3-VL-8B and DeepSeek-Flash, historical inputs improve performance on TwinPast, reversing the recent-frame advantage on existing benchmarks. The strongest evaluated streaming system reaches 49.14% PairAcc, compared with 66.57-91.43% for the evaluated general-purpose models. This gap persists across all five tasks. Case studies further show that models select states from the wrong time or contradict recorded event order despite retaining the relevant evidence. These findings highlight the need to connect historical memory and retrieval with reasoning that uses evidence according to the question's temporal references and event relations.
Summary / 总结
High accuracy on streaming video benchmarks does not establish whether a model uses visual history.
Normative Deviation Modeling for EEG-based Depression Detection
Authors: Wan Jiang, Jingqi Cheng, Hui Zhang, Xu Zhao, Xiaojing Chen, Shengeng Tang, Jingjing Wu, Richang Hong
First: 2026-10-27T14:44:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T16:18:19+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Web-enabled digital health services are expanding access to physiological monitoring and mental health screening. However, electroencephalography (EEG)-based major depressive disorder (MDD) detection remains challenging across heterogeneous data sources, where individual variability and acquisition differences may affect disease-related representations. In this work, we investigate the use of healthy normative references for EEG-based MDD detection. Specifically, structural and distributional reference models are independently trained using healthy control (HC) recordings to characterize departures from healthy EEG patterns. We develop two diagnostic approaches that incorporate normative deviations at different representation levels. Scalar-level Normative Deviation-guided Diagnosis (S-NDD) summarizes structural and distributional deviations into scalar scores and integrates them with deep EEG representations. Feature-level Normative Deviation-guided Diagnosis (F-NDD) retains dimension-wise residuals and combines them with deep EEG representations through attention-based aggregation and auxiliary supervision. We also employ target HC-assisted score calibration for cross-source evaluation without updating the trained models. Experiments on two public EEG datasets examine both approaches under subject-independent within-source and bidirectional cross-source settings. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of normative deviation-guided diagnosis across different evaluation settings.
Summary / 总结
Web-enabled digital health services are expanding access to physiological monitoring and mental health screening.
Shock Loading Accesses Reaction Pathways beyond Accelerated Thermolysis
Authors: Mingjie Wen, Jiahe Han, Zihan Zhou, Hao Wang, Xueyong Guo, Qingzhao Chu, Dongping Chen
First: 2026-10-30T16:08:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T16:08:07+00:00

Abstract
Thermolysis and shock loading can produce similar major stable products in energetic materials, despite different chemical evolution and reaction pathways. Here, we develop EMFF-MACE, a reactive machine-learning potential for C/H/N/O energetic materials, enabling thermolysis simulations of 20 materials and shock simulations of 10 on a common potential energy surface. Comparisons at matched normalized reaction progress reveal divergent chemical states and local structural environments. Mean reaction-state similarity decreases from 94.0% at 20% progress to 25.9% at trajectory endpoints, while 47.1-92.6% of shock local environments lie outside corresponding material-specific thermolysis reference domains. Structural and reaction-network analyses reveal enhanced intermolecular reorganization and additional association and rearrangement branches under shock loading, despite partially shared early decomposition chemistry. These findings show that loading history influences the structural states and subsequent reaction pathways accessed during decomposition, rather than merely the rate of thermolysis.

Optimizing Stochastic Policy Geometry for Few-Step Flow Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Zhuguanyu Wu, Congjia Chen, Ruihao Gong, Yang Yong, Shiqiao Gu, Yi Xu, Xianglong Liu
First: 2026-10-30T16:05:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T16:05:17+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Few-step flow models generate samples through a handful of large denoising transitions. Applying GRPO to such models requires introducing stochasticity into the denoising transitions, and when a single transition spans a wide range of noise levels, the choice of the resulting stochastic policy becomes a major factor in optimization. Recent constructions constrain the policy to preserve the target flow coefficients, but this constraint admits a family of policies and offers no criterion for choosing among them. We introduce Geo-FSRL (Geometry-Optimized Few-Step Reinforcement Learning), built on a selection principle: at a fixed conditioning state and timestep pair, choose the admissible policy that minimizes the conditional KL divergence induced by any given nonzero velocity-output update. Within a coefficient-preserving family of isotropic Gaussian transitions, this criterion reduces to minimizing a single scalar geometry factor that determines the conditional KL for every velocity-output update. The same factor governs velocity-space Fisher information and conditional transition log-likelihood-ratio variance. We derive the unique closed-form optimizer within this family, shared across all nonzero update magnitudes and directions, with transition coefficients determined by both the source and target noise levels. Because the minimized geometry factor still varies across timesteps, we further adopt gradient-scale matching from prior work and derive closed-form policy-gradient and KL weights that normalize explicit score scales and reference-KL curvature. Geo-FSRL trains on the original few-step schedule and retains the deterministic solver at inference. Our method achieves higher final reward scores than Flow-GRPO and Flow-CPS on both image generation and video generation models under the 4-step setting. Code is available at https://github.com/ModelTC/LightX2V/tree/main/lightx2v_rl

Few-step flow models generate samples through a handful of large denoising transitions.
Observable-Specific Validity Tests for Scientific AI: Controlled Approximations and Intervention Response
Authors: Xiaoshn Nee, Haobo Zhong, Xiaomin Ni
First: 2026-10-30T15:57:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T15:57:51+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
An approximation can reproduce an energy while missing its response, or satisfy an error tolerance without being certified by a given bound. We formulate observable-specific validity records for scientific artificial intelligence (AI) that link a reduction to its reference model, error measure, tolerance, parameter domain, and transformations or interventions. Explicit displacement and velocity certificates for a singularly perturbed oscillator recover distinct convergence orders and provide sufficient validity guarantees. Exact Hubbard dimer relations resolve the different coupling domains accepted by energy and response criteria. Across 16 repeated neural fits, imposing dimensional homogeneity reduces energy scale transfer error from 52.3% to 0.0768% and raises the median fraction jointly satisfying gap and response tolerances from zero to 100%. Paired transformations verify the protected scaling relation, while a coupling crossover identifies the additional information needed for response transfer. A causal model recovers a positive intervention response beneath a strong negative observational association. For two mechanisms observed with Gaussian noise whose responses first differ at order $x^k$, we derive an exact fixed control query requirement scaling as $x^{-2k}$ and verify its full success curve numerically. Together with a 256-task evaluation interface, these results provide independently checkable tests of controlled approximation, physical transfer, and mechanism discrimination in scientific AI.
Summary / 总结
An approximation can reproduce an energy while missing its response, or satisfy an error tolerance without being certified by a given bound.
