Real-time virtual circuits for plasma shape control via neural network emulators
Authors: Alasdair Ross, George K. Holt, Kamran Pentland, Adriano Agnello, Nicola C. Amorisco, Pedro Cavestany, Aran Garrod, Timothy Nunn, Charles Vincent, Graham McArdle
First: 2026-05-14T15:15:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T17:53:53+00:00
Comments: V3: Updates after peer review comments. Added new figure 5, and Table 5. Text clarifications in all sections
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reliable position and shape control in tokamak plasmas requires accurate real-time regulation of several strongly coupled shape parameters. The control vectors that disentangle these couplings, referred to as \textit{virtual circuits} (VCs), enable independent shape parameter control for a specific Grad--Shafranov (GS) equilibrium. Numerical calculation of VCs is not currently feasible in real time, therefore VCs are usually computed prior to each experiment, using a small number of reference GS equilibria sampled along the desired scenario trajectory, with each VC used to control the plasma within a preset time interval. While effective near the reference equilibrium, this approach can lead to degraded performance as the plasma departs from the reference equilibrium and/or from the desired trajectory, and it complicates the design of robust control strategies for rapidly evolving plasma configurations. In this paper, we construct neural-network-based emulators of plasma shape parameters from which VCs can be derived, to provide the MAST Upgrade (MAST-U) plasma control system with state-aware VCs in real-time. To do this, we develop an extensive library of over a million simulated GS equilibria, covering a substantial portion of the MAST-U operational space. These emulators provide differentiable functions whose gradients can be rapidly computed, enabling the derivation of accurate VCs for real-time shape control. We perform extensive verification of the emulated VCs by testing whether they disentangle the control problem. The neural-network-based approach delivers high accuracy and orthogonality across a diverse range of equilibria. This work establishes the physical validity of emulated VCs as a scalable and general alternative to schedules of precomputed VCs.

Reliable position and shape control in tokamak plasmas requires accurate real-time regulation of several strongly coupled shape parameters.
HIVE-Diff: High Frame Rate HDR Video Reconstruction from a Single LDR Frame and Events

Abstract
High frame rate HDR video is important in applications such as autonomous driving and scientific imaging, where rapid motion and extreme illumination often occur simultaneously. Conventional frame cameras face a trade-off between temporal resolution and dynamic range, while event cameras provide complementary high-speed brightness-change measurements over a wide illumination range. These properties have motivated event-based HDR reconstruction, with existing methods broadly following event-only and hybrid event-frame paradigms. Event-only HDR reconstruction, however, suffers from brightness and color drift due to the lack of absolute intensity references. Hybrid event-frame methods alleviate this ambiguity using low-dynamic-range (LDR) frames as anchors, but most perform one-to-one HDR reconstruction at available LDR timestamps. In this work, we investigate single-reference HDR temporal expansion, reconstructing N HDR frames from one LDR reference and events instead of N aligned LDR observations. We develop HIVE-Diff, an event-conditioned video diffusion framework that decomposes the task into event-guided temporal generation and HDR content completion. Because paired real HDR-event data are scarce, we train on synthetic data and adapt the model to real event domains using LDR-event sequences as proxy supervision with teacher-student distillation. Experiments on synthetic and multiple real-world datasets demonstrate strong reconstruction quality and robust real-data adaptation.

Inference-Time Alignment for Diffusion Models via Variationally Stable Doob's Matching
Authors: Jinyuan Chang, Chenguang Duan, Yuling Jiao, Ruoxuan Li, Yi Xu, Jerry Zhijian Yang
First: 2026-01-10T10:28:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T16:58:32+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Inference-time alignment for diffusion models aims to adapt a pre-trained reference diffusion model toward a target distribution without retraining the reference score network, thereby preserving the generative capacity of the reference model while enforcing desired properties at inference time. A central mechanism for achieving such alignment is guidance, which modifies the sampling dynamics through an additional drift term. In this work, we introduce variationally stable Doob's matching, a novel framework for provable guidance estimation grounded in Doob's $h$-transform. Our approach formulates guidance as the gradient of the logarithm of an underlying Doob's $h$-function and employs gradient-regularized regression to simultaneously estimate both the $h$-function and its gradient, resulting in a consistent estimator of the guidance. Theoretically, we establish non-asymptotic convergence rates for the estimated guidance. Moreover, we analyze the resulting controllable diffusion processes and prove non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for the generated distributions in the Wasserstein-2 distance. Finally, we show that variationally stable guidance estimators are adaptive to unknown low dimensionality, effectively mitigating the curse of dimensionality under low-dimensional linear subspace assumptions. Numerical experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed method and show the advantages compared to the baselines.

Abstract
We study the gradient query complexity of sampling from a density $π(x)\propto\mathrm{e}^{-V(x)}$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$, where $V$ has a $β$-Lipschitz gradient but need not be convex. Our approach uses the proximal sampler, which alternates Gaussian perturbation with sampling a position conditional on the noisy observation. We implement its observation chain using the high-order noisy conditional sampler of [OpenAI 2026], obtaining improved dimension dependence under functional inequalities. If $π$ satisfies a log-Sobolev inequality (LSI) with constant $α$, then for every fixed $ν>0$, there is a finite constant $C_ν$ depending only on $ν$ such that the algorithm returns a sample within total-variation distance $\varepsilon$ using at most $C_νκ(d+κ+\varepsilon^{-1}+\|\nabla V(x_0)\|^2/β)^ν$ queries, where $κ=β/α$ and $x_0$ is an arbitrary reference point for initialization. The analysis controls center-dependent local errors along exact proximal histories and removes the final observation noise by conditional decoding. Under a Poincaré inequality (PI), we obtain a corresponding guarantee from a warm start with a known bound on its Rényi divergence. An oracle lower bound shows that a stationary point alone, which suffices under LSI, is insufficient under PI.

Authors: Yexin Liu, Yunlong Lin, Zehan Wang, Guangxin He, Xinli Xu, Jiajun Zha, Yaofu Liu, Yueze Wang, Bowen Ping, Yuanming Li, Amie Wang, Bernard Ghanem, Bing Li, Harry Yang

Abstract
Video dubbing aims to synthesize lip movements that match target speech while preserving the speaker identity, head pose, and background of a source video. Existing editors are predominantly trained via self-reconstruction under original paired audio, even though the source mouth becomes a conflicting target when driving speech is replaced. We introduce replacement-aware cross-audio supervision: paired-audio samples use full-frame reconstruction, whereas unpaired replacement-audio samples mask out source-mouth reconstruction losses and supervise the mouth solely through audio-visual synchronization while preserving unmasked facial regions and background. Because offline dubbing provides the complete source video and target speech beforehand, we present VidDub, a full-context diffusion editor distilled into two denoising steps (2 NFE). Our replacement-aware teacher first learns accurate lip synchronization without regressing toward conflicting source mouth movements. Matched trajectory regression then transfers the teacher's CFG-guided trajectories to a two-step student, and distribution matching distillation recovers visual quality after step compression. We will release the curated training dataset, VidDub-Bench, fixed paired- and cross-audio evaluation manifests, preprocessing outputs, and unified evaluation code. Across three training seeds, masking out conflicting source-mouth reconstruction improves cross-audio Sync-C by 0.833; the final two-step model combines strong lip synchronization with superior visual quality, identity preservation, and visual speech recognition across four benchmarks.

Abstract
AI-generated videos often depict people within detailed scenes while leaving their small facial regions poorly resolved. Visual super-resolution can sharpen these regions without resolving whether the recovered lips and teeth agree with the spoken audio. We present an audio-guided facial video restoration framework that addresses this ambiguity within a reference-guided video flow model. Source--noise mixing initializes reconstruction from a partially preserved observation, and structured reference injection separates appearance anchors from continuous motion context. Training combines restoration of degraded images and videos with correction of degraded dubbed sources under target audio. Appearance and synchronization supervision allow the model to recover facial detail and support visual dubbing when the audio is replaced. On talking-head restoration benchmarks, our method achieves the best paired perceptual similarity and synchronization scores among the compared restoration methods. Component comparisons examine audio conditioning, the reconstruction formulation, and reference injection. EO-Jev: Decision Model for Earth Observation
Authors: Mohamed Atef Abouelhadid, Diaa Addeen Abuhani, Muhammad Akhtar Munir, Fahad Shahbaz Khan, Salman Khan
First: 2026-11-02T16:16:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T16:16:16+00:00

Abstract
Earth observation (EO) workflows require selecting among known outcomes, estimating confidence, and processing large volumes of imagery. Generative vision-language models can address these tasks, but autoregressive decoding is computationally costly and their confidence estimates are unreliable. Decision models offer an alternative, returning probabilities over candidate answers in a single forward pass. However, their accuracy, calibration, and transfer across EO tasks remain insufficiently understood. We investigate these properties through a diagnostic study of d3-flash (8.4B parameters) across 30 settings from 23 public EO benchmarks. We find that compositional reasoning transfers well and calibration improves over its Qwen3.5-9B backbone on 24 settings, while counting, change quantification, damage grading, and absent-object recognition remain challenging. To address these limitations, we adapt d3-flash through low-rank fine-tuning on public EO training splits, producing EO-Jev. EO-Jev improves significantly over d3-flash on 26 of 30 settings and matches or exceeds specialist references on 20 of 28. It also achieves an average gain of 8.0 points across 12 held-out settings and lower expected calibration error than specialists on 21 of 28 settings. These improvements come at a modest cost of 1.8 points in general decision performance. Our results demonstrate the potential of adapting general decision models into competitive EO generalists while highlighting persistent challenges in quantitative perception, option-order consistency, and calibration under distribution shift.
Summary / 总结
Earth observation (EO) workflows require selecting among known outcomes, estimating confidence, and processing large volumes of imagery.
NormMoX: A Normative Mixture of Experts for Explainable Autism Classification from Structural and Functional MRI
Authors: Muhammad Hasan Masrur, Fani Deligianni
First: 2026-11-02T16:02:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T16:02:54+00:00
Comments: 5 pages, 3 figures, 2 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
While structural and functional magnetic resonance imaging (sMRI, fMRI) offer profound potential for researching autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and understanding individual neurodivergence, current classifiers lack the explanatory power needed for scientific study, clinical use and personal-level insights. Most models learn from imaging features that also vary with age, sex, scanner site and head motion. They fuse the two modalities without a common reference and explain decisions against arbitrary reference inputs rather than a typical brain. We present NormMoX, a normative mixture of experts that makes the typical brain the shared reference for prediction, fusion and explanation. Normative models trained on large healthy cohorts give the value each brain measure is expected to take for a person's age and sex. NormMoX learns from each person's deviations from these expected values. For each modality, a neural network expert turns the deviations into a score of evidence for ASD. A fusion head, MoX, compares each score with those of typical controls before combining them. It therefore needs no imputation when a modality is missing and shows in which modality each person is atypical. On 1,035 participants of ABIDE I, NormMoX reached an AUROC of $0.728 \pm 0.004$ in repeated cross-validation, above linear, single-modality and deep fusion baselines. NormMoX thus shows not only whether a person is likely to have ASD, but where, and in which modality, their brain departs from typical. The implementation is available at https://github.com/Novelex/NormMox.

GibbsLens: Training-Free Camera Control via Block-Conditional Gibbs Refinement
Authors: Yuzhu Wang, Xi Ye, Duo Su, Yangyang Xu, Jun Zhu
First: 2026-05-12T09:51:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T15:31:32+00:00
Comments: Accepted by NeurIPS2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Training-free camera control for pretrained flow-matching video generators is a partial-observation inverse problem: a depth-warped guidance video supplies noisy evidence on a subset of latent sites, which the sampler must reconcile with the pretrained prior. Existing methods struggle to balance the trade-off between trajectory adherence and visual quality and the heuristic guidance-strength tuning lacks robustness. We propose GibbsLens, which resolves this dilemma through a structural change to the sampler: each outer hard-replacement guidance step is augmented with an inner-loop block-conditional pseudo-Gibbs refinement on the unobserved complement at the same noise level, with provable convergence to the partial-observation conditional data law. To accelerate convergence on high-dimensional video latents, we exploit their conditional locality, partitioning the unobserved complement into 3D patches, each tracked by a custom mixing indicator that adaptively freezes converged patches. On RealEstate10K and DAVIS, GibbsLens attains the best FVD against all seven training-free and training-based competitors, outperforming every training-free baseline on every reported metric. Project page: https://xiye20.github.io/projects/GibbsLens/.

Abstract
We identify an empirical inference-compute frontier for limit order book prediction on FI-2010. A power law fitted to low- and mid-compute models extrapolates to an excluded MLPLOB-style mixer family whose work is $56$--$2{,}515$ times the largest budget in the corresponding fit. Across nine folds and five horizons, pooled $R^2$ is $0.934$ on the realized target frontier, with 59\% lower RMSE than constant endpoint extrapolation, and $0.731$ on all excluded operating points. Measured latency gives a weaker relationship and reorders model families. We therefore design FastBiNLOB to pack computation into dense temporal and feature mixing. H120 exceeds the published state-of-the-art macro-F1 on two of four horizons in every one of five seeds. Under our CPU/MPS protocol, H96 reduces median latency by 23.6\% relative to the W128 MLPLOB reference, and H120 by 60.2\% relative to a TLOB-inspired dual-axis attention reference.
