PolicyIK: High-Precision Inverse Kinematics for Robot Arms with Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Yuhan Liu, Xinqi Liu, Zixuan Dong, Keith W. Ross, Shenji Wan
First: 2026-11-03T18:59:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T18:59:14+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Inverse kinematics (IK) converts end-effector pose commands into joint motion, and many vision-language-action (VLA) policies rely on it to act on a given arm and to transfer across embodiments. Precise IK remains laborious: redundancy and singularities complicate the inverse mapping, execution depends on hand-tuned controllers, and training data on physical robots are costly to collect. We present PolicyIK, a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning policy for high-precision IK. It updates joint-position references from pose feedback and observes its accumulated reference relative to the measured joints, so it can retain the offset that the position controller needs at rest. Two-scale pose errors with an error-dependent action gain resolve millimeter residuals while preserving large motions. Progress and dwell rewards teach it to both reach the tolerance and stay within it. In simulation, a single attempt by the trained PPO policy keeps the mean errors over the final second within 1 mm and 0.2° on 93.5% of held-out tasks, and a deployment protocol raises success to 98.9%. When trained directly on a physical arm with an off-policy RL learner, PolicyIK achieves higher reaching and holding success than all four classical IK controllers under the same time budgets. In simulated pick-and-place, PolicyIK executes the end-effector commands of the $π_{0.5}$ VLA policy with 95.7% success. These results show that reinforcement learning can provide high-precision inverse kinematics for robot arms. Code is available at https://github.com/Tinaliu0123/PolicyIK.

Inverse kinematics (IK) converts end-effector pose commands into joint motion, and many vision-language-action (VLA) policies rely on it to act on a given arm and to transfer across embodiments.
Visibility-Aware Reference Generation for Benchmarking Visual 3D Reconstruction in Vegetation-Rich Environments
Authors: Zhipeng Zhong, Matthew Coombes, Cunjia Liu
First: 2026-11-03T18:54:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T18:54:52+00:00
Comments: 20 pages, 12 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reliable evaluation of visual 3D reconstruction and depth estimation in vegetation-rich outdoor environments is limited by the scarcity of geometric ground-truth data and the cost of complete scanner surveys. We present a LiDARbased reference-generation pipeline for evaluating the zero-shot performance of feed-forward visual-geometry models alongside conventional structure-from-motion (SfM) methods. The pipeline reuses camera images, LiDAR scans, and reference poses from existing multi-sensor localization datasets to generate per-view depth maps and visible reference point clouds. To address visibility errors in accumulated LiDAR scans, we introduce the LargeDepth-Gap Visibility Score (LDGVS) for point-wise visibility filtering and automatic scale selection in Hidden Point Removal(HPR). Experiments on TartanAirV2 and TartanGround show that, compared with Poisson surface reconstruction, which is an approach suited to completed scan surveys, the combined HPR and LDGVS filtering retains approximately three times the point density at comparable absolute relative error. We also apply the pipeline to the BotanicGarden dataset to systematically compare depth estimation and 3D reconstruction methods. Feedforward methods produce dense depth predictions efficiently, but their 3D reconstructions can contain duplicated structures. Conventional SfM generally produces more coherent geometry, but its reconstructions remain sparse and more prone to failure in challenging vegetated scenes. The benchmark toolkit and datasets will be released.

Reliable evaluation of visual 3D reconstruction and depth estimation in vegetation-rich outdoor environments is limited by the scarcity of geometric ground-truth data and the cost of complete scanner surveys.
MedPhysBench: A Benchmark for Evaluating Language Models on Medical Physics Calculations and Error Detection
Authors: Omar Nusrat
First: 2026-11-03T18:40:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T18:40:35+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Medical physicists turn a prescription into a delivered dose and check one another's calculations, because one error in a beam calibration or a monitor-unit (MU) calculation can affect every patient treated with it. MedPhysBench has ten seeded generators of calculation items in radiation therapy, diagnostic imaging, nuclear medicine and radiation protection, up to clinical tasks such as a TG-51 calibration worksheet. Reference answers come from code implementing the governing protocols, and a grader converts units and applies tolerance bands. From the same inputs, separate checking items test error detection, showing a colleague's worksheet or only the colleague's result, half correct and half with one planted error. On a held-out test split of 1,200 items (half for two models), 12 models scored 33.3 to 100.0% on calculation. Of the nine answers graded incorrect for the four best (99.3-100.0%), five contained model errors and four followed NCRP Report 151 on points our items misstate or leave open. Below them, most models did worst on level-3 clinical tasks, and Claude Sonnet 5.5 omitted the inverse-square correction in 19 of 20 isocentric MU items. Five models detected at least 96.7% of mostly large planted errors with at most 4% false alarms; for four of the other seven, checking only the result raised false alarms significantly. In an exploratory targeted test with 20 public cases of an omitted inverse-square correction, the three best models tested caught it in every worksheet, and three weaker models accepted 16 to 18 of 20 planted worksheets, one of them while applying the correction in most of its own calculations. Claude Opus 5.5 accepted 6 of 20 wrong results, although it applied the correction in all 20 of its own. We report the effect of two problems in our shielding items, found after the runs, and release the code, per-item outcomes and targeted-test responses.

Medical physicists turn a prescription into a delivered dose and check one another's calculations, because one error in a beam calibration or a monitor-unit (MU) calculation can affect every patient treated with it.
Wonder 4: A Mixture-of-Experts Recipe for Image Quality Enhancement
Authors: Yunan Wu, Bin Sun, Partha Acharjee, Xiaoyu Wang
First: 2026-11-03T18:07:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T18:07:52+00:00
Comments: Full version will be released soon
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A real photograph is often blurry, grainy, compressed, scratched, and low-resolution at the same time. Each defect has its own severity and its own place in the image. The corrections for these defects conflict with each other. Exist- ing models describe the whole image with one description, so they cannot say where each correction belongs. To solve this problem, we present Wonder 4, a mixture-of-experts (MoE) model for image quality enhancement. It corrects several defects of a real photograph at the same time, each in the region where it occurs. Every block of the model holds a mixture of N = 13 experts. A router, trained with the model, sends every image patch to k = 5 of them. No degradation classifier, mask, prompt, detector, or task label is used. Another issue is that standard MoE training, taken from language models, pushes every expert toward equal use. Equal use applies every correction everywhere, even where it is not needed. We replace this rule with a bias that only sets a minimum share of use. Across all patches, an expert used less than this minimum gets higher router scores until it is selected again. An expert above the minimum is not changed, so no expert is forced to be used as often as the others. The bias needs no extra loss. The method adds no extra module and no extra processing step. We evaluate Wonder 4 on DIV2K and Flickr2K, against five published methods and three open-weight pipelines. It reaches the best LPIPS, DISTS, and NIQE on all three test settings (DIV2K x4, DIV2K x8, and Flickr2K x4).

A real photograph is often blurry, grainy, compressed, scratched, and low-resolution at the same time.
Route Anything: A Study of Model and Policy Routing for General-Purpose AI Systems
Authors: Pengfei Zhou, Yixing Ma, Hexin Wang, Weidong Tang, Zhenglin Wan, Wangbo Zhao, Yang You
First: 2026-11-03T17:56:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T17:56:42+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Artificial general intelligence (AGI) requires a method that can understand, generate, reason, and act across diverse domains and modalities. Rather than relying on a single model to master every capability, a promising path toward AGI now is to orchestrate a heterogeneous ecosystem of specialized models, tools, and policies (candidates) into a unified AI system. At the heart of this vision lies a fundamental question: how can an AI system dynamically select and coordinate the most capable candidate for each task while balancing performance and computational cost? In this work, we present Route Anything, a unified framework for studying model and policy routing across general-purpose AI systems. We develop a unified theoretical foundation that characterizes optimal routing under quality--cost trade-offs and establishes how the information available to a router determines its attainable performance. To systematically investigate routing across diverse capabilities, we first construct an omni-modal trajectory matrix spanning 25 benchmarks across language understanding, image, video, and speech generation, coding and workplace agents, and robotic manipulation, comprising 374,401 scored runs from 452 candidate configurations. Building on this framework, we train and test different routers to investigate the potential of omni-modal model routing, the capabilities of learned routers, and the conditions under which routing improves efficiency. Our results reveal substantial opportunities for capability orchestration: per-input oracle routing outperforms the best fixed candidate by 5.4 points, and learned routers outperform it by 0.3 points across 16 benchmarks, by 2.1 points on video generation, and by 1.1 points on robot manipulation while using 24% less policy time. More importantly, routing raises efficiency by 8.82x on average compared with the reference model.
Summary / 总结
Artificial general intelligence (AGI) requires a method that can understand, generate, reason, and act across diverse domains and modalities.
Stabilizing On-Policy Targeted Guidance in Agentic Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Kevin Frans, Alexander M Rush, Sergey Levine, Pieter Abbeel, Ashvin Nair
First: 2026-11-03T17:54:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T17:54:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Naive on-policy RL is often bottlenecked by the capabilities of the base model --- insufficient coverage can result in an inability to learn from certain rewards. At the same time, rewards in modern post-training systems often come from rich textual sources, e.g. judge models with a rubric of criteria. Can we utilize such structures to provide a rich directional signal that enables improving on otherwise difficult to optimize rewards? In this work, we examine a framework we refer to as targeted guidance that learns from per-turn textual feedback via an on-policy self-distillation objective. Our focus is not only on directly improving hard-to-optimize behaviors, but on how to do so throughout the training process without degrading baseline RL performance. Over a range of settings across an agentic multi-turn software engineering environment, we find success by combining targeted self-distillation with a per-criteria RL reward, and by limiting feedback to turns where the policy specifically fails. We further examine the design choices that lead to stable entropy and reduce oscillatory behavior throughout training.
Summary / 总结
Naive on-policy RL is often bottlenecked by the capabilities of the base model --- insufficient coverage can result in an inability to learn from certain rewards.
Beyond Unit-Circle Eigenvalues: Invariant Bases for Stable State Space Dynamics
Authors: Jiaqian Zhu, Yang Zhang, Junhua Ding, Xiaowei Yu
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-11-01T20:23:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T17:31:43+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 40 pages, 13 figures, 27 tables. Code: https://github.com/Jiaqian-Janelle/NeurIPS26-Kronecker-Cayley-Stable-State-Space-Dynamics
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Diagonal state space models process long sequences at linear cost, yet the same transition is applied at every step of a rollout, so an unconstrained recurrence holds no quantity fixed and the state decays or grows without bound. Current designs constrain one quantity, and preserving one does not guarantee preservation of the others, so a model can hold its norm to machine precision while momentum drifts by hundreds of percent. We claim that the conserved quantities of a diagonal recurrence are layered, that each layer requires a strictly stronger condition on the operator, and that a unit-modulus spectrum reaches only the outermost. Acting on the claim, we enforce the conditions required for every layer by construction. The generator of the Kronecker-Cayley transition (KC) is a graph Laplacian over object subspaces, $\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{L}\otimes I_m$, advanced by a Cayley transform, and the step stays diagonal at $O(N^3+N^2m)$ cost. The conserved set is exactly a $2m^2$-dimensional family of quadratic forms, held at every parameter value, so the quantities and the dynamics the construction reaches are fixed before training. On four conservation benchmarks, KC holds total mass at or near the reference floor while every diagonal baseline loses it by orders of magnitude. We further compare against mass correction and find that two correctors fail once the underlying prediction diverges, which shows that assembly rather than correction is necessary for a guarantee that holds across the rollout. A free modal basis trained under a conservation penalty conserves only by freezing its spectrum, whereas the assembled operator conserves with every mode but one free to rotate. Beyond these benchmarks, KC holds the linear momentum of three $N$-body systems and, at the operator level, the conserved quantity in molecular dynamics, chemical kinetics, pharmacokinetics and power-grid swing dynamics.
Summary / 总结
Diagonal state space models process long sequences at linear cost, yet the same transition is applied at every step of a rollout, so an unconstrained recurrence holds no quantity fixed and the state decays or grows without bound.
Attribution-based diagnostics for single-cell foundation models
Authors: Farnoush Rezaei Jafari, Tristan Lazard, Kenza Bouzid, Shruthi Bannur, Jeremiah Wander, Ashley Mae Conard, Stephanie Hyland, Lorin Crawford, Daniel Coelho de Castro
First: 2026-11-02T00:32:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T17:31:13+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Single-cell RNA foundation models (scFMs) are increasingly used for in-silico biological discovery, yet predictive performance alone does not reveal whether their decisions rely on biologically meaningful signals or model-specific shortcuts. We systematically investigate post-hoc attribution of downstream predictions across two tasks: cell-type annotation using pretrained Cell2Sentence and scGPT models, and perturbation prediction using scGPT+STATE in a controlled setting. For cell annotation, we use attribution to assess whether predictions rely on established marker genes and how their relevance varies with model architecture, expression rank, and the surrounding cellular context. For perturbation prediction, we compare attribution patterns with known regulatory ground-truth in a synthetic setting to assess what they reveal about the model's learned computations. Across these tasks, attribution reveals biological feature use, contextual interactions, contributions from different input pathways, learning progression, associations with prediction quality, and potential pitfalls that are not fully captured by standard performance metrics. Experiments with complete and limited regulatory knowledge further demonstrate that conclusions drawn from attribution depend strongly on the choice of biological reference, baseline, and null model. Together, these findings highlight the value of attribution as a flexible post-hoc diagnostic for understanding model behavior, identifying potential issues, and informing the development of scFMs.

Single-cell RNA foundation models (scFMs) are increasingly used for in-silico biological discovery, yet predictive performance alone does not reveal whether their decisions rely on biologically meaningful signals or model-specific shortcuts.
Conditional Collapse in Sign Language Production: A Diagnostic and a Coverage Argument
Authors: Rui Hong, Jana Košecká
First: 2026-06-01T03:50:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T17:16:38+00:00
Comments: 23 pages. v2: adds Phoenix-2014T, CSL-Daily and ASL3DWord experiments, the conditioning-coverage analysis, and a frame-level DTW analysis; appendix included
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The goal of Sign Language Production (SLP) is to translate natural language text into corresponding sign language movements performed by a virtual avatar. The quality of the generated motion is typically evaluated by a motion-space Frechet distance (FID) and back-translation BLEU score on benchmarks such as How2Sign. The existing approaches can improve both metrics substantially while the underlying generator fails to represent the input sentence, a failure we call conditional collapse. In this work we propose to evaluate the generated motion at three independent levels: (tau1) initial-pose conditioning, (tau2) output diversity, and (tau3) target faithfulness. We compute these as pairwise-distance ratios using latent representations of a frozen motion autoencoder (MoAE), judged against each dataset's own GT-vs-GT reference. We evaluate diffusion and regression SLP models on How2Sign, including a re-implemented Neural Sign Actors, and show that tau3 faithfulness is never attained, while FID varies by nearly two orders of magnitude and is uncorrelated with faithfulness. We then apply the same models and diagnostic to Phoenix-2014T, CSL-Daily, and gloss-level ASL3DWord and show that faithfulness varies proportionally to how densely the training sentences cover the test conditions (conditioning coverage) and not corpus size. The smaller but densely covered Phoenix-2014T and ASL3DWord datasets generate faithful sign motion, while CSL-Daily follows the input text yet falls short of sign language faithfulness. Using the largest, open-domain How2Sign dataset, the models collapse at every scale. We introduce two measurements of the text data that rank the corpora in the order of how densely the training sentences fill the embedding space, and how close each test sentence lies to its nearest training sentence.

The goal of Sign Language Production (SLP) is to translate natural language text into corresponding sign language movements performed by a virtual avatar.
Post-Inference Model Chaining: A Problem Statement and a Model-based Bootstrap for Training End-to-End Digital Twins
Authors: Lukas Madenach, Timo Bailer, Tobias Eilert
First: 2026-11-03T17:11:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T17:11:06+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
End-to-end digital twins can be constructed by fitting separate stage models and connecting them after inference. We investigate the Frequentist prediction properties of a commonly used Monte Carlo simulation scheme that forwards complete predictive draws through such a chain when measured stage outputs contain independent normal errors, but the latent stage means are deterministic. Analytical two-stage examples show persistent asymptotic interval and coverage inflation and, with quadratic propagation, mean bias dependent on the parameters to be estimated. Simulations of four- and eight-stage linear-quadratic univariate chains with 7, 15 or 30 training runs demonstrate pronounced input-dependent distortions. A diagnostic using error-free latent training predictors shows that the effect is mainly not attributable to errors in variables, but to the MCSim approach even showing underestimation of coverage. We propose a model-based bootstrap: an initial chain forwards fitted means, bootstrap chains are refitted on fixed-center bootstrap data while forwarding their mean prediction, and a target-stage future error enters a studentized prediction root. Across the investigated scenarios, relative mean bias is substantially reduced, and coverage is closer to the nominal 90%. At sample size 30, the eight-stage bootstrap has mean width ratios of 1.16-1.42 relative to the known-parameter normal reference, compared with 4.11-4.97 for observed-to-observed MCSim. These results support improved endpoint prediction in the tested setting. Further investigations of the bootstrap scheme in the supplement for a bivariate 6-stage process show similar satisfactory results.
Summary / 总结
End-to-end digital twins can be constructed by fitting separate stage models and connecting them after inference.
