LongBanana: An Expert-Verified Benchmark for Long-Context Multi-Reference Image Synthesis
Authors: Haoxiang Cao, Yuxuan Zhang, Penghui Du, Bo Li, Jiajiong Cao, Yingying Fan, Zimeng Wu, Yifu Luo, Mingzhe Zheng, Junwen Miao, Huaisong Zhang, Yang Yao, Yu Wang, Dongfu Jiang, Ping Nie, Wenhu Chen, Chaoqun Wang, Changqian Yu, Kelsey R. Allen
Venue: NeurIPS 2026 long
First: 2026-11-04T18:11:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T18:11:56+00:00
Comments: NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets Track. Project page: https://long-banana.com/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We introduce LongBanana, to our knowledge the first expert-designed and expert-verified benchmark for long-context multi-reference image synthesis in the age of agent-driven image generation. The task requires retrieving dispersed visual evidence, rejecting distractors, binding entities and relations, and composing one coherent final image from multiple references. LongBanana contains 90 carefully designed tasks organized by a 3-by-3 taxonomy; each task includes about 32 reference images, 20 constraints, and 50 evaluation items, yielding 4,670 expert-specified atomic evaluation items in total. Its graph-grounded evaluation design instantiates each task with reference images and an entity-relation graph, then converts node, edge, and whole-graph obligations into checklist items for Content Fidelity, Relational Binding, and Global Constraint Satisfaction. Reviewed expert labels include a 15% double-labeled subset with 89.4% exact agreement and 0.83 quadratic weighted kappa. We further introduce LongBanana-Gen, an agent-harness framework for long-horizon image generation; experiments show that minimal agent harnesses improve over direct generation, while a ReAct-Plan-Exec-Reflect (RPR) workflow yields harness-dependent gains by strengthening evidence grounding and repair. Together, LongBanana provides an auditable testbed for measuring dense visual evidence use and a shared target for developing stronger agent-driven image-generation systems. Code and data are available at the project page: https://long-banana.com/.

We introduce LongBanana, to our knowledge the first expert-designed and expert-verified benchmark for long-context multi-reference image synthesis in the age of agent-driven image generation.
GeoFlip Preview: Benchmarking Geometry-Sensitive Physics in Video Generation
Authors: Yuqi Li, Junming Liu, Bowen Hu, Ruize Niu, Huiran Duan, Ming-Hsuan Yang, Yingli Tian, Hao Wu
First: 2026-10-30T16:45:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T18:05:53+00:00
Comments: 14 pages, 7 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Video generation models synthesize realistic motion, but can they respond correctly to changes in initial geometry? We introduce GeoFlip, a video generation benchmark centered on physical outcome flips induced by geometry. With shared physical settings and textual prompts held fixed, GeoFlip applies controlled interventions to support extent, object placement, and mechanism configuration, constructing paired scenes with contrasting reference events, such as remaining stable versus falling or tipping. These pairs test whether models translate geometric differences in the first frame into correct physical evolution. The benchmark comprises 482 scene pairs and 964 reference videos across six physical categories, with reference outcomes established through procedural scene construction, physics simulation, and numerical validation and verification of physical events. We evaluate 16 models for image to video generation along three dimensions: event correctness, structural integrity, and similarity to reference videos. We examine evaluation consistency with independent vision language judges. On the 442 conditions shared by all models, the highest event correctness averaged across tasks is 27.98%, revealing substantial limitations in physical generation conditioned on geometry. Model strengths vary across physical mechanisms, and the three evaluation dimensions capture distinct capabilities. Typical failures include ignoring geometric changes, generating incorrect object motion, bending rigid objects, and introducing additional components. Rank correlations between judges reach 0.906 for event correctness and 0.929 for structural integrity. By combining controlled geometric interventions, verifiable physical references, and multidimensional evaluation, GeoFlip assesses progress from visual realism toward physical consistency conditioned on geometry.
Summary / 总结
Video generation models synthesize realistic motion, but can they respond correctly to changes in initial geometry?
Stabilizing On-Policy Targeted Guidance in Agentic Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Kevin Frans, Alexander M Rush, Sergey Levine, Pieter Abbeel, Ashvin Nair
First: 2026-11-03T17:54:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T17:41:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Naive on-policy RL is often bottlenecked by the capabilities of the base model --- insufficient coverage can result in an inability to learn from certain rewards. At the same time, rewards in modern post-training systems often come from rich textual sources, e.g. judge models with a rubric of criteria. Can we utilize such structures to provide a rich directional signal that enables improving on otherwise difficult to optimize rewards? In this work, we examine a framework we refer to as targeted guidance that learns from per-turn textual feedback via an on-policy self-distillation objective. Our focus is not only on directly improving hard-to-optimize behaviors, but on how to do so throughout the training process without degrading baseline RL performance. Over a range of settings across an agentic multi-turn software engineering environment, we find success by combining targeted self-distillation with a per-criteria RL reward, and by limiting feedback to turns where the policy specifically fails. We further examine the design choices that lead to stable entropy and reduce oscillatory behavior throughout training.
Summary / 总结
Naive on-policy RL is often bottlenecked by the capabilities of the base model --- insufficient coverage can result in an inability to learn from certain rewards.
SimForcing: Distilling Simulation Motion Priors into Real-Domain Robot World Models
Authors: Xiaodong Wang, Tianle Li, Chuanxin Song, Junliang Xie, Zhanmi Zhong, Suiying Wu, Peixi Peng
First: 2026-10-05T16:08:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T16:27:13+00:00
Comments: Code: https://github.com/Wang-Xiaodong1899/SimForcing
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Action-conditioned robot world models must respond precisely to robot trajectories while preserving realistic visual dynamics, yet learning both from heterogeneous robot videos remains challenging. Simulation offers structured motion supervision, but appearance differences hinder direct transfer, and inaccurate simulation predictions can misguide real-video generation. We present SimForcing, a simulation-guided framework that uses simulation both as a source of transferable motion knowledge and as a controllable reference for prediction. First, we transfer motion knowledge from a simulation teacher through latent-motion distillation, aligning temporal changes in latent space to internalize motion priors while mitigating the influence of appearance differences. Second, we introduce multi-block simulation conditioning with condition dropout to exploit predicted simulation trajectories without relying excessively on their accuracy. Our simulation-conditioning classifier-free guidance scheme unifies these two ideas by balancing predictions based on internalized motion knowledge with those additionally guided by simulation latents. The jointly trained student generates both simulation conditions and real-domain videos, requiring no additional world model at inference. On Bridge, SimForcing achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FVD among the compared methods without external embodied pretraining. Evaluation on InternData-A1 further supports its applicability across robot datasets. Moreover, using our trained world model to initialize a vision-language-action model improves LIBERO success, suggesting its utility for downstream policy learning. https://github.com/Wang-Xiaodong1899/SimForcing

Action-conditioned robot world models must respond precisely to robot trajectories while preserving realistic visual dynamics, yet learning both from heterogeneous robot videos remains challenging.
TailSteer: Trajectory-Tail Steering for Drift-Resistant Multi-turn Image Editing
Authors: Ruikang Li, Zixuan Chen, Jiarui Wu, Mingde Yao, Yiyuan Zhang, Yujin Wang, Hanming Deng, Lewei Lu, Tianfan Xue
First: 2026-11-04T16:23:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T16:23:24+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 9 figures. Project page: https://lyricccco.github.io/tailsteer/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Strong single-turn editing performance does not guarantee reliable multi-turn interaction: as edited outputs are repeatedly reused, small unintended changes can accumulate into severe visual drift. This long-term drift is often mitigated through training on multi-turn data or cross-turn rollouts, potentially altering pretrained priors, or through specialized inference-time mechanisms that are difficult to scale to foundation models. We introduce TailSteer, a final-stage training framework that improves multi-turn robustness while retaining the generative and editing capabilities acquired through earlier training. TailSteer follows a simple principle: keep early denoising dynamics close to those of the base model and concentrate preservation corrections near the trajectory tail, where fine-grained deviations can be suppressed. These corrections are learned from the editor's own on-policy denoising rollouts, using unchanged source content as a preservation reference without requiring curated ground-truth editing pairs. Experiments across the FLUX, SenseNova, and Qwen families show that TailSteer substantially reduces cumulative drift over 15-20 editing turns while largely preserving base-model performance on extensive text-to-image and single-image editing benchmarks. These results demonstrate that long-horizon editing robustness can be unlocked in foundation models after TailSteer, rather than by relearning their existing capabilities.
Summary / 总结
Strong single-turn editing performance does not guarantee reliable multi-turn interaction: as edited outputs are repeatedly reused, small unintended changes can accumulate into severe visual drift.
AbsoluteDegradation: A Physics-Inspired Synthetic Film-Degradation Pipeline and Archival Film Restoration Benchmark
Authors: Mikołaj Jastrzębski, Dawid Glinkowski, Dawid Zieliński, Daniel Borkowski, Wojciech Kozłowski, Kamil Adamczewski
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-07-02T13:08:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T15:58:56+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (Evaluations and Datasets Track). 29 pages, 11 figures, 10 tables. Code: https://github.com/TytanMikJas/AbsoluteDegradation ; Datasets: https://www.kaggle.com/absolutedegradation/datasets ; Project page: https://absolutecinema.absolutedegradation.workers.dev
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Restoring archival film remains a fundamentally challenging problem due to the absence of paired training data and the lack of standardized evaluation benchmarks. Pristine versions of deteriorated footage are physically unrecoverable, requiring supervised methods to rely on synthetic data that often fail to capture the complex, temporally coherent nature of real film degradation. At the same time, existing real-world datasets are limited in scale, quality, and accessibility, hindering reliable evaluation and fair comparison across methods. We address both limitations with AbsoluteDegradation, a physics-inspired, modular pipeline for synthesizing realistic film degradations, and a new large-scale archival benchmark. The proposed pipeline models the analog-to-digital process as a structured composition of artifact families, incorporating signal-dependent grain, parametric scratches, and temporally coherent camera motion, enabling controlled generation of diverse degradation regimes. In parallel, we introduce a curated dataset of 81,576 high-resolution frames sourced from real archival footage, designed for consistent evaluation under real-world conditions. Together, these contributions provide a unified framework for training and benchmarking restoration models. Experiments across six restoration architectures, evaluated with no-reference image quality metrics, clip-level temporal quality assessment, and a blinded human preference study (93 raters, 1,395 trials), show that models trained with AbsoluteDegradation generalize better to real-world footage: human raters preferred them in 64.7% of comparisons and for every evaluated architecture. The proposed benchmark also reveals systematic failure modes of current methods and of no-reference metrics. We hope this work establishes a foundation for reproducible and domain-authentic evaluation in archival film restoration.
Summary / 总结
Restoring archival film remains a fundamentally challenging problem due to the absence of paired training data and the lack of standardized evaluation benchmarks.
LifeVal: Evaluating AI Model Performance on Personalized Agentic Consumer Tasks
Authors: Jenny Yang, Elizabeth Proehl, Anjan Bharadwaj, Emily Fan, Charlotte Cole, James Kim, Noam Brown, Phoebe Thacker, Amelia Glaese, Tejal Patwardhan
First: 2026-11-04T15:55:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T15:55:20+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 6 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We introduce LifeVal, a benchmark evaluating AI model performance on personalized agentic consumer tasks. LifeVal covers the most valuable life tasks that a personalized digital superassistant could automate, representing over 1.7 trillion US dollars in annual willingness-to-pay across 148 consumer task categories (e.g., managing finances, moving apartments, searching for a job, finding childcare). To construct our activity distribution, we conducted a new survey of willingness-to-pay for a digital superassistant across a demographically representative sample of over 3,800 consumers, cross-referencing our results against the American Time Use Survey (Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025) and the Multinational Time Use Study (Fisher et al., 2025). For the most valuable consumer activities, we recruited task creators to create AI evaluation tasks personalized using real ChatGPT conversations and memories, reference files, and personal context. Task creators had to pass our expertise threshold (including personally completing the task multiple times and seeing the results through in their real lives for over a year) and also write a rubric, ensuring that our evaluation measured the best personalized completion for that task. All tasks had to pass at least three rounds of human quality-control before acceptance into our final evaluation sets.
Summary / 总结
We introduce LifeVal, a benchmark evaluating AI model performance on personalized agentic consumer tasks.
RoboTryOn: Synthesizing Dexterous Robot Demonstrations from Human Videos via Virtual Try-On
Authors: Seungyong Lee, Suhyeok Jang, Minyoung Kim, Jinwoo Shin
First: 2026-11-03T16:35:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T15:43:46+00:00
Comments: 29 pages, 15 figures, 13 tables. Project page: https://ryan-seungyong-lee.github.io/RoboTryOn
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Robot foundation models have shown remarkable performance in robot control, yet adapting them to a new task still relies heavily on teleoperated demonstrations. Egocentric human videos offer a scalable substitute, but overlaying rendered robots on human videos or retargeting human poses into robot actions leaves significant visual or action gaps for high-DoF robot embodiments. To address this limitation, we propose RoboTryOn, which treats human-to-robot translation as virtual try-on: try-on keeps the person and swaps the garment, RoboTryOn keeps the demonstrated motion and swaps the human for the robot. Specifically, RoboTryOn converts a human video into a video of the target robot specified by a reference image, while preserving the original task, objects, and background. To maintain visual consistency, we translate selected keyframes autoregressively and use them to guide video synthesis with motion conditioned on the human hand skeleton. Additionally, we use an inverse dynamics model (IDM) that incorporates depth features to infer action labels from synthesized videos for policy learning. Our experiments across multiple humanoid embodiments show that policies trained with our synthetic data improve the average success rate on novel tasks, including challenging bimanual manipulation, from 2% to 60%, without any real robot demonstration of those tasks. Moreover, training with our synthetic data matches the performance of policies trained solely on teleoperated demonstrations at approximately a third of the collection cost, demonstrating the cost efficiency of our approach.
Summary / 总结
Robot foundation models have shown remarkable performance in robot control, yet adapting them to a new task still relies heavily on teleoperated demonstrations.
Human Motion Prediction via Continual Prior Compensation
Authors: Jianwei Tang, Jian-Fang Hu, Tianming Liang, Xiaotong Lin, Jiangxin Sun, Wei-Shi Zheng, Jianhuang Lai
Venue: IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence (2026)
First: 2026-11-04T15:19:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T15:19:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Human Motion Prediction (HMP) aims to predict future human poses at different moments according to observed past motion sequences. Previous approaches mainly treated the prediction of different temporal moments as a single prediction task and learned the predictions of varied moments simultaneously, which would encounter a main limitation: the learning of short-term predictions (referring to ``near-future'' prediction) could be hindered by the predictions of long-term (referring to ``far-future'' prediction) motions. In this paper, we develop a novel temporal continual learning framework called Continual Prior Compensation (CPC) to progressively train HMP models, in which we divide the prediction task of motions corresponding to varied temporal moments into several subtasks and train the model in a multi-stage manner. To mitigate the prior information forgetting in the progressive training, we further introduce a learnable random variable Prior Compensation Factor (PCF) to explicitly measure the prior knowledge loss. We theoretically show that the PCF can be efficiently learned together with the model parameters by minimizing a reasonable upper bound of the objective function. The proposed CPC is further enhanced to estimate the prior information loss for each subtask and a new framework called Continual Prior Compensation++ (CPC++) with Fine-Grained Prior Compensation Factor (FGPCF) is finally developed. Our CPC and CPC++ frameworks are quite flexible and can be easily integrated with different HMP backbone models and adapted to various datasets and applications. Extensive experiments on three HMP benchmark datasets using multiple SOTA HMP backbones (PGBIG, siMLPe, MotionMixer, and LTD) demonstrate the effectiveness and flexibility of our frameworks.
Summary / 总结
Human Motion Prediction (HMP) aims to predict future human poses at different moments according to observed past motion sequences.
TokenRepel: Generating Diverse Image Sets Without Compromising Quality
Authors: Qingtao Yu, Zhen Wang, Ning Lai, Boyang Hu, Hongdong Li, Dylan Campbell
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-29T12:20:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T14:36:07+00:00
Comments: NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We present an inference-time approach that enhances the visual diversity of image sets generated by text-to-image models, without compromising the average image quality. Our method leverages the intrinsic geometry of hidden representations in pretrained models to diversify images generated from the same prompt. First, we introduce a token repulsion mechanism that increases the angle between feature tokens across samples. It takes a negative Riemannian gradient step on a von Mises-Fisher mixture distribution of tokens on the unit hypersphere. Second, to preserve the quality of each output image, we propose a token density preservation mechanism that projects the repulsion-induced prediction change onto a half-space aligned with the model's score direction. This encourages the updates to remain within the high-density regions learned by the generative model. We further extend this mechanism to the output of each intermediate layer, enabling finer-grained iterative guidance. We evaluate our method on three types of pretrained text-to-image models. Experiments show substantial gains in visual diversity under identical prompts, while maintaining a comparable level of image fidelity.
Summary / 总结
We present an inference-time approach that enhances the visual diversity of image sets generated by text-to-image models, without compromising the average image quality.
