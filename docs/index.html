SceneBloom: Agentic Indoor Environment Generation with Realistic and Diverse Layouts for Embodied Learning
Authors: Yuhan Liu, Lianhui Qin, Shenji Wan
First: 2026-10-23T17:59:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T17:59:21+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Training generalizable embodied agents requires realistic, varied, and simulation-ready indoor environments. Language-guided methods support semantic planning and object-level reasoning, but precise spatial grounding remains difficult. Visual generative models offer rich priors over plausible arrangements, yet their outputs require geometric reasoning to become physically valid 3D scenes. We introduce SceneBloom, an agentic framework that combines visual layout sampling with contract-guided 3D compilation. A hierarchical sampler uses diffusion priors to generate furniture arrangements within functional zones. A compiler then uses relations extracted from each proposal to guide geometric fitting and local pose correction. Across five room types, SceneBloom attains the highest visual quality and physical stability among the compared methods and is the only method with no detected mesh collisions and out-of-bounds objects. Its layouts are also closer to the distribution of real rooms than those of all compared baselines. Object navigation policies trained in these realistic scenes achieve a higher success rate in held-out real scans than those trained on an equal number of real training scans. Code is available at https://github.com/Tinaliu0123/scenebloom.
Summary / 总结
Training generalizable embodied agents requires realistic, varied, and simulation-ready indoor environments.
ViT-H: Generative Video-To-Humanoid Control with Motion Tracking Feedback
Authors: Zimo He, Tianyi Bian, Zizhong Yang, Hongjie Li, Nan Jiang, Jieming Cui, Baoxiong Jia, Yizhou Wang, Siyuan Huang, Yixin Zhu
First: 2026-10-23T17:54:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T17:54:09+00:00
Comments: The first four authors contributed equally. Project website: https://awfuact.github.io/vit-h/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Learning humanoid control from monocular human videos offers a scalable way to acquire diverse whole-body behaviors. Yet, existing reconstruction-retargeting-tracking pipelines are limited in system-level scalability by separately optimized intermediate stages and cannot directly optimize reference motion executability using downstream tracking feedback. We present ViT-H, a video-to-humanoid control framework following a generation-and-tracking paradigm. ViT-H directly generates humanoid reference motions from videos with a diffusion model, bypassing human motion reconstruction and retargeting, and uses motion-tracking outcomes to post-train the generator with direct preference optimization. We further introduce an autoregressive variant that enables real-time humanoid video teleoperation with sub-400 ms latency. Extensive experiments on the Unitree G1 demonstrate that ViT-H outperforms state-of-the-art reconstruction-retargeting-tracking pipelines in both motion quality and executability. We further show that ViT-H scales favorably with model size and with both pre-training and post-training data scale, while motion-tracking feedback consistently improves executability across different motion trackers.
Summary / 总结
Learning humanoid control from monocular human videos offers a scalable way to acquire diverse whole-body behaviors.
When Clinical Judgment Becomes a Predictive Shortcut: Two Questions Clinical AI Must Answer Before Deployment
Authors: Jing Yang, Long R. Jiao, Zongjiu Zhang, Xiujun Cai

Comments: 27 pages, 1 figure, 3 tables, 1 box; includes Multimedia Appendix 1
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Clinical AI can predict outcomes using information generated by existing clinical judgment. Such performance alone cannot establish usefulness before recognition or across care processes. We propose evidence requirements matched to intended use and information conditions, with additional state-reference and proxy-relative timing tests for stronger recognition claims. This framework helps hospitals assess whether the conditions supporting performance hold when assistance is most needed. These questions remain outside current evaluation practice leakage checks, overall predictive performance, and external validation do not address them; the framework makes them explicit and specifies the direct evidence required to answer them.

SemantEq: Faithful Bidirectional Equivalence of Formal Statements
Authors: Xinrui Li, Pawan Sasanka Ammanamanchi, Siddharth Bhat, Rianna Jamdar, Yongxi Lin, Stella Biderman
First: 2026-10-23T17:20:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T17:20:12+00:00
Comments: 37 pages, 4 figures, 6 tables. Code and data: https://github.com/hlxr89/SemantEq
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Statement autoformalization translates mathematics from natural language into a proof assistant such as Lean 4, and models now do it at scale. As provers get stronger, the burden of correctness shifts from the proof to the statement, that is, to whether the formal statement faithfully translates its source. Given a trusted reference formalization, the natural test is logical equivalence, and it proves too much. $P \leftrightarrow Q$ holds trivially for any two statements $P$ and $Q$ that the library proves, because a prover can prove each separately and pass through $P \leftrightarrow \mathrm{True} \leftrightarrow Q$. The stronger the prover, the more unrelated pairs pass. Earlier methods restrict the prover and check that each proof names the other statement. We introduce SemantEq, a more principled relation that instead requires each statement to turn into the other only through a chain of allowed steps, such as logical rearrangement and rewriting with the library's own equations. Our programs judge a proof by the proof term Lean checks, not by its tactic script. SemantEq-Eval, a search without a model, reaches 100% precision and 65.6% recall on the ProofNetVerif test split under our adjudicated labels, against 95.8% and 61.8% for BEq+, and an 8.9-fold increase in its CPU time adds at most three pairs. A language model finds more proofs, but under two of four prompts it proves both directions of about two in five of the non-equivalent pairs. SemantEq-ProofAudit audits each model-written proof under a policy derived from the relation and accepts none of the 104 non-equivalent pairs, and it raises recall on 407 equivalent pairs from 65.4% to 81.1% under the best prompt. Separating the generation of equivalence proofs from their acceptance lets a stronger prover raise recall without changing what an acceptance means. We release code and data at: https://github.com/hlxr89/SemantEq
Summary / 总结
Statement autoformalization translates mathematics from natural language into a proof assistant such as Lean 4, and models now do it at scale.
Demo: Generative AI helps Radiotherapy Planning with User Preference
Authors: Riqiang Gao, Simon Arberet, Martin Kraus, Han Liu, Wilko F. A. R. Verbakel, Dorin Comaniciu, Florin-Cristian Ghesu, Ali Kamen
Venue: NeurIPS 2025 Best paper
First: 2025-12-08T16:49:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T15:42:29+00:00
Comments: Best paper in GenAI4Health at NeurIPS 2025
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Radiotherapy planning is a highly complex process that often varies significantly across institutions and individual planners. Most existing deep learning approaches for 3D dose prediction rely on reference plans as ground truth during training, which can inadvertently bias models toward specific planning styles or institutional preferences. In this study, we introduce a novel generative model that predicts 3D dose distributions based solely on user-defined preference flavors. These customizable preferences enable planners to prioritize specific trade-offs between organs-at-risk (OARs) and planning target volumes (PTVs), offering greater flexibility and personalization. Designed for seamless integration with clinical treatment planning systems, our approach assists users in generating high-quality plans efficiently. Comparative evaluations demonstrate that our method can surpasses the Varian RapidPlan model in both adaptability and plan quality in some scenarios.
Summary / 总结
Radiotherapy planning is a highly complex process that often varies significantly across institutions and individual planners.
Every Edge Needs a Quote: Building a Quote-Grounded Knowledge Graph of Healthcare Software Adoption with Language-Model Agents
Authors: Juan Felipe Duran, Arthur Tobler, Martin Vallières
First: 2026-10-23T15:28:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T15:28:39+00:00
Comments: 19 pages, 3 figures, 9 tables. Code: https://github.com/MEDomicsLab/healthcare-agentic-kg
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Hospitals regularly buy software that clinicians and patients depend on. Because these purchases are costly, hard to reverse and can affect patient care, buyers look for evidence of which peer institutions already use a product and which systems it connects to. This evidence is public but scattered across vendor websites, press releases and third-party pages. Language-model agents can now collect it at low cost, but the correctness of the extracted claims is difficult to establish, and a wrongly listed customer can mislead buyers and misrepresent an organisation. We present a pipeline that builds a knowledge graph of customer and integration relations from the open web under one rule: every edge must be supported by a verbatim quote from its source page. Pages come from a deterministic crawl of each vendor's site and a budgeted ReAct agent that searches the rest of the web. A permissive extractor proposes relations with quotes, and a layered grounding stage decides which to keep, using a referent step that reads the whole page when a quote names only one side, deterministic checks, and a panel of five model calls voting on nine criteria. On 61 vendors, the pipeline reaches 0.94 precision and 0.71 recall against a page-level key covering the 6,428 candidate relations proposed by its extractor, whose verdicts were drafted by one model and re-checked with a second. Against the golden reference, the marketplace's own curated list, it scores lower, but 89% of the relations it keeps that the reference lacks are stated on a page. A confidence score built from the panel's votes keeps more correct relations than the fixed rule at the same precision, and reaches 0.90 precision at 0.90 recall. We also discuss how narrow, bounded model calls, string-matched evidence and voting address long-context degradation and run-to-run variance.
Summary / 总结
Hospitals regularly buy software that clinicians and patients depend on.
PlayerEmbed: Learning Behavioral Fingerprint Representations from Raw Input Sequences
Authors: Tobias Schneider, Lorenz Sparrenberg, Priya Tomar, Shahzeb Qamar, Tobias Deußer, Rafet Sifa
First: 2026-10-23T14:48:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T14:48:02+00:00
Comments: Accepted at IEEE DSAA 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Raw player input sequences in video games contain fine-grained behavior, but are high-dimensional and noisy, which makes large-scale analytics challenging. To address this, we introduce PlayerEmbed, a framework that learns compact and discriminative player embeddings using deep metric learning. Our approach uses a hybrid CNN-LSTM encoder trained on raw input sequences from 1,000 players. We evaluate the model on held-out sequences from these players and on sequences from 1,000 players entirely unseen during training to assess generalization to unseen identities. For player identification, a nearest-centroid classifier built from reference embeddings achieves strong performance, reaching balanced accuracies of 0.950 for players seen during training and 0.879 for players unseen during training. Systematically increasing the number of training players substantially improves generalization to unseen players, indicating a transition from simple fingerprinting to learning transferable behavioral patterns.
Summary / 总结
Raw player input sequences in video games contain fine-grained behavior, but are high-dimensional and noisy, which makes large-scale analytics challenging.
Probing the limits of forward screening and inverse design of crystal materials
Authors: Zhilong Song, Zijun Pang, Lixue Cheng
First: 2026-10-23T14:15:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T14:15:47+00:00
Comments: 23 pages, 5 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Crystal materials are designed either by screening known crystals for a target property or by generating new crystals with that property. Both routes are judged against public databases of known crystals, but these databases name the same crystal differently. Several materials announced as new by recent generative and autonomous discovery campaigns have since proved to be known phases or close variants of them. The limits of both routes remain unmeasured. Here we probe them by matching the crystals of 55 databases by structure into one self-extending database of 14.6 million crystals and 131.8 million property values, each traced to its source. Only 4.5% of its crystal-property pairs carry a value. To fill the empty pairs, we developed CGTNet, an equivariant graph transformer with state-of-the-art accuracy on several tasks of two public benchmarks. Trained on each task, it forms a library of property models whose predictions we release. The reach of forward screening has been underestimated. Known crystals hold tens of thousands of ultra-incompressible candidates without a recorded bulk modulus, and a post hoc audit of synthesis evidence finds among them ReN2, whose bulk modulus was measured at 428 GPa. The novelty claimed for inverse design has been overestimated. At an equal verification budget, a stiffness-conditioned generator returned more stiff hits than screening (100 versus 55), but all contained osmium and 82 repeated recorded compositions. Integrating and completing the databases of known crystals therefore moves the limits of both routes: it widens what screening can reach and sets the reference against which generated crystals are judged new.
Summary / 总结
Crystal materials are designed either by screening known crystals for a target property or by generating new crystals with that property.
Computational imaging through turbid, bubbly and flowing water near the telescope
Authors: Alexander Ingold, Sabina D. Menon, Aadhi Umamageswaran, Alec Ikei, May H. Hlaing, Syed N. Qadri, Rajesh Menon
First: 2026-10-23T13:54:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T13:54:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Cameras in the surf zone or behind ship wakes look through turbid, bubbly, moving water close to the lens, yet restoration networks are usually scored only on displayed images. We introduce a measured dataset of a telescope viewing displayed images and physical objects through a 25~mm water layer 45~mm in front of its objective, under seven combinations of flow, bubbles and turbidity, with clear-water recordings as a second reference. Because the suspension scatters light far outside the 1.3\textdegree{} field of view, turbidity mainly removes contrast, 4.4- to 4.8-fold on a Siemens star. Restoring the mean of eight frames improves structural similarity (SSIM) by 0.032 over a single frame, and learned fusion of the frames adds little. One raw frame supports five-category recognition (96.8\%) as well as restored images. Adding synthetic training pairs leaves display-image scores unchanged but raises the correlation of a restored Siemens star with its clear-water recording from below 0.5 to above 0.9. These networks also improve ordinary underwater photographs without retraining, where training on measured pairs alone fails. Scoring against the digital source or a clear-water recording reverses the ranking of raw and restored images. These results support a simple pipeline: average the frames, classify raw frames directly, and restore with networks trained on measured and synthetic pairs.
Summary / 总结
Cameras in the surf zone or behind ship wakes look through turbid, bubbly, moving water close to the lens, yet restoration networks are usually scored only on displayed images.
Attractive Faces Skew Text-to-Image Reward Models and Leaderboards
Authors: Harvey Mannering, Zhiwu Huang, Adam Prügel-Bennett
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-10-20T13:35:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T13:47:00+00:00
Comments: WACV 2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Human preference is now central to the evaluation, guidance, finetuning, and ranking of text-to-image models. However, the susceptibility of human preferences to bias in image generation remains underexplored. We identify a key observation: for human faces, perceived attractiveness overrides image quality in preference judgments, even when comparing real photographs of comparable fidelity. In a controlled user study, annotators rank more attractive faces as higher quality, even when unattractive real faces are compared with attractive AI-generated ones. We examine the downstream consequences of this bias. Firstly, simulating Elo rankings from user study data shows attractiveness can distort a leaderboard's ranking, elevating synthetic attractive faces over real, unattractive ones. Secondly, the same bias reappears in the human preference models. Editing 40 annotated facial attributes in isolation, we find that attractiveness is the strongest correlate of score for four of five human preference models (PickScore, MPS, HPSv2.1, HPSv3), and the strongest predictor of score in regression analysis. We further show that preference aligned generation shifts faces toward younger, more feminine faces, and can override explicit prompts for unattractive faces, thereby limiting user control. Finally, we explore mitigation strategies, both for data collection and post training on human preference models. These findings highlight the need for preference models that disentangle image quality from human cognitive bias, enabling more reliable evaluation.
Summary / 总结
Human preference is now central to the evaluation, guidance, finetuning, and ranking of text-to-image models.
