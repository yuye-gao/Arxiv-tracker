One to More, More to One: Category-Aware Iterative Expert Training for Software Engineering Agents
Authors: Jie Zhao, Ziyu Jiang, Suhang Zheng, Minghui Shan, Xiaoxiao Xu, Lin Qu
First: 2026-09-20T05:58:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:55:58+00:00
Comments: 47 pages, 12 figures. v2: added evaluations on SWE-bench Pro (731 tasks), SWE-bench Pro V2/Hard-51, and LiveCodeBench v6 (175 tasks); updated SWE-bench Multilingual results; added 35B-A3B student experiments. Code: https://github.com/alibaba/AgenticBigBang
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Repository-level software engineering (SWE) comprises heterogeneous task categories, whose progress under pooled agentic reinforcement learning can be uneven: gains in some categories coincide with regressions in others, while aggregate resolution obscures these changes. Motivated by this category see-saw, we develop a category-aware expert-training and policy-integration framework. Executable task construction and SWE Labeler, an evidence-grounded multi-axis labeling system, organize the training pools. Initial category-specific RL improves average training success while leaving uneven instance-level progress, motivating explicit consolidation of successful behavior and policy-adaptive task selection. Same-origin category experts alternate long-horizon Agentic-miniRL with Refresh-Repair-Expand (RRE): the updated policy refreshes instance mastery, reuses its own verified successful trajectories for Repair SFT, and reselects tasks for further RL. Label-routed multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) consolidates the experts into one deployable student, with ReLU-gated reward extrapolation keeping only each teacher's improving direction over the reference. Expert training and policy integration require no external model to provide solution trajectories or action targets. On a fixed, audit-filtered Pro-618 subset, we connect Pooled RL and Balanced RL, expert development, and single-model integration through overall and per-category resolution and expert-gain recovery. In final-model evaluations, MOPD achieves mean resolution of 86.19% on SWE-bench Pro V2 and 71.78% on SWE-bench Multilingual, exceeding the base model by 4.57 and 3.89 percentage points, respectively. The same three 27B experts also improve a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B student across the evaluated task sets.
Summary / 总结
Repository-level software engineering (SWE) comprises heterogeneous task categories, whose progress under pooled agentic reinforcement learning can be uneven: gains in some categories coincide with regressions in others, while aggregate resolution obscures these changes.
Difficulty Calibration Across Time for Membership Inference in Diffusion Models
Authors: Michael Joseph, Yedid Hoshen
First: 2026-10-29T17:54:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:54:54+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Modern diffusion models are trained on massive datasets that often include copyrighted or private images. Membership inference attacks (MIAs) address this by testing whether a given image was in a model's training set. The standard approach scores an image by its reconstruction loss and flags low-loss images as members. This often fails because some images are simply easier to reconstruct than others, so a simple unseen image can score a lower loss than a complex training image. Difficulty calibration separates memorization from how difficult an image is to begin with. We show how to calibrate along two axes of time: training time and diffusion time. First, across training time, we introduce Reference-Model Calibration (RMC), which compares a fine-tuned model's loss against an earlier checkpoint. This cleanly isolates newly memorized data and gives near-perfect auditing, even without text captions. Since earlier checkpoints are not always available, we next present Temporal Self-Calibration (TSC), which calibrates across diffusion time using only a single model. Our key observation is that an image's difficulty persists across noise levels, whereas memorization is localized in time. By comparing the loss at a target timestep against neighboring timesteps from the same model, TSC attenuates image difficulty using the model's own trajectory. Empirically, our methods beat the state of the art by a wide margin, and unlike most existing attacks, generalize across different model parameterizations such as $x$-, $v$-, and $ε$-prediction models. Code is available at https://github.com/MichaelJoseph777/TSC.
Summary / 总结
Modern diffusion models are trained on massive datasets that often include copyrighted or private images.
Fight World Model: Customizable Combat through Visual Interaction Proxies
Authors: Jiahua Li, Zixu John, Tom Zhong, Fuping Wu, Tianhao Xu, Ruicheng Zhang, Jun Zhou, Zunnan Xu
First: 2026-10-29T17:53:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:53:02+00:00
Comments: Tech Report
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
A playable, programmable game world must maintain persistent state, enforce executable rules, and support extensible gameplay mechanics. Existing video world models can generate plausible combat, yet struggle to keep hit reactions and health updates consistent with game rules. We introduce Fight World Model (FWM), which separates rule-governed combat execution from video rendering. An executable engine maintains state and resolves interactions. A deterministic compiler encodes these outcomes and attempted actions in a spatially and temporally aligned interaction proxy. The proxy aligns action and skill windows, brief hit reactions, and persistent health by fighter identity and timing. These cues can be controlled separately to render interactions under modified rules. Identity-bound references preserve character appearance across side switches. We train FWM on FightWorld Corpus, spanning 52 skill effects, 187 character-appearance variants, and 15 arenas. Without retraining the renderer, FWM supports damage scaling, limited-hit immunity, lifesteal, and multi-stage skill mechanisms beyond the native game. It also allows character and arena customization. Experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of FWM, which outperforms existing game world models in action fidelity, hit reactions, health-state consistency, and character appearance preservation. Our project is available at https://fight-world-model.github.io.
Summary / 总结
A playable, programmable game world must maintain persistent state, enforce executable rules, and support extensible gameplay mechanics.
VeriCUA: Verification as Agentic Exploration for Computer-Using Agents
Authors: Qiushi Sun, Da Yu, Alexander Knop, Amer Sinha, Lingpeng Kong, Ben Kao, Ravi Kumar, Pritish Kamath
First: 2026-10-29T17:45:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:45:26+00:00
Comments: Work in progress
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Building and scaling computer-using agents (CUAs) across desktop, mobile, and web environments requires reliable task-completion verification for data curation, evaluation, and reinforcement learning. Typical automated approaches prompt vision-language models (VLMs) to judge trajectories or generate programs to inspect application state. However, both rely on partial views of complex digital environments. VLM judges are restricted to supplied observations, while generated programs depend on accessible state. These views can omit decisive evidence or leave completion judgments unsupported. Tailoring separate verifiers to different platforms and access settings adds further engineering cost. We present VeriCUA, a platform-agnostic framework for verification through agentic exploration. It treats verification as dynamic evidence gathering and assessment within a shared procedure for online and offline settings across platforms and observation modalities. After inspecting the available context to construct a task rubric, the verifier selects GUI or code checks for unresolved requirements and combines their findings under explicit evidence rules. Experiments show improved agreement with reference labels over the compared baselines across these settings, with the largest offline gains on hard cases and stronger rejection of false completion claims online. Further analyses connect these gains to complementary evidence and test-time exploration.
Summary / 总结
Building and scaling computer-using agents (CUAs) across desktop, mobile, and web environments requires reliable task-completion verification for data curation, evaluation, and reinforcement learning.
Homebrewed SLMs: Autoregressive and Masked Diffusion Language Models Built at Home for Portuguese
Authors: Pedro Carvalho Brom
First: 2026-10-29T17:34:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:34:06+00:00
Comments: 34 pages, 14 figures, 13 tables. Code and notebooks: https://github.com/pcbrom/homebrewed-slms
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Building a language model of one's own no longer requires a data centre. This tutorial trains one from scratch, tokenizer included, on one consumer GPU, and shows how to tell whether it is good for the tasks of a specialist domain. It proposes no new method. It releases a tested Python package and two notebooks in Brazilian Portuguese with autoregressive and masked diffusion generation on one network, a preregistered comparison protocol, legal tasks built from text alone with a Kneser-Ney floor and public yardsticks, reference numbers for models of 11 to 12M parameters, and a catalogue of defects that only measurement revealed. On Brazilian prose and federal laws, at minutes per run, the autoregressive mode peaks at 64 epochs over the same text and then memorises it, while masked diffusion improves up to 256. At their best points on test, the diffusion bound exceeds the autoregressive likelihood by 0.06 bits per byte, within the preregistered minimum effect. On held-out laws and on acts published after training, the model ties a 6-gram floor in likelihood on the same windows, and it loses to the floor at continuing those acts. It beats the floor at detecting tampered text, and fills cross-references more often than the floor and public models of 160M and 630M parameters, only in decrees, a genre absent from training, where those scorers stay near or below chance, and in filling the blank only when each group weighs the same, since per item it ties the 630M model. On laws, the public models, trained with about 5x10^2 to 10^5 times its compute, lead in tampered text and continuation. The blind human reading is preregistered and not part of this version.

Building a language model of one's own no longer requires a data centre.
NEOPIC: A Neural Operator Framework for Particle-based Kinetic Plasma Simulations
Authors: Sriramkrishnan Muralikrishnan, Chelsea Maria John, Jeffrey Kelling, Michael Bussmann, Robert Speck, Paul Gibbon
First: 2026-10-29T17:33:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:33:20+00:00
Comments: 84 pages, 19 figures, 3 tables. Code: https://github.com/srikrrish/operator_learning (tags neopic-arxiv-v1, neopic-arxiv-v1-nufft); analysis scripts: https://github.com/NEOPIC-framework/neopic-paper-scripts
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
We introduce NEOPIC, a framework for learning the field operator in particle-based methods applied to kinetic plasma simulations. Its key novelty is a neural operator that acts directly on the empirical measure of the particles, rather than on a grid, and is evaluated at the particle positions. No mesh or tree is needed and, unlike grid-based neural operators, no deposition onto a mesh takes place, so the particle noise that dominates the inputs of particle simulations is not aliased. Since it replaces only the field solve, it serves as a plug-and-play field solver for particle-based methods: it can be trained on data from particle-in-cell, particle-in-Fourier or tree codes, and combined with any time integrator, including implicit schemes. We prove that the discrete evaluation coincides with the continuous operator at any number of particles (representation equivalence) and derive an error analysis that separates sampling, learning and time-stepping errors: up to the learning error, NEOPIC has the error structure of standard Monte Carlo particle methods. We show that the particle noise in the training data acts as an implicit regularizer, which gives guidance on the number of training particles in relation to the particle numbers used at inference. A complexity analysis shows that NEOPIC is an $O(N_p)$ method whose constants are set by the architecture alone, independently of any mesh or tree. Numerical experiments on electrostatic benchmarks in two and three dimensions, including Landau damping, two-stream and bump-on-tail instabilities, a cyclotron mini-app and a Penning trap, confirm the theoretical findings. A single model trained on several mini-apps generated by different reference methods captures all of them and remains stable up to twice the training time window, which points towards foundation models for fields in particle-based methods.
Summary / 总结
We introduce NEOPIC, a framework for learning the field operator in particle-based methods applied to kinetic plasma simulations.
Accurate MRI-free PET Quantification and Reliability Assessment via CT-to-MRI Synthesis and Conformal Prediction
Authors: Jungwoo Seo, Seung Yun Choi, Kun Ho Lee, Seun Jeon, Byoung Seok Ye, Jiook Cha
First: 2026-10-29T17:11:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:11:19+00:00
Comments: 28 pages, 8 figures, including supplementary material. Submitted to Medical Image Analysis
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Standardized uptake value ratio (SUVR) quantification of amyloid PET commonly relies on a subject-specific T1-weighted MRI, yet a usable MRI is not always available. We therefore combined CT-to-MRI synthesis with conformal prediction for MRI-free PET quantification with reliability assessment. A slice-consistent Brownian Bridge Diffusion Model was strengthened with multi-dataset training and data-source conditioning to enrich the anatomical prior learned from MRI, and with expanded multi-slice CT context to exploit the through-plane continuity of subject-specific anatomy in CT. Synthetic MRI was used for FreeSurfer-based anatomical parcellation and PET quantification, while deep-ensemble uncertainty was calibrated with conformal prediction to estimate a plausible range for the corresponding real MRI-based SUVR. On $^{11}$C-PiB PET from OASIS-3, the proposed method achieved the highest synthesis and parcellation fidelity among the compared methods. Mean cortical SUVR from the proposed synthetic MRI agreed closely with the real MRI-based reference (MAE $0.016 \pm 0.015$, Pearson $r = 0.9987$) and yielded the same amyloid-positivity classification as the real MRI in all 87 test subjects at the prespecified cut-off. Conformal prediction achieved empirical coverage close to the prespecified levels, and at 90% target coverage, 85 of 87 subjects had prediction intervals that supported an unambiguous amyloid-positivity decision. Zero-shot evaluation on an independent external cohort showed that the proposed CT-to-MRI model could support PET quantification without retraining, although conformal calibration required target-cohort recalibration to recover the intended coverage. These findings support CT-to-MRI-based MRI-free PET quantification as a promising approach for providing accurate SUVR measurements and an explicit assessment of their reliability.
Summary / 总结
Standardized uptake value ratio (SUVR) quantification of amyloid PET commonly relies on a subject-specific T1-weighted MRI, yet a usable MRI is not always available.
PhysInsert: Physically Plausible Object Insertion with Geometric Priors and Bidirectional Object-Scene Interactions
Authors: Yue Gong, Le Zhang, Rui Chen, Song Chang, Xiaochao Qu, Luoqi Liu, Ting Liu, Yebin Liu
First: 2026-10-29T17:01:13+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:01:13+00:00
Comments: 51 pages, including supplementary material. Project page: https://arthuring.github.io/PhysInsert/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
We present PhysInsert, a framework for physically plausible reference-based object insertion that jointly adapts objects and their surrounding scenes. Existing methods often rely on masked inputs, generation-based pairs, or training pairs with limited viewpoint variation, leaving pose and illumination adaptation and bidirectional object-scene interactions insufficiently constrained. We construct the Insert World dataset with strictly aligned Blender-rendered counterfactual pairs and object references across viewpoints and lighting conditions. Building on these data, Geometry-Guided Adaptation couples a VGGT geometry stream with image and text streams under target-scene feature supervision to guide spatially consistent insertion. Complementary 2D Object-Scene Spatial Supervision jointly supervises object occupancy and environmental changes while grounding spatial instructions in object regions through image-text attention. We introduce PhysInsert-Bench with dedicated cross-environment and cross-view cases requiring illumination and pose adaptation beyond appearance copying. Experiments on PhysInsert-Bench and DreamBooth demonstrate improved image fidelity; a user study further shows more accurate placement and improved lighting integration, perspective, and pose plausibility.
Summary / 总结
We present PhysInsert, a framework for physically plausible reference-based object insertion that jointly adapts objects and their surrounding scenes.
Scaling Mathematical Proof Supervision from Structured Textbooks
Authors: Youqing Fang, Zhouqi Hua, Yinhao Tang, Yuzhe Gu, Lingkai Kong, Bin Liu, Weiming Zhang, Kai Chen, Yanan Sun, Wenwei Zhang
First: 2026-10-29T16:32:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T16:32:59+00:00
Comments: 35 pages, 18 figures, 14 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Mathematical supervision for language models is typically flattened into isolated problem-solution pairs, discarding the curricular organization and evidential structure of the original sources. We introduce TOME-Math (Textbook-Oriented Mathematical Evidence), a large-scale corpus that converts 2,118 textbooks spanning the core areas of university-level mathematics subjects into approximately 380K structured proof-supervision records while preserving chapter order, proposition-proof links, dependency references, and source provenance. Leveraging these structural signals, we explore curriculum learning across multiple mathematical subjects and characterize scaling patterns with supervision volume and source breadth. At full scale, fine-tuning on TOME-Math raises the unweighted mean score across our held-out evaluations and established public benchmarks by 16.7 percentage points over the base model, while exceeding all three public instruction-data baselines evaluated in aggregate. Our findings highlight structured textbooks as both a scalable source of mathematical supervision and an empirical framework for studying the impact of data organization on mathematical reasoning. We will release the corpus to facilitate further research on structured mathematical supervision.

Wizard: Manipulate Any World in Real Time via Agentic Control
Authors: Yuzhe Wu, Zijun Lin, Yeying Jin
First: 2026-10-29T16:03:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T16:03:19+00:00
Comments: Project Page: https://wyzardwu.github.io/wizard
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Recent advances in video world models have enabled the generation of increasingly realistic and interactive virtual environments, opening new possibilities for player-customizable gameplay. Agentic frameworks further advance this direction by decoupling explicit game logic and controllable world representations from visual generation. However, existing approaches support only simple game settings and predefined motion patterns, generalize poorly to unseen environments and characters, and offer limited real-time interactivity. In this paper, we introduce Wizard, an agentic game world model that enables real-time, precise control over character motions while generalizing zero-shot across novel scenes, characters, and actions. To achieve this, we construct a diverse dataset spanning 750 scenes and 178 characters, incorporating skeleton and geometry information as structured representations. We leverage these representations as intermediate proxies to condition a video generation model, together with an adaptive masking mechanism that improves alignment between the conditioning signals and generated video rollouts. Furthermore, we introduce an agentic framework that explicitly maintains and updates game states, allowing players to freely configure their virtual worlds and exercise fine-grained control over character motions while preserving visual quality and gameplay consistency. By combining agentic control with structured proxy-based generation, our framework unlocks new possibilities for player-driven game creation with real-time, generalizable, and mechanically consistent world simulation.
Summary / 总结
Recent advances in video world models have enabled the generation of increasingly realistic and interactive virtual environments, opening new possibilities for player-customizable gameplay.
