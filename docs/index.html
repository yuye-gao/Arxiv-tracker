ENFR: Evidence Networks with Flow-Matched References for Amortized Simulation-Based Model Inference
Authors: Kai Lehman, Jameson Dong, Carolina Cuesta-Lazaro, Niall Jeffrey, Benjamin D. Wandelt, Francisco Villaescusa-Navarro, Sven Krippendorf, Jochen Weller
First: 2026-10-16T17:48:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:48:26+00:00
Comments: 28 pages, 6 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The evidence, $\mathcal{Z} = p(x | \mathcal{M})$, of a data-generating model $\mathcal{M}$ is a powerful tool in Bayesian frameworks: it enables model inference, model misspecification tests, and goodness-of-fit assessment as an alternative to standard discrepancy statistics, e.g. $χ^2$. While versatile, the evidence is notoriously difficult to compute, even when the likelihood is available. In both likelihood-based and simulation-based inference, estimating $\mathcal{Z}$ provides an immediate solution to high-dimensional goodness-of-fit or model misspecification tests: using $\mathcal{Z}(x)$ as the test statistic induces a minimum-volume level-$α$ test. Such tests are essential to assess model fidelity, which is necessary to trust simulation-based parameter inference. Existing learned approaches largely target Bayes factors, which only measure evidence ratios. Instead, we provide accurate, fast, and amortized estimates of the absolute evidence. We present ENFR: Evidence Networks with Flow-matched References. Building on the Evidence Network framework of Jeffrey & Wandelt (2024), the proposed method first learns Bayes factors with respect to a flow-matched reference, from which the absolute evidence is recovered. We show improvement in estimation of $\mathcal{Z}$ over the flow-based baseline in all applications considered and the best performance of all density estimation methods considered in our benchmarks. We furthermore introduce and provide the proof for an architecture that jointly learns the individual evidences of a set of models $\{\mathcal{M}_i\}$. We demonstrate the performance and scaling behavior of these approaches on data from a variety of scientific applications.

FuTransformer: FP8 prefill and INT4 decode for Llama-style inference on Hopper
Authors: Wei Wang
First: 2026-10-16T17:22:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:22:01+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Transformer prefill reuses weights across tokens and is dominated by matrix multiplication and activation data movement; single-token decode has limited reuse and is dominated by weight and KV-cache reads. We present FUTRANSFORMER, a CUDA implementation of a Llama-style Transformer block for NVIDIA Hopper GPUs. It applies CODA's GEMM-plus-epilogue approach to inference, adding direct KV-cache placement, folding the fixed RMSNorm gain into weights, writing FP8 output directly with delayed scaling, and supporting prefill without padding. Decode uses INT4 projection weights and an INT4 KV-cache copy. Weights and keys follow Tensor Core fragment layouts for direct dequantization into MMA operands without full intermediate tiles. A block-diagonal Hadamard transform reduces cache quantization error from outlier channels. On one H100, prefill is up to 1.48x faster than compiled PyTorch FP8 and reaches 1.29 PFLOP/s. For gate/up projection with SwiGLU at the 8B shape, our BF16 path is 1.13x faster than CODA. Our FP8 path is 2.02-2.18x faster than the evaluated BF16 CODA path. Across 8B and 70B shapes at batches 1-32, INT4 decode is 1.3-2.4x faster than the faster of PyTorch's BF16 and INT4 blocks at each point: 2.1-3.3x faster than compiled, CUDA-graphed BF16 and 1.3-6.3x faster than the same block using PyTorch's INT4 projection kernel, which targets a single query row and loses to BF16 at batch 32. It is also 31-51% faster than our FP8 path. Complete Llama-3.1-8B decode takes 2.8 ms per step at batch one, versus 8.1 ms for the compiled transformers implementation. Decoding every WikiText-2 token through the INT4 weight path raises perplexity from 6.24 to 7.23 with round-to-nearest weights or 6.99 with GPTQ in the same format; the rotated INT4 cache adds 0.20. The kernels match a quantized reference computation within 0.001 perplexity.

Transformer prefill reuses weights across tokens and is dominated by matrix multiplication and activation data movement; single-token decode has limited reuse and is dominated by weight and KV-cache reads.
DrugHarness: An Evolvable Agent Harness for Computational Drug Discovery
Authors: Yuxing Chen, Zhisheng Zhang, Cheng Tang, Wei Li, Minghui Zhang, Ziyan Huang, Shaocong Wei, Jiawei Chen, Junru Hu, Hongze Zhu, Jianghan Shen, Min Cen, Xinyuan Zhu, Yaxue Zhao, Jubo Wang, Yuqiang Li, Chun-Mei Feng, Zongyuan Ge, Rui Su, Jie Xu, Yirong Chen, Xi Xu, Ming Hu
First: 2026-10-16T17:20:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:20:19+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Computational drug discovery requires agents to compose scientific operations and adapt to changing evidence and constraints. Here we introduce DrugHarness, an evolvable agent harness coupling composable scientific execution with conditional skill induction. Its environment integrates over 80 scientific operations, biomedical resources and task-specific code, preserving associations among candidates, results and artifacts. From execution trajectories and outcome feedback, DrugHarness induces action hypotheses with observable applicability conditions, recommended actions and supporting evidence. An independent review agent evaluates candidate skills derived from these hypotheses through source verification and, when needed, targeted execution trials. Admitted skills guide subsequent tasks and are revised through feedback. Evaluation on MolBench and SMDD-Bench demonstrates capabilities in molecular screening, optimization and constrained design. Without evolution, DrugHarness exceeds reported MolClaw-CC results by 10.8 percentage points in affinity-comparison accuracy and 55% in mean top-three active hits on MolBench. Retrospective evaluation on skill-development tasks shows that evolution increases Qwen's affinity-comparison accuracy from 62.2% to 81.1% and optimization success from 79.5% to 87.2%. In a prospective HPK1 study, DrugHarness achieves a more favorable median docking score than Biomni among system-specific candidates. A synthesized compound achieves a cellular IC50 of 14.19 nM, a 2.64-fold potency improvement over the best input reference, with activity competitive with two selected compounds designed by experienced medicinal chemists. These results support conditional skill evolution for scientific agents and demonstrate a workflow connecting computational drug design with experimental validation.
Summary / 总结
Computational drug discovery requires agents to compose scientific operations and adapt to changing evidence and constraints.
MTEdit: Packed Trajectories Beat Rollouts for Multi-Turn Image Editing
Authors: Houyuan Chen, Fanzhou Wang, Xiangyu Fan, Songlin Yang, Zhitao Yang, Lvmin Zhang, Pei Yang, Hao Zhao, Lei Yang, Anyi Rao
First: 2026-10-16T16:56:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T16:56:48+00:00
Comments: 41 pages, 22 figures, 8 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multi-turn image editing aims to modify an image according to a sequence of instructions, making it better aligned with human-AI collaborative creation than single-turn editing. However, constructing high-quality multi-turn training data remains challenging, as conventional sequential rollout accumulates errors and introduces visual-quality drift across editing turns. To address this challenge, we introduce MTEdit, a multi-turn image editing dataset built through a pipeline centered on packed trajectory canvas (PTC) generation. By jointly generating multiple editing states within a unified visual context, this pipeline reduces visual-quality drift across turns. Covering both step-wise and context-aware editing scenarios, MTEdit comprises 500K multi-turn image editing trajectories, with up to 8 turns and an average of 5.38 turns per trajectory, making it one of the most extensive datasets for multi-turn image editing. We further introduce MTEdit-Bench, a benchmark designed to evaluate both scenarios by assessing models' ability to follow editing instructions, maintain content consistency, resist visual-quality drift, and effectively use historical context across turns. Using MTEdit, we fine-tune Qwen-Image-Edit-Causal to obtain Qwen-Image-MTEdit. On MTEdit-Bench, we evaluate a broad range of closed-source and open-source image editing models, including Qwen-Image-MTEdit. Experimental results show that Qwen-Image-MTEdit achieves the best performance among the evaluated open-source models, demonstrating the effectiveness of MTEdit in improving models' multi-turn image editing capabilities.

Multi-turn image editing aims to modify an image according to a sequence of instructions, making it better aligned with human-AI collaborative creation than single-turn editing.
HardEval: An Execution-Verified Benchmark for AI-Driven Security Hardening and Compliance Remediation
Authors: Manohar Vellala, Soumya Banerjee, Sandip Roy, Ross Gore, Sachin Shetty
First: 2026-10-16T16:14:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T16:14:58+00:00
Comments: 8 pages, 4 figures, 4 tables. Code and data: https://github.com/manoharvellala/Hardeval The paper has 4 figure environments (two are images, two are the example-prompt panels) and 4 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Implementing Security Technical Implementation Guide (STIG) controls remains a demanding operating-system administration task. Each natural-language security requirement must be translated into a precise and persistent configuration change. These changes span Secure Shell (SSH) configuration, authentication policy, audit rules, and kernel parameters. Existing benchmarks do not measure whether a model can perform this task on a live system. Code benchmarks evaluate isolated programs without persistent system state, while agent benchmarks largely focus on applications rather than operating-system configuration. In this paper, we introduce \textbf{HardEval}, an execution-verified benchmark for evaluating language-model-assisted security hardening. HardEval is built from Red Hat Enterprise Linux 8 STIG controls that fail on an unhardened baseline system. Expert-written reference remediations and Open Vulnerability and Assessment Language (OVAL) checks serve as hidden ground truth for grading; they are never shown to the model, and each task states only the required security outcome while withholding the implementation. The model produces a Bash script, which is executed on a live virtual machine and graded from fail to pass using the corresponding OpenSCAP/OVAL compliance check. Evaluation of fourteen frontier and open-weight configurations reveals large and uneven capability differences across security domains. A recurring failure mode is plausible security code that does not satisfy the exact compliance condition. For four open-weight configurations, multi-run Wilson confidence intervals preserve the observed ordering. HardEval is publicly available.
Summary / 总结
Implementing Security Technical Implementation Guide (STIG) controls remains a demanding operating-system administration task.
Would you still call this Dax? Novel Visual References in VLMs and Humans
Authors: Ada Defne Tür, Gaurav Kamath, Joyce Chai, Siva Reddy, Benno Krojer
Venue: EMNLP
First: 2026-06-03T20:23:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T15:49:10+00:00
Comments: EMNLP Main Conference 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs), like human learners, are frequently exposed to new visual concepts, but how they map novel visual references to language after exposure remains largely underexplored, particularly when those references contradict prior knowledge from pre-training. To study this, we present the Novel Visual References Dataset (NVRD): 19,176 images spanning 90 visual concepts across different levels of visual novelty, each with up to 20 increasingly perturbed versions of the original object to probe generalization. Unlike prior work on visual augmentations of familiar concepts, NVRD comprises entirely novel, open-ended stimuli constructed from scratch, mirroring how humans encounter genuinely new concepts. We evaluate 3 open- and 2 closed-source models alongside 2,400 human judgments for direct human-model comparison, and find that (i) models struggle to acquire novel concepts in-context when they contradict prior knowledge, and (ii) while models and humans show correlated sensitivity to visual perturbations, models significantly overgeneralize, extending learned labels to stimuli that humans reject. We contribute NVRD as a corpus and benchmark for research on visual concept learning in both humans and machines.

Vision-language models (VLMs), like human learners, are frequently exposed to new visual concepts, but how they map novel visual references to language after exposure remains largely underexplored, particularly when those references contradict prior knowledge from pre-training.
Worst-Case Optimal $\sqrtε$-Rate Stability of KL Divergence under Perturbations of Gaussian Distributions
Authors: Jialu Pan, Yufeng Zhang, Nan Hu, Zhenbang Chen, Ji Wang, Keqin Li
First: 2026-04-13T05:49:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T15:32:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We establish a stability bound for the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence when its Gaussian reference distribution is perturbed. Existing relaxed triangle inequalities of KL divergence between Gaussian distributions require all three distributions to be Gaussian, which limits their use in applications such as out-of-distribution detection with flow-based generative models. Let $\mathcal{N}_1$ and $\mathcal{N}_2$ be $d$-dimensional Gaussian distributions, and let $P$ be any $d$-dimensional distribution satisfying $\mathbb{E}_P[\|X\|^2]<\infty$ and $\mathrm{KL}(P\Vert \mathcal{N}_1)<\infty$. If $\mathrm{KL}(\mathcal{N}_1\Vert\mathcal{N}_2)\leqε$ for $0<ε<1/12$, we give an upper bound of $\left|\mathrm{KL}(P\Vert\mathcal{N}_1)-\mathrm{KL}(P\Vert\mathcal{N}_2)\right|$. Under suitable uniform boundedness conditions, the bound is $ \frac{\sqrt{6dε}}{2}+K\sqrtε+o(\sqrtε)$ as $ε\rightarrow 0$ for fixed $d$, where $K$ depends on the parameters of $\mathcal{N}_1$ and $\mathcal{N}_2$, and on $\mathbb{E}_P[\|X\|]$ and $\mathbb{E}_P[\|X\|^2]$. Furthermore, we prove that the $\sqrtε$ dependence is worst-case optimal. The result extends stability guarantees restricted to Gaussian triples to comparisons involving an arbitrary distribution and two nearby Gaussian references. Experiments on synthetic distributions and latent representations from flow-based models support the predicted stability behavior.
Summary / 总结
We establish a stability bound for the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence when its Gaussian reference distribution is perturbed.

When Magnitude Suffices: Reconstructing Time-Domain-Gated Microwave Signals Without Phase on a Thoracic Phantom
Authors: Maciej Cichoń, Patrycja Gołaś, Maksymilian Bartosik, Angelika Walczak, Agata Bielecka-Dąbrowa, Maciej Ślot
First: 2026-10-16T15:22:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T15:22:35+00:00
Comments: 18 pages, 7 figures, 4 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Time-domain gating separates the arrivals that form a microwave S-parameter measurement. It requires the measurement of complex values, so this application almost always uses a vector network analyzer (VNA). We show that, on one calibrated measurement configuration, the gated magnitude the VNA would give can be reconstructed from a magnitude-only sweep of a spectrum analyzer with a tracking generator (SA). We select the gate center for separability of the measured states, correct each instrument once by a per-bin subtraction of a reference state, and train a fully connected network on sweeps from both instruments. The test object is a torso phantom with lung-shaped compartments whose filling takes eleven dielectric states, each held out in turn. On $S_{11}$ the reconstruction from SA input reaches a shape error of 0.08 dB$^2$ with a state ordering of 0.81. This matches the reconstruction from VNA input. On $S_{21}$ it reaches 0.82 dB$^2$ and keeps the ordered groups of the states. An explicit phase prediction performs much worse, because gating needs the phase to about 0.2 rad RMS. Across eleven measurement configurations the dependence of phase on magnitude is strong for reflection and weak for transmission. This explains the difference between $S_{11}$ and $S_{21}$. Where the magnitude carries enough of the phase, a scalar instrument gives the gated response without any phase measurement after installation. On the open UM-BMID breast data set, with whole phantoms held out, the shape error of the network is 30 percent lower than that of a per-bin linear map.

Time-domain gating separates the arrivals that form a microwave S-parameter measurement.
SemPurify: Semantics-Aware Data Purification for Black-box Backdoor Defense
Authors: Jiawei Yu, Syed Irfan Ali Meerza, Yi Wu, Amir Sadovnik, Jian Liu
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-10-16T15:09:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T15:09:14+00:00
Comments: Accepted by the IEEE/CVF Winter Conference on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV 2027)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Backdoor attacks have emerged as a critical security threat in machine learning, where adversaries embed triggers within poisoned training samples to manipulate a model's predictions on targeted inputs while leaving its general behavior unaffected. This stealthy nature makes backdoor attacks difficult to detect, as the model functions normally until activated by a specific malicious trigger. Current purification-based defenses use image transformations (e.g., blurring) to disrupt trigger patterns but often degrade image quality. Even with advanced restoration techniques like diffusion models, these approaches can still alter visual or semantic information, reducing utility for downstream tasks. To address this, we propose the first semantics-aware data purification framework for black-box backdoor defense, SemPurify, which explicitly incorporates multi-level semantics (i.e., low-level semantic embeddings and high-level image tags) within a text-to-image (T2I) framework to guide image restoration during purification. Using this semantic information, the T2I model subsequently refines the transformed samples, helping preserve visual details and semantic information during restoration. Extensive experiments spanning multiple backdoor scenarios, i.e., classification models, CLIP-style encoders, and generative models, demonstrate that SemPurify reliably removes backdoors while effectively preserving semantic information for downstream tasks.
Summary / 总结
Backdoor attacks have emerged as a critical security threat in machine learning, where adversaries embed triggers within poisoned training samples to manipulate a model's predictions on targeted inputs while leaving its general behavior unaffected.
