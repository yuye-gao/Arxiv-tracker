Rethinking Video Autoencoders for Interactive World Models
Authors: Adrien Ramanana Rahary, Yutong Bai, Nicolas Dufour, Patrick Pérez, David Picard
First: 2026-10-21T17:58:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T17:58:11+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Video world models predict future frames from past frames and actions. They serve as simulators in which agents train, plan or are evaluated, and that humans can control interactively. They generate in the latent space of a video autoencoder, whose compression sets their cost per frame and how long each action waits to take effect. Yet world models often reuse the autoencoders of text-to-video generators, built for offline generation with no agent waiting and, without their training data and code, not retrainable at another compression. We therefore ask how, where and how much a world model should compress. We build CADRE, an open family of causal diffusion representation autoencoders spanning multiple spatio-temporal compressions. Its members differ only in a small bottleneck on frozen features and share a diffusion decoder self-distilled to one or a few steps. We train the same world model on each autoencoder in two domains, manipulation and navigation, with two environments each. CADRE forms most of the latency-quality frontier, especially with a moving camera. Our study also leads to four findings. At strong compression, a diffusion decoder is sharper than perceptual regression. In robot manipulation, temporal compression is more effective in the autoencoder than in the world model. Manipulation and navigation call for opposite compressions: temporal compression costs little when the camera is still, and spatial compression when it moves. Surprisingly, with a still camera, coarser spatial tokens hurt motion far more than image quality. Code, models and a live demo are at https://kyutai.org/blog/2026-10-21-cadre

Video world models predict future frames from past frames and actions.
VoxProg: 3D Spatial Reasoning through Voxel Programming
Authors: Zhichao Zhai, Menglei Zhao, Guikun Chen, Zihao Yan, Kanzhi Wu, Wenguan Wang, Jun Xiao
First: 2026-10-21T17:58:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T17:58:05+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://voxprog.github.io
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Language models' abilities to interpret tasks and generate programs offer a promising basis for 3D spatial reasoning. Yet following spatial instructions in 3D scenes requires programs to construct geometric references that language leaves implicit. In this work, we introduce VoxProg, which formulates 3D object placement through voxel programming: a language model translates task interpretations into executable programs over local voxel views of scene surfaces. Because these views index surface geometry on regular grids with explicit coordinates and neighborhood relations, an evidence program can isolate task-relevant regions and boundaries directly from scene meshes, and a downstream pose program can measure the resulting geometry to compute a target $SE(3)$ pose. Visual and numerical feedback from execution lets the model inspect intermediate selections and candidate placements to iteratively revise its code. To evaluate open-ended placement across functional use, geometric fit, and contextual arrangements, we introduce the 71-task VoxProg-Bench benchmark along with an evaluation suite pairing perceptual Intent Alignment and 3D Physical Validity. Across VoxProg-Bench and external benchmarks, VoxProg consistently outperforms relation-based, keypoint-based, and mesh-based baselines.

Language models' abilities to interpret tasks and generate programs offer a promising basis for 3D spatial reasoning.
The End of Consensus Research?
Authors: Clément Burgelin, Rachid Guerraoui, Zicong Hong, Yinan Hu, Anne-Marie Kermarrec, Rafael Pires, Rasmus Moorits Veski, Martijn de Vos
First: 2026-10-21T17:55:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T17:55:19+00:00
Comments: 39 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Consensus is a fundamental problem in distributed computing and at the core of state machine replication (SMR). Despite decades of active human research, discovering practical low-latency solutions to the problem is still open, as it requires combining protocol knowledge, correctness reasoning, implementation, and experimental evaluation. We report on how AI helped us discover A-greement, a new consensus protocol for geo-replicated state machines that significantly outperforms state-of-the-art solutions. Averaged across six deployments with 5 or 9 replicas, A-greement reduces the mean latency of a geo-distributed key-value store over the fastest baseline by 12.2% and 33.3% on write-heavy and read-heavy workloads, respectively, and matches the fastest baseline on read-only workloads. A-greement combines lease-authorized local reads, direct client learning for leader-ordered writes, and a fast fixed-quorum per-key write path, selected according to network topology and workload. At the heart of our discovery lies AI-greement, an agent harness for the "autonomous" discovery, implementation, experimentation, and verification of candidate protocols. AI-greement integrates (1) a concept-oriented protocol knowledge base, (2) a modular implementation skeleton, and (3) a Lean-based verification framework with reusable safety definitions, reference specifications, and proofs. AI-greement is of independent interest: its Lean formalization uncovers several previously unreported vulnerabilities in existing consensus protocols, and it provides guidelines on how domain-specific harnesses can turn general-purpose coding agents into effective tools for (distributed) computing research. Yet we argue that human consensus experts remain essential before, during, and after the search: agents reshape the researcher's role rather than end consensus research.
Summary / 总结
Consensus is a fundamental problem in distributed computing and at the core of state machine replication (SMR).
Which Differences Matter? Controlled Tests of Rule Transfer, Joint Reliability, and Information Use in Decision Models
Authors: Tianyou Lai, Wentao Yue, Zhiwei Zuo, Qilei Li
First: 2026-10-21T17:04:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T17:04:02+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Decision models map stated rules and observed states to discrete answers. Aggregate accuracy leaves unresolved which rule and information changes guide those answers. We connect controlled input changes to transfer, joint reliability, and directed information use through executable arithmetic rules. Semantic rule separation and crossed rule and state tests make transfer and joint success separately measurable. We compare equivalent wordings, calibrate joint correctness through marginal-preserving reassociation, and alter tables to define alternative answer targets. Adaptation gains increase with nearest-source answer agreement; fully conflicting quartets yield small pooled gains and different gain signs across source splits. Equivalent rewrites have opposing task-level effects, linking transfer gains to rule presentation. Joint-score departures depend on which rule and state associations the reference preserves. A decision service changes answers more across equivalent wordings than across identical-text repeats and usually follows the answer targets implied by altered table values or mappings.
Summary / 总结
Decision models map stated rules and observed states to discrete answers.
DONIC: Generalist Dexterous Bimanual Tracking Control from Human Demonstrations
Authors: Mingze Sun, Ziang Cao, Yuan Fei, Qiankuan Tao, Zihan Wang, Tianqi Liu, Junhao Chen, Haiwen Diao, Fangzhou Hong, Zhaoxi Chen, Ziwei Liu
First: 2026-10-21T16:36:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:36:02+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Despite recent progress in dexterous reference tracking, existing models focus primarily on single-hand manipulation and a limited range of interactions, leaving the bimanual dexterous behaviors common in real life unaddressed. The scarcity of contact-rich bimanual manipulation data and the increased complexity of coordinating contacts make developing a generalist bimanual tracker particularly challenging. Therefore, we present 1) DONIC, the first generalist bimanual tracker jointly trained across operation categories and human-motion sources, together with 2) BiHOI, a multi-view dataset of 1,575 sequences spanning 229 objects and emphasizing sustained bimanual interaction, where reconstructed interactions are retargeted into robot references. DONIC combines proximal policy optimization with behavioral cloning in a feedback loop that recycles verified successful executions into training demonstrations. An object-centered per-hand reward maintains object-motion feedback while supervising each hand according to its reference contact role. Clip-Centered Advantages remove reference-dependent advantage offsets while preserving within-trajectory differences. Together, these designs enable one single policy to learn coordinated manipulation across heterogeneous references. Without finetuning, a single checkpoint achieves 86.79% success on unseen trajectories of training objects and 57.32% on held-out objects, surpassing the state-of-the-art bimanual tracking baselines by 77.36 and 45.73 percentage points.
Summary / 总结
Despite recent progress in dexterous reference tracking, existing models focus primarily on single-hand manipulation and a limited range of interactions, leaving the bimanual dexterous behaviors common in real life unaddressed.
The Price of Current-Effect Inference in Nonstationary Multi-Armed Bandits
Authors: Sharan Sahu, Martin T. Wells
First: 2026-10-21T16:26:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:26:48+00:00
Comments: 49 pages, 8 figures, 5 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Adaptive experiments use bandit algorithms to favor interventions with higher expected rewards. However, decision-makers also require reliable inference on current treatment effects: differences between intervention and reference-arm mean rewards at the present time, rather than historical averages. These objectives conflict: reward maximization favors better interventions, whereas current-effect inference requires continued exploration of potentially suboptimal interventions as outcomes change over time. We characterize this tradeoff for $K$-arm bandits with Hölder-smooth mean rewards of exponent $β\in(0,1]$ and constant $L$. Requiring high-probability simultaneous coverage of all current effects by confidence intervals of width at most $w$, we establish matching minimax bounds, up to logarithmic factors, on expected dynamic regret. Over horizon $T$, our algorithm TRACE (Tracking Rewards and Current Effects) achieves regret of order $\widetilde{O}_β\left(\sqrt{KT}+K^{β/(2β+1)}L^{1/(2β+1)}T^{(β+1)/(2β+1)}+Kw^{-2}(1+L^{1/β}w^{-1/β})\right)$, where the notation hides logarithms and constants depending only on $β$. This regret--precision tradeoff separates reward-learning regret from the cost of refreshing current-effect estimates, which remains unavoidable even when one arm is uniformly optimal. An empirical-Bernstein e-process extension adapts exploration to realized prediction errors while preserving worst-case guarantees. When within-block drift is negligible relative to sampling error, all observations, including reward-learning observations, can be reused for asymptotically normal pointwise inference and stronger local stationarity yields local asymptotic efficiency. Finally, we show that regret constraints can prevent honest adaptation to unknown smoothness and support our theory with extensive numerical experiments.
Summary / 总结
Adaptive experiments use bandit algorithms to favor interventions with higher expected rewards.
Mastering Model Fit: Applications of the InterModel Vigorish for Interpretable Machine Learning
Authors: Jiani Yan, Pengyun Wang, Klint Kanopka, Ben Domingue, Charles Rahal
First: 2026-10-21T16:17:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:17:17+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Accuracy alone gives little information about which features or model components improve probabilistic fit, which outcomes benefit, or how fit changes during training. We introduce the InterModel Vigorish (IMV) into the Machine Intelligence space in order to express differences in fit as an expected betting advantage over a reference model. SHAP-IMV attributes gains to features through Shapley-weighted subset comparisons, Multi-IMV evaluates one-vs-rest and pairwise class discrimination, and Ablate-IMV assesses architectural components and feature groups by ablation. We evaluate these applications over ten random seeds using tabular data, images, sensor sequences, and graphs. Feature rankings vary across estimators. High one-vs-rest accuracy can accompany negligible improvement over a null, while ablation can preserve or improve fit under a fixed training budget. On six graph-classification benchmarks, we time-profile IMV over 200 training epochs, reporting seed means and ranges. The open-source imvpy library and accompanying notebooks provide code to replicate our work and to use the IMV more widely.
Summary / 总结
Accuracy alone gives little information about which features or model components improve probabilistic fit, which outcomes benefit, or how fit changes during training.
Proposals and challenges for zero-knowledge verification of frontier AI training
Authors: Pierre Peigné, Ky Nguyen, Paul Wang
First: 2026-06-03T20:57:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:10:51+00:00
Comments: 71 pages, 2 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Proposals for Frontier AI rules often use cumulative training compute as the main criterion for identifying high-impact models. Recent governance analyses call zero-knowledge proofs promising but currently impractical at frontier scale [26, 4]. We argue this barrier reflects the current paradigm, not a fundamental limit. Our frontier dense-pre-training design combines a pre-committed specification, inter-node network observations, and live Merkle commitments to intermediate computation. Our reference zero-knowledge virtual machine (zkVM) provides a succinct proof layer with native BF16/FP32 precompiles. We qualitatively compare zkVMs, custom circuits, and direct interactive audits by transferability, soundness, and cost. Public randomness beacons are a separate audit-selection choice. At challenged positions, proofs check the committed computation against declared BF16/FP32 semantics bit-for-bit. A private training specification targets model-architecture confidentiality. Genesis proofs cover initialisation; then, in-training step proofs verify selected steps across the run. Ex-ante attestations enforce policy-relevant claims as running invariants on every proved step. Together they create a governance-auditable training record. Detection-probability formulas quantify sampling guarantees from the sampling budget and the share of computation altered. We give numerical examples and explain what sampling does not cover. We note, however, that weight smuggling could reuse or merge weights trained elsewhere without charging that work to the current run. We then argue that regimes with global training budgets are more robust than regimes with per-run FLOP caps. This advantage depends on counting all contributing training. We catalogue remaining research and engineering problems for external contributors.
Summary / 总结
Proposals for Frontier AI rules often use cumulative training compute as the main criterion for identifying high-impact models.
Learning to Interfere in Non-Orthogonal Multiple-Access Joint Source-Channel Coding
Authors: Selim F. Yilmaz, Can Karamanli, Deniz Gunduz
Venue: IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications, vol. 25, pp. 16859-16873, 2026
First: 2025-03-23T12:27:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:06:31+00:00
Comments: 20 pages, 19 figures, 4 tables, including appendix. Published in IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We consider multiple transmitters aiming to communicate their source signals (e.g., images) over a multiple access channel (MAC). Conventional communication systems minimize interference by orthogonally allocating resources (time and/or bandwidth) among users, which limits their capacity. We introduce a machine learning (ML)-aided wireless image transmission method that merges compression and channel coding using a multi-view autoencoder, which allows the transmitters to use all the available channel resources simultaneously, resulting in a non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) scheme. The receiver must recover all the images from the received superposed signal, while also associating each image with its transmitter. Traditional ML models deal with individual samples, whereas our model allows signals from different users to interfere in order to leverage gains from NOMA under limited bandwidth and power constraints. We introduce a progressive fine-tuning algorithm that doubles the number of users at each iteration, maintaining initial performance with orthogonalized user-specific projections, which is then improved through fine-tuning steps. Remarkably, our method supports up to 64 users with potential for further scaling, with only a 2.4% increase in the number of trainable parameters compared to a single-user model, significantly enhancing recovered image quality and outperforming existing NOMA-based methods over a wide range of datasets, metrics, and channel conditions. Our approach paves the way for more efficient and robust multi-user communication systems, leveraging innovative ML components and strategies.

CausalAlign: Causal Alignment of Video Generation Models for Consistent Ego-Motion
Authors: Kai Zhu, Yintianrun Zhang, Shuwei Jiang, Ruolin Zuo, Yixin Chen, Duomin Wang, Miao Liu, Yin Li, Daquan Zhou, Yiwu Zhong
First: 2026-10-21T15:54:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T15:54:57+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Diffusion-based video generation models (VGMs) can produce visually plausible videos, yet their camera ego-motion often drifts over long horizons. Camera ego-motion determines how the viewpoint changes between frames and is essential for maintaining consistent geometry and trajectories. To address this problem, we propose CausalAlign, a causal alignment framework with two components. First, we diagnose how precise ego motion is represented, used, and retained within VGMs using linear probes and causal interventions, revealing a rise-and-fall pattern during denoising. Second, we strengthen ego-motion state in VGM by aligning the representations at the identified layers to a 3D foundation model through delta-token space. This space encodes the changes between consecutive frames, allowing the alignment to focus on ego-motion dynamics rather than static scene content. With Wan2.2 fine-tuned on DL3DV and RealEstate10K, CausalAlign can largely improve motion accuracy, geometric consistency, and temporal smoothness, while preserving visual quality. For example, it improves geometric consistency by more than 20% relative to the base model. Moreover, with the fine-tuned VGM, CausalAlign improves the success rate of world action models (WAMs) by 10 points on mobile manipulation tasks of RoboCasa365 benchmark. Overall, CausalAlign improves ego-motion consistency in diffusion-based VGMs and yields noticeable gains in embodied models.
Summary / 总结
Diffusion-based video generation models (VGMs) can produce visually plausible videos, yet their camera ego-motion often drifts over long horizons.
