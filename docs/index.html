MoI-Bench: A Benchmark for Image Generation and Editing on Mobile Devices
Authors: Kailai Feng, Haoxiang Rao, Yuxiang Wei, Bo Chen, Junsong Li, Yang Pan, Chengkun Cai, Jagmohan Chauhan
First: 2026-10-27T17:31:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:31:40+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://carlofkl.github.io/MoI-Bench-project/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Developing on-device image models requires considering practical user needs, task types and device resource constraints. However, existing benchmarks provide limited guidance on identifying capability gaps between on-device models and server-side models. We introduce MoI-Bench, a benchmark grounded in mobile user needs, comprising 64 subtasks and 1150 samples. It defines 16 operations, such as removal and replacement, and eight visual target categories covering content such as portraits and text. These form two orthogonal axes whose applicable combinations define specific tasks, such as removing facial makeup. To better guide model improvement, we further propose MoI-Eval, which introduces task-specific criteria covering instruction following, visual quality and detail preservation. We further construct sample-level evaluation questions and aggregate their scores into overall quality and capability profiles along the operation, target and criteria axes. We evaluate representative on-device and server-side models and measure mobile deployment costs. Results show that this decoupled Operation x Target design provides finer-grained guidance for improving compact models. For example, it highlights the need to prioritize specific capability gaps (e.g., text-replacement) over broad application-level goals (e.g., improving text editing). Unlike conventional benchmarks that rank models using a single score, we introduce a two-dimensional Quality-Deployability capability map to assess models for practical mobile use. The map shows that current on-device models can achieve efficient generation or editing but expanding their task coverage deserves greater attention than further improving deployment efficiency. Further progress requires improving editing quality, particularly identity preservation and text rendering, while broadening task coverage.
Summary / 总结
Developing on-device image models requires considering practical user needs, task types and device resource constraints.
ReferStream: Interactive Subject Injection for Streaming Video Generation
Authors: Shuolin Xu, Guanyuan Li, Boyu Li, Kelu Yao, Xiaogang Xu, Hongchuan Yu, Shuicheng Yan, Xiaobin Hu
First: 2026-10-27T17:12:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:12:42+00:00
Comments: 18 pages. Project page: https://xushuolin.github.io/ReferStream/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Injecting new subjects naturally during streaming video generation requires integrating newly supplied visual references into the existing video while preserving established subjects and the scene. Encoding references independently of the current scene provides little guidance for this integration, while keeping their features in persistent memory can interfere with established context. We propose ReferStream, a framework that requires no additional training and combines reference construction using the current scene with transient cache injection. Joint Latent places reference latents in target regions of the latest scene latent, allowing reference features to be extracted in context. Cache Restoration uses these features to condition the injection chunk, then restores the overwritten global cache entries while retaining the newly generated history. This procedure incorporates new visual conditions without retraining the generator or revising emitted frames. We further introduce ReferStream Bench, a new benchmark with scheduled reference arrivals, evolving instructions, and explicit annotations of subject presence for InShot and MultiShot generation. Evaluation follows the event schedule and measures both the fidelity of new subjects and the preservation of established subjects, alongside video quality, text adherence, and count accuracy. Experiments on ReferStream Bench show higher scores for text adherence, subject similarity, and count accuracy than the compared baselines, including offline methods given all references in advance. Project page: https://xushuolin.github.io/ReferStream/.

Injecting new subjects naturally during streaming video generation requires integrating newly supplied visual references into the existing video while preserving established subjects and the scene.
Learning response-aware patient dynamics for respiratory support
Authors: Xiaolei Lu, Shamim Nemati
First: 2026-09-26T16:56:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T16:42:43+00:00
Comments: Under review
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Respiratory support can shape the short-term physiological trajectory of critically ill patients, but patients receiving the same intervention may follow different physiological trajectories. Clinical patient dynamics models typically predict future states from recent physiology and recorded interventions, while physiological change is mainly represented through the predicted future state. We propose a response-aware patient dynamics model that explicitly represents physiological change during autoregressive state updating. The model decomposes predicted physiological change into state-dependent baseline dynamics and respiratory-support-associated deviations, with room air providing a reference for the decomposition. We provide a formal analysis of this reference-anchored formulation. A response pathway encodes the predicted physiological change and uses it to update the latent patient state across the forecast horizon. Across ICU cohorts from two independent institutions, the proposed model achieves comparable overall trajectory prediction to patient dynamics baselines, with more consistent improvements when physiological states are changing.
Summary / 总结
Respiratory support can shape the short-term physiological trajectory of critically ill patients, but patients receiving the same intervention may follow different physiological trajectories.
EvoIn: Bridging Evolution and Internalization for Agent Fine-Tuning
Authors: Shihan Dou, Shaofan Liu, Zhonghang Lu, Jiahang Lin, Shichun Liu, Binghai Wang, Jiajie Jin, Guanting Dong, Tao Gui, Qi Zhang, Xuanjing Huang
First: 2026-09-28T14:36:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T16:31:42+00:00
Comments: 36 pages, 3 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recent work has explored improving agents by jointly evolving their harnesses and models, but often takes a ''potpourri'' approach that bundles together new tools, new decision-making procedures, and model adaptation to the evolved harness under a single notion of agent improvement. In this paper, we instead investigate how agents can improve their decision-making procedures. In particular, we propose EvoIn, an agent fine-tuning framework that bridges evolution and internalization. EvoIn first analyzes agent execution traces to evolve and validate new decision-making procedures by temporarily instantiating them in the harness. The validated procedures guide the agent to generate improved reasoning traces. These traces are then rewritten into self-contained reasoning traces, removing explicit references to harness instructions while expressing the induced decision logic as the model's own reasoning. Finally, EvoIn fine-tunes the model on the rewritten traces, internalizing these procedures so that the improved decision-making persists without the evolved harness at inference time. We evaluate EvoIn on diverse benchmarks and find that it consistently enables agents to learn stronger decision-making procedures, raising the pass rate by 10.9 points in-domain and by 9.2 points out-of-domain. Results further show that the internalized decision procedures generalize to unseen tasks. Case studies show that agents can learn to decide how to solve a task before solving it, for example by checking a document's length to choose between reading it in full and searching it. EvoIn is also broadly applicable, showing consistent improvements on another model family.
Summary / 总结
Recent work has explored improving agents by jointly evolving their harnesses and models, but often takes a ''potpourri'' approach that bundles together new tools, new decision-making procedures, and model adaptation to the evolved harness under a single notion of agent improvement.
HGPRGDel: GPR-Aware Learning and Ranking for Growth-Coupled Gene Deletion Design
Authors: Ziwei Yang, Takeyuki Tamura
First: 2026-10-27T16:07:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T16:07:02+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Designing microbial strains for growth-coupled production, in which cellular growth is linked to the synthesis of a target compound through genetic interventions, is a fundamental goal in metabolic engineering. Constraint-based methods can identify growth-coupled deletion strategies, but design for each target typically requires a separate search across a large number of possible gene-deletion combinations. This motivates a reusable approach that learns from previously evaluated strain designs to prioritize candidate interventions before downstream constraint-based evaluation. We present a reproducible protocol for growth-coupled gene deletion design that learns and ranks gene-deletion priorities for each target metabolite. Using computationally curated deletion strategies as learning resources, the protocol generates continuous deletion-priority rankings, constructs candidate strategies at adjustable search-space budgets, and evaluates these candidates through constraint-based metabolic analysis. Its learning component, HGPRGDel, explicitly represents gene-protein-reaction (GPR) logic, capturing how multiple genes can jointly support a reaction or alternative genes can maintain its activity, while integrating molecular and reaction-level information. Across e_coli_core, iMM904, and iML1515, the protocol achieves the highest macro-F1 and AUC among the evaluated learning configurations. HGPRGDel's rankings recovered an average of 90% of the reference deletion genes from MetNetComp by examining fewer genes than the evaluated baseline methods. By separating learned intervention prioritization from downstream constraint-based assessment, the protocol provides a reproducible framework for reusing strain-design knowledge while controlling the size of the candidate space and retaining mechanistic evaluation of growth-coupled production.
Summary / 总结
Designing microbial strains for growth-coupled production, in which cellular growth is linked to the synthesis of a target compound through genetic interventions, is a fundamental goal in metabolic engineering.
QD-PINN:A Physics-Informed Neural Network Approach to Bayesian Quickest Detection in High-Dimensional Diffusions
Authors: Hang Ye, Yi Liu, Haipeng Xing
First: 2026-10-27T16:05:13+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T16:05:13+00:00
Comments: 24 pages, 9 figures,
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Quickest detection problems (QDPs) arise in a wide range of applications. However, the complementarity structure of the associated variational inequalities poses substantial challenges for traditional numerical methods, particularly in high-dimensional settings. We propose a PINN-based framework for estimating the optimal stopping boundary in QDPs with high-dimensional diffusion processes and spatial dependence, while overcoming the curse of dimensionality. Theoretically, we establish a novel equivalence between the free-boundary PDE and an infinite-dimensional stochastic optimization problem, rigorously justifying the use of finite-dimensional neural networks to approximate the true solution. Furthermore, we assess the robustness of the proposed framework by quantifying uncertainty in the learned stopping boundaries using deep ensembles and Monte Carlo dropout. The results show low variability across independent training runs and stochastic forward passes, indicating stable and consistent boundary estimates. We also compare our PINN results with reference numerical solutions where available and find close agreement.
Summary / 总结
Quickest detection problems (QDPs) arise in a wide range of applications.
VDE Bench: Evaluating The Capability of Image Editing Models to Modify Visual Documents
Authors: Hongzhu Yi, Yujia Yang, Yuanxiang Wang, Tong Li, Zhenyu Guan, Tianyu Zong, Jiahuan Chen, Chenxi Bao, Tiankun Yang, Haopeng Jin, Yixuan Yuan, Xinming Wang, Tao Yu, Ruilin Gao, Ruiwen Tao, Haijin Liang, Jin Ma, Jinwen Luo, Yeshani, Xinyu Zuo, Jungang Xu

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Image editing models can modify visual content through natural language, but editing dense documents requires precise local changes amid tightly coupled text, tables, and layout. We introduce VDE Bench, an evaluation resource for studying instruction-guided editing of Chinese and English document images. VDE Bench contains 3,506 instruction-guided image editing samples, including 1,578 text-region edits and 1,928 table-region edits. The benchmark pairs editing instructions and reference images with annotated editing regions and structured OCR outputs. Its evaluation protocol combines region-level and full-page measurements to assess textual correctness, structural fidelity, and visual content preservation. By focusing on the interaction between local edits and their surrounding document context, VDE Bench complements existing visual text editing benchmarks and provides a resource for diagnosing document-specific editing failures.

M4Bench: Evaluating Procedural Specification for Clinical EHR Derivation Agents
Authors: Hannes Ill, Rafi Al Attrach, Rajna Fani, Ahram Han, Ryohei Yamamoto, Marlene Louisa Moerig, Dukyong Yoon, Leo Anthony Celi
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-27T15:59:13+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T15:59:13+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (Evaluations and Datasets Track). 10 pages main text, 33 pages total. Code: https://github.com/hannesill/m4/tree/m4bench-neurips2026-camera-ready/benchmark . Evidence: https://huggingface.co/datasets/hannesill/m4bench-evidence
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Clinical research over electronic health records depends on derived variables whose definitions sit outside the schema, in item codes, time windows, missingness rules, and output-grain choices that change cohorts and downstream conclusions. We introduce M4Bench, 28 clinical derivation tasks across 15 task families on MIMIC-IV and eICU, to test whether AI coding agents can reproduce such derivations and how clinician-reviewed agent skills change their results. Its controls decompose the value of skills into specification completeness, delivery format, and schema familiarity. In a Docker-isolated Codex campaign (GPT 5.4 Mini and GPT 5.5), skills raise reward by 0.136 over the no-skill baseline, with 26 of 28 tasks improving. Specification completeness accounts for most of this gain: on an eight-task control subset, an operational spec alone raises reward by 0.162, and skills add only 0.024 on top, indistinguishable from zero. On a ten-task subset, decoy skills score like no skill and raw reference SQL nearly matches targeted skills, arguing against generic context or the skill format as the explanation. Schema obfuscation and restructuring preserve 86% and 70% of the skill gain (GPT 5.4 Mini, 26 MIMIC-IV tasks), a descriptive robustness check rather than a bound on memorization. A descriptive Claude Code replication on all 28 tasks (Opus 4.7 and Sonnet 4.6, 560 runs) is directionally consistent (+0.120, 21 of 28 tasks improving). These gains are partial agreement with the reference, not completed tasks: only 25 of 280 with-skill and 13 of 280 no-skill Codex runs pass every strict check, and skills barely change which rows agents return (key F1 +0.003). Skills are released as a versioned, clinician-reviewable markdown delivery format, with a family-level reference-validity audit. M4Bench releases tasks, skills, evaluation code, and sanitized per-run evidence as an open-source benchmark.
Summary / 总结
Clinical research over electronic health records depends on derived variables whose definitions sit outside the schema, in item codes, time windows, missingness rules, and output-grain choices that change cohorts and downstream conclusions.
Memorize When Needed: Decoupled Memory Control for Spatially Consistent Long-Horizon Video Generation
Authors: Yanjun Guo, Zhengqiang Zhang, Pengfei Wang, Xinyue Liang, Zhiyuan Ma, Lei Zhang
First: 2026-04-20T13:00:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T15:32:01+00:00
Comments: 24 pages, with supplementary material
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Spatially consistent long-horizon video generation aims to maintain temporal and spatial consistency along predefined camera trajectories. Existing methods mostly entangle memory modeling with video generation, leading to inconsistent content during scene revisits and diminished generative capacity when exploring novel regions, even when trained on extensive annotated data. To address these limitations, we propose a decoupled framework that separates memory conditioning from generation. Our approach significantly reduces training costs while simultaneously enhancing spatial consistency and preserving the generative capacity for novel scene exploration. Specifically, we employ a lightweight, independent memory branch to learn precise spatial consistency from historical observations. We first introduce a hybrid memory representation to capture complementary temporal and spatial cues from generated frames, then leverage a per-frame cross-attention mechanism to ensure each frame is conditioned exclusively on the most spatially relevant historical information, which is injected into the generative model to ensure spatial consistency. When generating new scenes, a camera-aware gating mechanism is proposed to mediate the interaction between memory and generation modules, enabling memory conditioning only when meaningful historical references exist. Compared with the existing method, our method is highly data-efficient, yet the experiments demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of both visual quality and spatial consistency. Project page can be found at https://iguoyanjun.github.io/memorize-when-needed-project-page/.

Displacement Moments and Path-Terminal Separation in Brownian Interpolant Sampling
Authors: Zhong Han
First: 2026-10-27T15:30:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T15:30:32+00:00
Comments: 56 pages, 4 figures; 8-page main text and complete appendices
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Brownian stochastic interpolants connect paired endpoint samples, yet their sampling error can depend on displacement moments even when neither endpoint has a first moment. For arbitrary Borel couplings, we prove early-stopped, target-to-output KL guarantees for standard Euler sampling from the true source. A fourth displacement moment gives an explicit $O(N^{-1})$ bound; a second moment suffices for fixed-law convergence as drift error vanishes. For one fixed coupling with no displacement moment above two, original bridge-path KL stays of order one, comparison-path KL is of order $1/\log N$, and terminal KL is at most $C/N^2$ on the same grid. The path decomposition identifies the information about the source discarded by Markov projection. When the original bridge is Markov, current-state Euler sampling instead has a second-moment $O((M_2+d)/N)$ bound; finite-entropy Schroedinger bridges for the probability Brownian reference are included. We also construct a current-state learner with expected output KL consistency from iid endpoint pairs. Numerical diagnostics illustrate the distinct errors. Conditional diffusion identities specify a restoration interface, while image controls find no iterative advantage over cheap probabilistic predictors. Uniform terminal rates for general second-moment couplings remain open.
Summary / 总结
Brownian stochastic interpolants connect paired endpoint samples, yet their sampling error can depend on displacement moments even when neither endpoint has a first moment.
