Multimodal Concert Video Editing: System Design and Exploratory Evaluation
Authors: Lennart Schiweck, Adrian Kempf, Michelle Retig, Moritz Feucht, Tim Mangliers, Benjamin Himpel
First: 2026-10-26T17:43:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T17:43:58+00:00
Comments: 19 pages, 3 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Editing multi-camera concerts requires coordinated decisions about musical relevance, visual quality and cut timing. We present a modular framework that combines instrument activity from multitrack audio, visible instruments and image sharpness with beat-based timing and probabilistic camera selection. An interpretable scoring model links these components to the choice of views over complete shot intervals. Component tests and an exploratory expert study on a four-camera concert yield mixed assessments across the editing variants. The findings motivate extending local audiovisual relevance with contextual and sequence-level criteria. The architecture provides a basis for testing these extensions and studying how component behaviour affects the quality of complete edits.

VidEdit3D: Flexible 3D Gaussian Editing with Video Diffusion
Authors: Yanming Yang, Chenxi Song, Tong Zhao, Juntao Xu, Wenhao Rao, Yiwen Chen, Yiwei Wang, Chi Zhang
First: 2026-10-26T17:03:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T17:03:44+00:00
Comments: Project Page: https://2hitee.github.io/VidEdit3D-page/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Recent advancements in text-guided 3D editing, particularly with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have shown promise but still lag behind the flexibility of 2D editing tools. Existing methods face a critical trade-off between preserving multi-view consistency and allowing structural modifications. Image-based editors can also struggle to apply the intended edit to objects viewed from unusual angles. To address these limitations, we introduce VidEdit3D, a framework that reformulates 3D scene editing as a trajectory-based video editing task. By drawing a parallel between camera movement in static 3D scenes and temporal dynamics in video, we leverage the robust spatiotemporal priors of pre-trained Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) to encourage semantic and structural coherence across views. Specifically, we propose Trajectory-Guided Rendering (TGR) to select informative keyframes and construct smooth video clips aligned with VDM input requirements. To propagate edits from an anchor view to these clips, we develop Flow-Aligned Video Editing (FAVE), a training-free framework incorporating an Editing Consistency Guidance (ECG) mechanism. ECG contrasts source and edited image conditions at a shared latent state, using scheduled source-image amplification to strengthen adherence to the edited reference while maintaining original scene context. Finally, we introduce Region-Selective 3DGS Refinement (RS-GS) to strictly confine geometric and appearance updates to target primitives, reducing background attribute leakage. Experiments demonstrate stronger instruction alignment and better multi-view consistency than the baselines.
Summary / 总结
Recent advancements in text-guided 3D editing, particularly with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have shown promise but still lag behind the flexibility of 2D editing tools.
Hierarchical GNNs for power flow: letting physics shape the hierarchy
Authors: Carmine Delle Femine, Leire Garin Atxaga, Asier Diaz-Iglesias, Juan Pablo Maroto Herrera, Ane Miren Florez-Tapia, Marco Quartulli, Izaro Goienetxea Urkizu
First: 2026-09-22T15:41:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T16:52:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Hierarchical latent communication improves the generalization of a power-flow model, shared across three grids, to new operating scenarios. The module exchanges information through two reduced graphs inside the corrective network of GENCO, replacing two of its local correction steps. We compare Kron-derived transports, a same-anchor Quotient construction and the flat GENCO Base architecture, all trained under one protocol of our own with about a hundred times fewer optimizer updates per grid than GENCO's reference training: 200 epochs on three grid topologies, fewer than 1,900 training scenarios per grid and three initialization seeds per model. Evaluation uses 200 newly generated, preselected scenarios per grid. On the training topologies, Kron reaches a macro family-balanced voltage error of $0.851\pm0.110$, 51.3% below a per-bus mean fitted on training solutions (1.747). Kron is below this reference on 98.5% of the 600 fresh scenarios, and both hierarchical models outperform it on every training topology in all three seeds. The flat baseline reaches $5.660\pm0.899$ and does not outperform the reference on any training topology, so Kron's 85.0% reduction relative to it compares architectures within our training regime. Kron is also 31.0% below Quotient ($1.235\pm0.225$). These results demonstrate generalization across operating scenarios within the studied topologies, with one set of learned parameters shared across grids. On two topologies unseen in training, the current models do not yet outperform the fitted reference in calibrated transfer; extrapolation to new topologies is the next development objective.
Summary / 总结
Hierarchical latent communication improves the generalization of a power-flow model, shared across three grids, to new operating scenarios.
Can JEV Support Clinical Trial Criteria Assessment?
Authors: Mengxian He, Ziyue Gao, Chun Li, Shunyi Zhang, Hao Wei, Minqing Zhang, Wu Yuan
First: 2026-10-26T16:41:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T16:41:12+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Clinical trial prescreening requires distinguishing unmet conditions from insufficient evidence in patient records. Given patient records, criterion questions, and predefined answer options, we retrospectively compared JEV, a non-generative decision model returning native option probabilities, with schema-constrained Qwen Flash across 2,048 test patient-condition pairs from TrialGPT and question-answer datasets derived from MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV, all evaluated under strict Zero Data Retention (ZDR) policies to ensure clinical data privacy. Both models assessed conditions individually or in batches sharing the same patient context, with performance measured against reference labels. In batch mode, JEV and Qwen achieved similar macro-F1 scores on TrialGPT (0.652 vs. 0.660) and MIMIC-IV (0.807 vs. 0.814), whereas JEV substantially outperformed Qwen on MIMIC-III (0.768 vs. 0.534; $Δ$ = +0.234, 95% CI: 0.187-0.281). This advantage largely reflected higher recall for conditions labeled as having insufficient information (56.3% vs. 9.9%). Across all three datasets, JEV also had lower observed median job latency (1.18-1.40 vs. 2.80-8.90 s) and 53%-74% lower estimated cost per condition. These findings position JEV as an initial assessment component that provides criterion-level judgments and flags potential information gaps for review. JEV's observed errors in interpreting clinical evidence underscore the need to verify its outputs against patient records before they inform eligibility decisions.
Summary / 总结
Clinical trial prescreening requires distinguishing unmet conditions from insufficient evidence in patient records.
OmniShowcase: Omnidirectional Subject-Consistent Video Generation from Multi-View Images
Authors: Junyeong Ahn, Geonwoo Kim, Kinam Kim, Gihwi Kim, Hoonjin Jung, Hyojin Jang, Jaegul Choo
First: 2026-10-26T16:19:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T16:19:59+00:00
Comments: 25 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Generating videos of a specific subject from a few reference images remains challenging when the camera reveals surfaces that are not visible in the input. Early subject-to-video (S2V) methods condition on a single reference image and must therefore infer the subject's unseen appearance and geometry as the viewpoint changes. To address this limitation, recent methods extend S2V to multiple reference views, providing more information about the subject from different viewpoints. However, they do not explicitly specify the 3D structure that connects these observations. In addition, existing training data do not guarantee that viewpoint changes preserve the same underlying 3D subject. We present OmniShowcase, built on two components: (i) a fully automated data synthesis pipeline that produces training videos along diverse camera trajectories, with viewpoint variation driven by underlying subject geometry, even spanning full 360-degree orbits; and (ii) a geometry-conditioning method that provides the video model with a turntable of surface-normal maps reconstructed from the reference views without requiring temporal alignment with the generated frames. We construct Showcase-4K, a corpus of 3D-asset-based videos covering twelve camera motions across 88 trajectory configurations with reconstruction-derived surface-normal conditioning, and ShowcaseBench, a held-out evaluation set with the source meshes and camera parameters used to render each video. Experiments show state-of-the-art subject identity and 3D consistency while maintaining competitive video quality and motion. Project page: justin4ai.github.io/OmniShowcase

Generating videos of a specific subject from a few reference images remains challenging when the camera reveals surfaces that are not visible in the input.
Label-Free Finite-Volume-Residual Training of Attention Graph Neural Networks for Coupled Thermo-Fluid Fields
Authors: Tianyu Li, Zhiwei Cao, Qingang Zhang, Ruihang Wang, Binyang Song, Yonggang Wen
First: 2026-07-22T16:07:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:42:28+00:00
Comments: 60 pages, 32 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Neural surrogates are widely used in scientific machine learning for fast prediction of three-dimensional (3D) thermo-fluid fields. However, generating training data using conventional numerical solvers often incurs substantial computational and storage costs. We propose to train an attention graph neural network by minimizing the finite-volume method (FVM) residuals of the governing equations. These residuals are evaluated directly on the mesh, requiring no labeled data. We evaluate the trained surrogates against computational fluid dynamics (CFD) references and a data-supervised baseline across four scenarios. On the two steady-state benchmarks, each solved on the same mesh as its CFD reference, the FVM-loss model reproduces the reference flow and temperature fields with mean field-averaged normalized root-mean-square errors (nRMSEs) near 3%, including the buoyancy-energy coupling. On the two parametric transient cases, the FVM-loss model attains lower rollout errors than the supervised baseline in almost every field, while avoiding the data-generation cost entirely. These results indicate that the FVM loss can provide a practical training signal for neural surrogates and reduce the model development cost.
Summary / 总结
Neural surrogates are widely used in scientific machine learning for fast prediction of three-dimensional (3D) thermo-fluid fields.
Recovering Wasted Compute in Autoresearch Agents
Authors: Au Kwok Chun, Abhigyan Acherjee, Amrutha Rao, Zaiqian Chen, Kazem Meidani, C. Bayan Bruss, Micah Goldblum
First: 2026-08-11T03:15:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:37:48+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A slew of recent works develop agents for solving research problems end-to-end, a paradigm increasingly referred to as autoresearch. Such agents have inspired large industry investment, motivated by their potential to automate time-consuming human labor and customize machine learning solutions for specialized applications. In this paper, we study the modeling pipeline at the core of these autoresearch systems and identify common failure modes when they are applied to tabular datasets: (1) they waste compute resolving the same bugs over and over again; (2) they often fail to tune hyperparameters even when they have a large remaining compute budget; (3) the tree-search algorithms that power them do not explore; and (4) they perform data analysis, mimicking the humans whose data they are trained on, but do not use that analysis to make downstream decisions. We explore targeted interventions and find that a global debug consultant that shares discovered runtime constraints across all branches of the search tree, prompt- and control-level enhancements, and refined tree-search algorithms successfully recover wasted compute. Our results show that large gains in autoresearch agent performance are achievable through agentic design alone, holding the underlying language model fixed.
Summary / 总结
A slew of recent works develop agents for solving research problems end-to-end, a paradigm increasingly referred to as autoresearch.
Synthetic Deformable Tubular Benchmark: A mechanically annotated dataset for evaluating deformable 3D reconstruction
Authors: Íñigo Cirauqui-Viloria
First: 2026-10-26T15:36:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:36:59+00:00
Comments: 34 pages, including 5 pages of Supplementary Methods; 10 main-text figures and 1 supplementary figure
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Evaluating deformable 3D reconstruction requires ground truth linking camera motion, surface deformation, material correspondence and image appearance. We introduce the Synthetic Deformable Tubular Benchmark, generated with LumenMorph, an open-source toolkit for creating, annotating and evaluating tubular scenes. The release contains 5,580 RGB images. Its baseline crosses three surface-motion conditions with moving and stationary cameras in six scene families; matched appearance variants cover only moving-camera finite-element actions in four families. Documented public and evaluation interfaces expose camera poses, geometry, depth, deformation fields, persistent material correspondences and discrete finite-element records. Persistent triangle identities and material weights let users track the same surface location across frames and query its three-dimensional trajectory. Separately implemented geometric and mechanical checks assess annotation consistency with the generating models. Structure-from-motion, optical-flow and supplied-geometry displacement-interpolation studies demonstrate how the resource distinguishes registration coverage from geometric accuracy, exposes camera- and surface-motion effects, and tests the benefits and mismatch of mechanical priors. Public readers, evaluation contracts and the configurable generator support reuse and extension. Data integrity, fixed-environment repeatability and retained-prediction score replay have distinct verification scopes. The benchmark provides a controlled synthetic reference without claiming anatomical or clinical realism.
Summary / 总结
Evaluating deformable 3D reconstruction requires ground truth linking camera motion, surface deformation, material correspondence and image appearance.
ExCal: Optimization-Free Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection via Excess Calibration
Authors: Kadir-Kaan Özer, René Ebeling, Markus Enzweiler
First: 2026-10-26T15:06:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:06:39+00:00
Comments: Accepted at the 2026 IEEE International Conference on Big Data (IEEE BigData 2026), Special Session on Intelligent Data Mining. 10 pages, 3 figures, 9 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We introduce ExCal, a normal-only multivariate time series anomaly detector that combines raw state retrieval, a signal/derivative subspace, and robust statistics across temporal scales. Each expert map receives a high normality floor from the training prefix. During scoring, positive excess is retained, attenuated for broadly firing maps, and combined with a small rank term. The method fits reference memories and projections without gradient optimization or anomaly labels. Prevalence and ranks depend on the complete scored sequence, making inference batch and transductive rather than online. On 180 series from 17 TSB-AD-M families, ExCal obtains AUC-PR $0.421\pm0.37$ and VUS-PR $0.452\pm0.37$ over three seeds, ranking second among 24 methods. Holm-adjusted paired tests detect no significant difference from AxonAD or StreamVAE, which are the two leading non-pretrained methods on TSB-AD-M, on either primary metric, while finding significant positive differences against the other 21 baselines. A separate single-run probe over three series measures median model-wrapper time of $0.0662$ s. Ablations associate the largest primary-metric changes with raw retrieval and excess-magnitude preservation. The evaluated score is decomposable by expert, but its diagnostic utility and online suitability remain untested.

We introduce ExCal, a normal-only multivariate time series anomaly detector that combines raw state retrieval, a signal/derivative subspace, and robust statistics across temporal scales.
Copying As Defense: Protecting Images from Editing By Eliciting Model Copying
Authors: Salma Abdel Magid, Sanghyuk Chun, Eric Slyman, Kushal Kafle, Olga Russakovsky
First: 2026-10-26T15:05:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:05:46+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Image editors built on diffusion transformers (DiTs), such as Flux Kontext, make it possible to turn a single photograph into a convincing manipulated image, including harmful and non-consensual edits. Proactive defenses aim to prevent such manipulations by adding an imperceptible perturbation before an image is shared. Existing defenses for DiT-based in-context editors make the output attend less to the protected reference image. But this leaves the model free to follow the edit instruction using the text and its own generated tokens, often producing a new, weakly grounded image rather than preventing the edit. We find that the opposite strategy is more effective: instead of leading the model to ignore the reference, we encourage it to copy it. Through controlled attention interventions, we show that concentrating target-token attention on the reference image in only a few early blocks is sufficient to collapse the editing behavior, causing the model to reproduce its input, often as a lossy copy, rather than execute the requested transformation. Based on this insight, we introduce Copying-as-Defense (CaD), which maximizes per-token target-to-reference attention mass in early transformer blocks. Since attention rows are normalized, increasing reference attention suppresses the attention allocated to the text and generated canvas. Restricting the objective to early blocks also lets us truncate the backpropagation path, keeping optimization cheap. Across editors and benchmarks, CaD reduces average edit success from 81.4% on unprotected images to 24.0%, a 70.6% relative reduction, and by a further 44.3% relative to DeContext, the strongest baseline (43.0%). CaD is also roughly 1.8-2.0x faster per optimization step than comparable methods.
Summary / 总结
Image editors built on diffusion transformers (DiTs), such as Flux Kontext, make it possible to turn a single photograph into a convincing manipulated image, including harmful and non-consensual edits.
