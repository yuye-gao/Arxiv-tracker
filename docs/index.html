Can Learning to Answer Spatial Questions Improve Novel-View Generation?
Authors: Jahyeok Koo, Hyeonseo Yu, Seungho Jang, Jaewoo Jung, Jiwon Kang, Seokju Cho, Seungryong Kim
First: 2026-10-14T17:59:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:59:58+00:00
Comments: Project Page: https://cvlab-kaist.github.io/Ask4NV/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Generating novel views from limited observations requires preserving both the appearance and spatial structure of a scene. Although recent approaches exploit fine-grained geometric cues, they do not explicitly model high-level semantic and spatial properties such as object identity, attributes, and inter-object relationships. In this work, we investigate whether 3D language understanding can provide complementary supervision for novel-view generation. We introduce Ask4NV, built on a pretrained unified multimodal model with a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture. Our model incorporates camera geometry into both VAE and ViT representations and leverages understanding features during generation through joint self-attention with a modality-aware attention masking strategy. Experiments demonstrate strong image quality and geometric consistency in novel-view generation, alongside competitive 3D language understanding. Further analysis shows that joint training improves cross-view correspondence in VAE representations and similarity in a 3D-aware feature space.
Summary / 总结
Generating novel views from limited observations requires preserving both the appearance and spatial structure of a scene.
Any Time, Any Space: A World-State-Centric Framework for Agentic Video Generation
Authors: Weijie Wang, Haomin Zhang, Ye Li, Akide Liu, Zeyu Zhang, Haoyu Zhao, Yefei He, Donny Y. Chen, Feng Chen, Bohan Zhuang
First: 2026-10-14T17:54:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:54:18+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://lhmd.top/atas/, Code: https://github.com/ziplab/ATAS
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Long-form agentic video generation must turn a screenplay into shots that remain spatially and temporally consistent across nonconsecutive requests. Passing prompts or recent frames between shots does not provide a shared spatial frame for revisiting a scene or a time-indexed record for replaying an earlier event. We present Any Time, Any Space (ATAS), which compiles a screenplay into shot requests with explicit location, camera, and event-time references. Before generation, it retrieves relevant evidence from a scene memory of registered static geometry and an event memory of dynamic observations indexed by scene, event, and time. After generation, ATAS reconstructs and registers the clip. It updates memory only when review and state checks accept the observation, while film delivery remains independent of memory admission. Across four video generators, ATAS improves all reported cross-shot consistency measures and achieves the highest aggregate benchmark score for each backbone. It also outperforms the compared agents and 3D-guided methods on target-image spatial revisit, leads three event-recapture criteria, and receives the highest human ratings on all six criteria. The video results and code are available on our project page: https://lhmd.top/atas/.
Summary / 总结
Long-form agentic video generation must turn a screenplay into shots that remain spatially and temporally consistent across nonconsecutive requests.
Speculative Sampling in Interactive Video Generation
Authors: Yubo Huang, Yuxuan Zhang, Jiaming Liu, Sirui Zhao, Enhong Chen
First: 2026-10-14T17:45:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:45:27+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://yubo-shankui.github.io/speculative_sampling
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Interactive video generation commonly relies on small bidirectional chunks to reduce response latency. Yet our preliminary experiments show that larger chunks improve video quality and better utilize GPU parallelism, allowing more latents to be sampled with only a modest increase in sampling time. Motivated by these observations, we propose Speculative Sampling, a strategy that combines the modeling power of large bidirectional windows with the low-latency interaction of small ones. It expands the modeling window by appending noise latents as padding and caches the resulting predictions for reuse when the condition remains unchanged. Predictions are delivered incrementally, while the uncommitted future remains replaceable. Our latency analysis shows that this removes the waiting-time lower bound imposed by chunk boundaries, shifting the optimization focus from reducing chunk size to accelerating per-chunk sampling. Bidirectional window size thus becomes a decision variable for jointly optimizing visual quality and sampling time, rather than a necessary constraint on low-latency interaction. This opens a path to serving large bidirectional models in interactive streaming systems without complex streaming post-training.
Summary / 总结
Interactive video generation commonly relies on small bidirectional chunks to reduce response latency.
The Physics Is the Network: Fast, Training-Free Neural Emulation
Authors: Luka Todorovic, Kaushal Gianchandani, Sai Ravela
First: 2026-10-14T17:24:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:24:48+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Neural emulation need not require learning a substitute for known physics. We investigate PolyNets that couple a continuous-time neural representation of polynomial dynamics with a simple recurrent network for discrete-time integration. When the governing coefficients are known, the resulting tensor graph requires no training and preserves the prescribed numerical scheme while enabling efficient hardware execution and parallel simulation. Using the one-dimensional Korteweg-de Vries equation, we compare PolyNet with CPU and GPU finite-difference Runge-Kutta solvers and three trained neural models: a state-map emulator, a physics-informed predictor, and a learned-tendency integrator. Analytical soliton solutions provide a reference for amplitude and phase errors across grid resolutions, 10^6 dispersively scaled time steps, ensembles of up to 10^4 trajectories, and varying initial amplitudes. PolyNet achieves accuracy comparable to the numerical solvers, including at amplitudes outside the learned models' training conditions. It combines this fidelity with high GPU throughput, approximately constant over the tested resolution range, and favorable ensemble scaling.
Summary / 总结
Neural emulation need not require learning a substitute for known physics.
A Two-Level Clinical Novelty Prioritization Framework for Disease Trajectories Discovered from Longitudinal Electronic Health Records
Authors: Jin-Jia Zheng, Vincent S. Tseng
First: 2026-10-14T17:14:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:14:29+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Graph-based analysis of electronic health records (EHRs) yields disease-trajectory catalogs, but structural support does not establish semantic novelty. We present a two-level framework to prioritize 99,527 branching motifs previously discovered by an upstream patient-disjoint, train-only MIMIC-IV v3.1 pipeline and frozen after validation-based catalog selection. The framework separates path-level and node-to-endpoint semantic sparsity from reliability-adjusted clinical priority. Three deterministic textualizations per motif or node-target pair were embedded with BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 and calibrated against 166,972 pre-2023 PubMed references using mean top-20 cosine distance. Positive recombination occurred in 8.63% of motifs and had negligible effect on global ranking, whereas specificity and support/GNN reliability terms changed clinical-priority ordering. Robustness analyses across neighbor counts, templates, encoders, generic-label taxonomies, score settings, PrimeKG, and corrected density-based RND showed that broad ordering was more stable than hard top-k membership. A semantic-dose analysis did not support a monotonic relation between score and forecasting benefit after endpoint and token-count adjustment (1.96 days per SD; 95% CI, -0.75 to 4.68). In the frozen catalog-q90 high-semantic subgroup, branch-enhanced forecasting reduced conditional event-time MAE by 20.1 days (95% CI, 13.3-27.0) and sMAPE by 2.37 percentage points (95% CI, 1.81-2.93), while discrimination and endpoint-ranking differences were inconclusive. Corrected RND showed low tail agreement with the primary score (Spearman rho = 0.430; q90 Jaccard = 0.125) despite a similar aggregate timing association. These findings support representation-aware, uncertainty-aware literature-relative prioritization of discovered trajectories rather than causal discovery or clinician-adjudicated novelty.
Summary / 总结
Graph-based analysis of electronic health records (EHRs) yields disease-trajectory catalogs, but structural support does not establish semantic novelty.
Hindsight-Privileged RLVR: Single-Rollout Token Credit Without Group Sampling
Authors: Wei Chen, Yubing Wu, Zhou Xu, Haoyue Zhang, Delu Zeng, Jiahao Wang, Junmei Yang, Pan tiewen, Jingfeng Liu, Zhiheng Zhou, Bo Li, Min Chen
First: 2026-10-14T17:06:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:06:59+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) trains language models from checked task outcomes, but assigning a terminal reward to individual reasoning tokens or actions remains difficult. Group-relative methods such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) sample several attempts per prompt, but lose their reward-based learning signal when all attempts receive the same outcome. We propose Hindsight-Privileged RLVR (HP-RLVR), a single-rollout method that turns a completed trajectory's own future into token-level update weights. The generating policy snapshot scores each eligible token under its causal history and under a hindsight context containing the realized future and verifier feedback. A normalized Jensen--Shannon divergence between the two views weights the reward relative to a historical baseline, yielding bounded token credit for both successful and failed attempts, with no reference solution or learned critic. In paired Qwen3-8B runs over a shared $100$-step schedule, HP-RLVR trains $1.21\times$, $1.46\times$, and $2.30\times$ faster than GRPO on mathematics, code generation, and ALFWorld, using one eighth the rollouts per prompt. Accuracy is comparable overall, with HP-RLVR ahead on mathematics at all three sizes and GRPO ahead on code and ALFWorld. In ALFWorld, $43.2\%$ (4B) and $49.0\%$ (8B) of GRPO's gradient-norm records are zero, versus none for HP-RLVR.
Summary / 总结
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) trains language models from checked task outcomes, but assigning a terminal reward to individual reasoning tokens or actions remains difficult.
The Neuro-Physical Inverter: A Modular Framework for Magnetotelluric Inversion Coupling Ensemble Conditioning with Residual Learning
Authors: Jae Deok Kim, Sai Ravela, Rob. L. Evans
First: 2026-10-02T12:41:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:14:37+00:00
Comments: Accepted by IEEE TGRS
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We present the Neuro-Physical Inverter (NPI), a modular, uncertainty-aware framework for geophysical inversion that couples ensemble-based conditioning with constrained residual learning, demonstrated in the 1D magnetotelluric (MT) setting as a controlled testbed. The framework operates in two stages. An Ensemble-Conditional Gaussian Process (EnsCGP) conditions a prior ensemble of resistivity models on the observed response, producing a physically admissible reference ensemble. A residual-learning neural network then predicts targeted corrections to this reference, trained on synthetic data and fine-tuned per station for field application through a physics-coupled objective. Because an ensemble is conditioned, refined, and propagated through both stages, every estimate carries an associated ensemble spread. Synthetic experiments show that NPI systematically reduces ensemble-mean error without destabilizing the ensemble. Applied to broadband MT data from the Gabbs Valley geothermal region (Nevada, USA), NPI reduces the across-station mean misfit over the mid-period band while retaining comparable ensemble spread. The propagated ensemble yields a factor of uncertainty that serves as an operational measure of constraint within the assumed model class. Both stages are dimension-agnostic in formulation, and the design principles established here are intended to scale to higher-dimensional parameterizations.
Summary / 总结
We present the Neuro-Physical Inverter (NPI), a modular, uncertainty-aware framework for geophysical inversion that couples ensemble-based conditioning with constrained residual learning, demonstrated in the 1D magnetotelluric (MT) setting as a controlled testbed.
Look Before You Restore: Learning to Predict Before Acting for Agentic Image Restoration
Authors: Xu Zhang, Jun Wan, Lefei Zhang, Liangpei Zhang
First: 2026-10-14T15:50:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T15:50:31+00:00
Comments: Under review
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Agents for mixed-degradation image restoration typically learn what a tool does to an image only after running it: failed calls waste computation and harmful outputs go unnoticed until too late. We propose PreActIR, an agentic restoration framework that predicts tool consequences before execution. For each candidate tool, a tool-conditioned world model forecasts changes in residual degradation, image quality, and fidelity, together with the risk of harm and the uncertainty of the prediction. The agent executes the top-ranked candidate, and an independent verifier commits or rolls back its output before the agent replans from the observed state. PreActIR also audits whether performance is limited by the selector or by the candidate space, and studies targeted tool adaptation as a controlled way to expand that space. In a controlled evaluation that fixes the candidate pool, verifier, and budget and employs reference-annotated degradation states, PreActIR uses 42.5% fewer real tool calls and 63.4% fewer rejected calls than reactive, execute-then-evaluate trial and error, while improving Y-PSNR by 0.92 dB.
Summary / 总结
Agents for mixed-degradation image restoration typically learn what a tool does to an image only after running it: failed calls waste computation and harmful outputs go unnoticed until too late.
Reference-Efficient Multimodal Bitstream-Corrupted Video Recovery via Depth-Conditioned Adaptation
Authors: Tianyi Liu, Jiaojiao Wang, Ye Lu, Kejun Wu, Chengtao Cai, Zhengguo Li, Chia-Wen Lin
First: 2026-10-11T17:52:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T15:06:06+00:00
Comments: 11 pages, 4 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recovering visually faithful content from damaged video bitstreams is important for reliable multimedia communication and storage. Existing methods exploit local residuals and non-local RGB references of the input video sequence, but both sources can become unreliable after decoding, while processing additional references increases receiver computation and memory. This paper studies multimodal bitstream-corrupted video recovery (MBSCVR) using RGB and depth information, where pre-transmission depth-based geometry aids reconstruction when the received appearance is corrupted. We propose a novel MBSCVR framework that selects and effectively utilizes a single selected non-local RGB reference per recovery window to achieve higher performance. It integrates a reliability-aware multimodal selector that combines valid appearance with depth compatibility before reference encoding. Depth-Conditioned Reference Adaptation (DCRA) introduces selected RGB evidence into feature completion and content generation, while frame-aligned depth conditions completion queries. On the complete BSCV benchmark with oracle masks, MBSCVR uses one selected reference to achieve 32.30 dB on YouTube-VOS and 28.93 dB on DAVIS, surpassing full-reference B2SCVR by 0.27 and 0.33 dB and improving SSIM on both subsets. Matched receiver-forward measurements show 36.8\% lower latency and 40.8\% lower peak memory. These results demonstrate how compact geometric guidance and adapted appearance processing improve the quality--resource trade-off of multimedia recovery.
Summary / 总结
Recovering visually faithful content from damaged video bitstreams is important for reliable multimedia communication and storage.
A Valid Watermark Is Not a Valid Identity: Spatial-Spectral Adversarial Face Evasion with Watermark Preservation
Authors: Jefferson David Rodriguez Chivata, Davide Ghiani, Simone Maurizio La Cava, Marco Micheletto, Giulia Orrù, Gian Luca Marcialis
First: 2026-10-14T14:37:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T14:37:52+00:00
Comments: Accepted at IEEE International Joint Conference on Biometrics (IJCB 2026)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Facial image watermarking can support traceability and certification in biometric pipelines, but provenance or manipulation detection alone does not guarantee reliable biometric identity. We introduce a watermark-preserving face evasion attack that targets this gap under a black-box watermarking setting, without access to the watermarking components. The proposed PGD-L_2 attack guides adversarial perturbations with spatial and spectral preservation terms to reduce face-recognition similarity while limiting pixel-level distortion and high-frequency changes that may interfere with hidden watermark cues. Evaluated on controlled facial images with two watermarking schemes, two face-recognition models, and two datasets, the attack achieves evasion rates above 92% at operational thresholds while preserving watermark recovery above 93% for StegaFormer and above 99.7% for FaceSigns, with visual quality above 47dB PSNR at epsilon_2=2.0. These results expose a structural gap in watermark-based facial certification: a face image can remain watermark-valid and visually faithful while becoming biometrically unreliable.
Summary / 总结
Facial image watermarking can support traceability and certification in biometric pipelines, but provenance or manipulation detection alone does not guarantee reliable biometric identity.
